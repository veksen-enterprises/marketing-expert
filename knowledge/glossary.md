Short definitions in plain English. Each entry opens with a one-line explanation for someone with no business background; use the term in advice, and give that line the first time you use it. "See" points to the playbook where the idea is used in depth. Formulas follow the definitions used in this knowledge base; companies often define them slightly differently, so always check the definition behind any number you compare.

## 0–9

- **70/20/10**: a rule for splitting a budget between what works, extensions of it, and new tries. 70% on proven channels, 20% on extending what works (new audiences, formats), 10% on new ideas. It comes from Coca-Cola's content planning, not from a study. See marketing-budget-and-team.

## A

- **A/B test**: show two versions to randomly chosen groups of people and compare the results. Pick the number you will judge it on before starting. See experimentation.
- **AARRR**: a five-step model of how people become and stay customers. Acquisition, activation, retention, referral, revenue ("pirate metrics", Dave McClure, 2007). Fits self-serve and consumer products; sales-led companies use deal stages instead. See metrics-and-measurement.
- **ABM (account-based marketing)**: sales and marketing jointly target a short, named list of companies. Instead of chasing individual leads, the team plans outreach around each chosen company. See outbound-and-abm, b2b-saas-sales-led.
- **Absorption**: a big platform builds your product's main feature into its own product. One of the main platform risks: a "good-enough" built-in version can remove the reason to buy yours. See platform-and-feature-risk.
- **Activation**: the early step after which a new user is likely to keep using the product. Defined from data as the earliest action (or set of actions) whose completion predicts a user will still be active later, such as a team sending its first invoice. Activation rate = share of sign-ups who reach it. In brand advertising, "activation" means short-term sales-focused ads instead. See self-serve-saas, activation-and-analytics, brand-and-demand.
- **ACV (annual contract value)**: how much one customer contract is worth per year, on average. A $36,000 three-year deal has an ACV of $12,000. ACV decides whether you can afford salespeople. In retail, ACV means all-commodity volume, a store-size-weighted measure of distribution (see retail-cpg). See b2b-saas-sales-led, channel-strategy.
- **AE (account executive)**: the salesperson who runs deals and closes them. See b2b-saas-sales-led.
- **Aggregator**: a product that gathers activity from many places into one. For example, trade listings from many community servers in one search; it is only useful once enough activity flows through it. See community-and-hobby-products.
- **Aha moment**: the point where a new user first feels what the product does for them. Used to design onboarding; activation is the measurable action that predicts it sticks. See self-serve-saas.
- **AI Overviews / AI Mode**: Google's AI-written answers shown in search results. They can reduce clicks to websites. See seo-and-ai-search.
- **AOV (average order value)**: how much a customer spends per order, on average. Revenue ÷ number of orders. See ecommerce-dtc.
- **ARPU (average revenue per user)**: how much money each user or account brings in, on average. Revenue ÷ users (or accounts) in a period. See channel-strategy.
- **ARR (annual recurring revenue)**: the yearly value of all subscriptions currently being paid. MRR × 12. Excludes one-off fees. See b2b-saas-sales-led.
- **ASO (app store optimisation)**: improving an app's store page so more people find and install it. Title, keywords, screenshots and ratings, in the App Store and Google Play. See consumer-apps, app-store-discovery.
- **Atomic network**: the smallest group of users that is useful on its own. For a marketplace, enough buyers and sellers in one city, campus or category that it keeps working without you pushing it (Andrew Chen). Build one, then copy it. See marketplaces.
- **ATT (App Tracking Transparency)**: Apple's rule that apps must ask before tracking people across other apps. In force since iOS 14.5 (2021); it cut the data available for mobile ad targeting and measurement. See consumer-apps, paid-acquisition.
- **Attribution**: deciding which ad, post or visit gets credit for a sale. Models include last click and data-driven. It shows correlation, not cause. See metrics-and-measurement.

## B

- **B2B / B2C / DTC**: selling to businesses / to consumers / directly to consumers through your own store. DTC means without going through retailers. See ecommerce-dtc.
- **Beachhead**: a small, specific first group of customers you can win quickly before expanding. Members should share an urgent problem, be reachable through a channel you can afford, and talk to each other. See launches-and-gtm, competing-with-incumbents.
- **Blended (CAC, ROAS)**: averaged across all your ways of finding customers, free ones included. Across all channels. Blended CAC can hide a paid CAC that is much higher. See metrics-and-measurement.
- **Bootstrapping**: growing a company on its own sales instead of investors' money. Suits markets too small for venture capital. See market-sizing-and-timing.
- **Bottom-up sizing**: estimating a market by counting real buyers and what each would pay. Real buyers × price, instead of taking a share of a big industry total ("top-down"). See market-sizing-and-timing.
- **Brand building**: broad, memorable advertising so more people think of you when they need what you sell. Pays back over months to years. See brand-and-demand.
- **Break-even**: the point where income from something just covers what it cost. Break-even CPA = AOV × contribution margin on a first order; break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ contribution margin %. See paid-acquisition, ecommerce-dtc.
- **Bullseye**: a method for finding your best way to reach customers. Brainstorm all channels, test a few cheaply, focus on the one that works (Weinberg & Mares, *Traction*, 2015). See channel-strategy.
- **Burn rate**: how much more money the company spends than it earns each month. Net burn = cash out − cash in per month. With runway, it tells you how long the money lasts. See startup-risk-and-opportunity.
- **Buying group**: the several people at a company who together decide a purchase. Typically a user, a champion, the economic buyer, IT or security, and finance. See b2b-saas-sales-led.

## C

- **CAC (customer acquisition cost)**: what it costs you, on average, to win one new customer. Sales and marketing spend ÷ new customers in the period. "Fully loaded" includes salaries and tools, not only ad spend; with a long sales cycle, lag the spend. See metrics-and-measurement.
- **CAC payback**: how many months a customer takes to earn back what you spent winning them. CAC ÷ monthly gross profit per customer: $1,200 ÷ $100 a month = 12 months. See metrics-and-measurement.
- **Canonical tag**: a line in a page's code naming its main web address among copies. When several URLs show the same content, it tells search engines which URL to rank. See seo-content-and-architecture, international-seo.
- **Case study**: a written story of one real customer's problem and the result they got with you. Lead with the before-state and a measured result the customer approves. A case study matching the buyer's industry and size beats a famous logo. See b2b-saas-sales-led, messaging-and-copy.
- **Category**: the kind of product buyers file you under, which sets who they compare you with. Also called market category; choosing it is part of positioning and sets assumptions about competitors, price and features. See positioning.
- **Category entry point**: a need or situation that makes someone start looking for a kind of product. "My laptop just died". Brands try to be linked to these in memory. See brand-and-demand.
- **ccTLD**: a country web address ending such as .de or .fr. See international-seo.
- **Champion**: the person inside a buying company who wants your product and argues for it. See b2b-saas-sales-led, founder-led-sales.
- **Churn**: the customers, or the revenue, you lose in a period. Logo churn counts customers lost; revenue churn counts money lost. Annual churn = 1 − (1 − monthly)^12, not monthly × 12: 3% a month is about 30.6% a year. See metrics-and-measurement, retention-and-expansion.
- **Citation (local SEO)**: a listing of your business name, address and phone on another site. Such as a directory. See local-seo.
- **COGS (cost of goods sold)**: the direct cost of making or buying what you sold. Excludes overheads such as rent and marketing. See ecommerce-dtc.
- **Cohort**: a group of customers who started in the same week or month, followed over time. Comparing cohorts shows whether retention is improving; an average over all customers hides worse recent groups. See metrics-and-measurement.
- **Cold start**: a new marketplace or network has too few users on each side to be useful. Also called the chicken-and-egg problem. See marketplaces.
- **Competitive alternatives**: what customers would do if your product did not exist. Usually not a named competitor: a spreadsheet, an agency, an intern, doing nothing. The first step of positioning. See positioning.
- **Confidence interval (CI)**: the range of true results that fit the test data. A wide interval means the test cannot tell a small gain from a loss. See experimentation, metrics-and-measurement.
- **Consent Mode**: Google's way of telling its ad tools whether each visitor accepted cookies. Version 2 has been required for ads measurement and remarketing in the EEA since March 2024. See paid-acquisition, privacy-and-marketing-law.
- **Contraction**: revenue lost when existing customers move to a cheaper plan without leaving. Downgrades and fewer seats. An early warning; track it apart from full churn. See retention-and-expansion.
- **Contribution margin (CM)**: what one order leaves you after the costs that order itself causes. Order revenue − product cost − pick, pack and shipping − payment fees − expected returns − variable marketing such as affiliate commission. CM2 also subtracts other marketing. It is lower than gross margin; using gross margin for ad targets overstates what you can afford. See ecommerce-dtc.
- **Conversion rate (CVR)**: the share of people who do what you hoped, such as sign up or buy. Share of visitors, clicks or users who complete a target action. See landing-pages-and-cro, paid-acquisition.
- **Conversions API (CAPI)**: Meta's way to report sales from your server instead of only the browser. Recovers data lost to browsers, ATT and consent. See paid-acquisition, privacy-and-marketing-law.
- **Core loop**: the main thing people come back to your product to do. For example, looking up an item or joining a group; keep it free and keep ads off it. See community-and-hobby-products.
- **Core Web Vitals**: Google's measures of how fast and stable a page feels. Loading (LCP), responsiveness (INP) and layout stability (CLS). See landing-pages-and-cro, seo-and-ai-search.
- **Counter-positioning**: a newcomer uses a business model the leader won't copy, because copying would hurt the leader. See competing-with-incumbents.
- **CPA / CPC / CPM**: ad cost per sale or sign-up, per click, and per thousand views. Cost per acquisition (per conversion), cost per click, cost per thousand impressions. See paid-acquisition.
- **CRM (customer relationship management)**: software that tracks every contact, deal and conversation with customers. A spreadsheet is enough for one founder with a few dozen deals; switch when a second person sells or follow-ups slip. See founder-led-sales, b2b-saas-sales-led.
- **CRO (conversion rate optimisation)**: steadily improving pages and steps so more visitors do what you hoped. See landing-pages-and-cro.
- **CTA (call to action)**: the button or link that asks the reader to take the next step. "Get my quote". See landing-pages-and-cro, messaging-and-copy.
- **CTR (click-through rate)**: the share of people who saw a link or ad and clicked it. Clicks ÷ impressions. See paid-acquisition, seo-and-ai-search.
- **CUPED**: a statistics method that uses past behaviour to make tests need fewer users. Uses each user's data from before the test to reduce noise; needs pre-test data per user. See experimentation.
- **Customer success**: a team or process that helps customers reach their goals so they renew and buy more. Often shortened to CS. Pays off when contract value can fund human time; below that, use in-product help and email. See retention-and-expansion.

## D

- **D1 / D7 / D30 retention**: the share of new users still active 1, 7 or 30 days after starting. Counted from install or sign-up. See consumer-apps.
- **Dark patterns**: designs that trick or pressure people into choices they would not make. Hidden cancel buttons, fake countdown timers, pre-ticked boxes; regulators treat many as illegal. See behavioral-science, privacy-and-marketing-law.
- **Dark social**: sharing that tracking tools cannot see, such as private messages and podcasts. Also Slack groups and other closed communities. See metrics-and-measurement, channel-strategy.
- **Data-driven attribution (DDA)**: Google's default way of splitting credit for a sale across ads. Uses path data; still attribution, not a lift test. See metrics-and-measurement.
- **Default alive**: whether you reach profit on the money you have, at current costs and growth. Paul Graham's test; "default dead" means you will need more money to survive. Recompute it monthly. See startup-risk-and-opportunity.
- **Deliverability**: whether your emails land in the inbox rather than spam. Depends on authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), complaints and list quality. See email-and-lifecycle.
- **Demand capture vs demand creation**: winning people already looking to buy, vs making more people want it later. Capture: search ads, review sites. Creation: brand, content. See brand-and-demand.
- **Demand generation**: marketing work aimed at creating interest that later turns into sales. Often the name of the marketing team that feeds sales in B2B. See b2b-saas-sales-led, marketing-budget-and-team.
- **Design partner**: an early customer who helps shape the product in return for early access. Often becomes a case study; agree the terms in writing. See launches-and-gtm, first-customers.
- **Discovery call**: an early sales conversation to learn the buyer's problem before pitching. Ask about their situation, the cost of the problem, who decides and how they buy. See founder-led-sales.
- **Distinctive brand assets**: colours, logos, characters, sounds and phrases people link only to your brand. See brand-and-demand.
- **Doorway page**: a page made mainly to catch a search that adds nothing for the reader. Often a template with only the town or keyword changed; against Google's spam policies. See local-seo, international-seo.
- **Double jeopardy**: small brands have fewer buyers, and those buyers are also slightly less loyal. Loyalty mostly tracks size, so growth comes mainly from more buyers. See brand-and-demand.
- **Dunning**: recovering subscription payments that failed. Retries, card updates, reminder emails and a grace period, to prevent involuntary churn. See self-serve-saas, retention-and-expansion.

## E–F

- **Earned media**: coverage and mentions you don't pay for. Press articles and other people's posts. See pr-and-influencers, ai-assistant-visibility.
- **Economic buyer**: the person at the customer who can approve the money. Often not the user or the champion; a deal with no known economic buyer usually stalls. See founder-led-sales, b2b-saas-sales-led.
- **Embargo**: an agreement that a journalist will not publish news before a set time. See pr-and-influencers.
- **Employee advocacy**: staff choosing to share company news and content from their own social accounts. See organic-social-and-community, content-marketing.
- **ESOV (excess share of voice)**: how much more you advertise than your market share would suggest. Your share of category advertising minus your share of market. Positive ESOV is linked with market share growth (about +0.5 points a year per +10 points ESOV, a rule of thumb). See brand-and-demand.
- **Exit**: when founders and investors turn their shares into money, usually by selling the company. Acquisition is the most common good exit; no exit at all is the most common outcome. See acquisition-and-exits.
- **Expansion revenue**: extra money from customers you already have. More seats, more usage, a higher plan, extra products. See retention-and-expansion, self-serve-saas.
- **Faceted navigation**: filters on listing pages (size, colour, price) that can create many near-copy web addresses. These can waste search engines' attention. See seo-content-and-architecture.
- **FMCG / CPG**: everyday products people buy often, such as food, drinks and toiletries. Fast-moving consumer goods / consumer packaged goods. See retail-cpg, brand-and-demand.
- **Forces of progress**: the four forces that decide whether someone switches products. Push of the current situation and pull of the new one, against anxiety about the new and habit. See customer-research.
- **Freemium vs free trial**: a free plan forever with limits, vs full access for a limited time. See pricing, self-serve-saas.
- **Funnel**: the steps people go through from first hearing of you to paying. For example visit → sign-up → activation → payment; each step loses some people, and conversion rate is measured per step. See metrics-and-measurement, landing-pages-and-cro.

## G

- **GA4**: Google Analytics 4, Google's current tool for measuring website and app visits. See metrics-and-measurement.
- **Gated content**: content you can read only after giving your contact details in a form. See b2b-saas-sales-led, content-marketing.
- **GBP (Google Business Profile)**: the free business listing that feeds Google Maps and local search results. See local-seo.
- **GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation)**: the EU law on collecting and using personal data. Covers consent for tracking and marketing email in the EU. See privacy-and-marketing-law.
- **GEO (generative engine optimisation)**: trying to get your product cited in AI chat answers. ChatGPT, AI Overviews and similar; much advice is speculation. Not the same as a "geo test". See seo-and-ai-search, ai-assistant-visibility.
- **Geo test (geo experiment)**: turning ads on or off in some regions and comparing with similar regions. Measures lift. See metrics-and-measurement.
- **GMV (gross merchandise value)**: the total value of all sales made through a marketplace. Before the marketplace takes its cut. See marketplaces.
- **Go-to-market (GTM)**: your plan for reaching and winning customers for a product. Who you target first, how you position it, how you sell (self-serve or sales), your first channel and the proof you show. See launches-and-gtm.
- **Gross margin**: the share of each sale left after the direct cost of delivering it. Gross profit = revenue − COGS (or, for software, hosting and support); gross margin = gross profit ÷ revenue. LTV and payback use gross profit, not revenue. Not the same as contribution margin. See ecommerce-dtc, metrics-and-measurement.
- **Growth loop**: a cycle where new users bring in more users without new spending. For example, new supply creates new pages, which bring new buyers, who attract more supply. See marketplaces, referral-programs.
- **GRR (gross revenue retention)**: the share of last period's revenue you kept, ignoring extra sales to those customers. (Start revenue − churn − contraction) ÷ start revenue; never above 100%. See metrics-and-measurement.
- **Guardrail metric**: a number a test must not make worse, even if the main result improves. Such as refunds or retention. See experimentation.

## H–I

- **Health score**: one combined score per customer account showing how likely it is to stay. Usually built from usage, active users vs seats bought, support tickets, payment status and survey answers. Check it against real renewals. See retention-and-expansion.
- **Holdout**: a random group deliberately left out of a campaign, to measure its real effect. See metrics-and-measurement, email-and-lifecycle.
- **hreflang**: a tag telling search engines which language or country version of a page to show. See international-seo.
- **ICP (ideal customer profile)**: a description of the customers who get the most value and are best to serve. The type of company that buys fastest, pays most and stays longest; build it from your own won and lost deals. See b2b-saas-sales-led, customer-research.
- **Incrementality / lift**: the extra results that happened only because of your marketing. Compared with what would have happened anyway; measured by experiments. See metrics-and-measurement.
- **Information gain**: what a page adds that other pages on the topic don't. Original data, first-hand experience, a worked example. See seo-content-and-architecture, seo-and-ai-search.
- **Intent data**: signals that a company is researching a topic right now. Often sold by vendors and used to time outreach. See outbound-and-abm.
- **Internal linking**: links between pages on your own site. They help search engines find pages and judge which matter. See seo-content-and-architecture.
- **Involuntary churn**: customers lost because a payment failed, not because they chose to leave. Expired card, bank decline. See self-serve-saas, retention-and-expansion.

## J–L

- **JTBD (jobs to be done)**: seeing a purchase as hiring a product to make progress in a situation. See customer-research.
- **K-factor (viral coefficient)**: how many new users each user brings in through invites. Invites per user × share of invites that convert. Above 1 means each user brings more than one more; almost never sustained. See referral-programs, consumer-apps.
- **Kill criteria**: results, written in advance, that will make you stop a project or test. In this server's advice, a move's stop line. See startup-risk-and-opportunity.
- **Lagging vs leading metric**: a number that is accurate but slow vs one that moves early and predicts it. Lagging: revenue, NRR, payback. Leading: activation rate, week-1 retention. Check in your own cohorts that the leading one predicts the lagging one. See metrics-and-measurement.
- **Lead**: a person or company that has shown interest and might buy. A lead becomes an opportunity once sales accepts it as a real potential deal. See b2b-saas-sales-led.
- **Lead magnet**: something free offered in exchange for an email address. A checklist, template or guide; use double opt-in and never buy lists. See email-and-lifecycle.
- **Leakage (disintermediation)**: buyers and sellers meet on your marketplace, then deal directly to skip your fee. Counter it by making on-platform deals clearly better (payment protection, insurance, scheduling). See marketplaces.
- **Lifecycle marketing**: messages sent automatically based on what a customer does or fails to do. Email, push or in-app. See email-and-lifecycle.
- **Lifestyle business**: a company built to pay its founder a good living, not to grow huge. Judge it by income per hour of work, not by growth rate. See community-and-hobby-products.
- **Lifetime deal**: a one-off price that gives a buyer access forever, often sold on deal sites. Brings early cash, but the buyers never pay again and still cost support; treat it as financing, not a price tier. See pricing.
- **Liquidity (marketplace)**: the chance a listing sells or a request is filled within a reasonable time. Measured by the match rate. See marketplaces.
- **LLM (large language model)**: the type of AI behind assistants such as ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini. See ai-in-marketing, ai-assistant-visibility.
- **Local pack**: the map with three business listings shown for local searches. See local-seo.
- **LSA (Local Services Ads)**: Google's ads for service businesses where you pay per enquiry, not per click. Shown above the local pack, with a "Google Verified" badge. See local-seo, local-services.
- **LTV (lifetime value)**: the profit you expect from one customer over the whole time they stay. Simple form: monthly gross profit per customer ÷ monthly churn; with low churn, cap the horizon (for example 36–60 months). See metrics-and-measurement.
- **LTV:CAC**: how many times a customer's lifetime profit covers the cost of winning them. LTV ÷ CAC; 3:1 is a rule of thumb, not a law. See metrics-and-measurement.

## M

- **M&A (mergers and acquisitions)**: companies buying or combining with other companies. For a startup, usually being bought. See acquisition-and-exits.
- **Marketing operations**: the role that runs marketing tools, data and reporting. See marketing-budget-and-team.
- **MDE (minimum detectable effect)**: the smallest change a test is built to detect reliably. Required sample grows with 1/MDE²: halving it needs about four times the traffic. See experimentation.
- **MEDDIC / MEDDPICC**: a checklist of what you must know for a deal to close. Metrics, economic buyer, decision criteria, decision process, paper process, identify pain, champion, competition. Blank fields are your next questions. See founder-led-sales.
- **Mental availability**: how likely buyers are to think of your brand when they need to buy. See brand-and-demand.
- **MER (marketing efficiency ratio)**: total revenue for every dollar of marketing spend. Total store revenue ÷ total marketing spend, across all channels; also called blended ROAS. Ad platforms can't inflate it. See ecommerce-dtc.
- **MMM (marketing mix modelling)**: a statistical model that estimates how much each kind of marketing adds to sales. Needs about 2–3 years of weekly data and spend that varies in each channel. See metrics-and-measurement.
- **MPP (Mail Privacy Protection)**: an Apple feature that makes email opens look recorded even when unread. It loads email images through a proxy, so open rates are no longer reliable. See email-and-lifecycle.
- **MQL (marketing-qualified lead)**: a person whose activity passed a marketing score threshold. Widely criticised because companies buy as groups. See b2b-saas-sales-led.
- **MRR (monthly recurring revenue)**: the monthly value of all subscriptions currently being paid. See metrics-and-measurement.
- **Multi-homing**: customers or suppliers using several competing platforms at once. A driver on two ride apps; it weakens network effects. See marketplaces, competitive-analysis.

## N–O

- **NAP**: name, address, phone; should be identical everywhere a local business is listed. See local-seo.
- **Network effect**: a product becomes more valuable as more people use it. See marketplaces, competitive-analysis.
- **noindex**: an instruction telling search engines not to show a page in results. See seo-content-and-architecture.
- **North-star metric**: the one number that best shows customers getting value and leads to revenue. Weekly active teams, orders delivered; pair it with numbers teams can move and guardrails. See metrics-and-measurement.
- **NPS (Net Promoter Score)**: a score from asking customers how likely they are to recommend you. 0–10 scale; % scoring 9–10 minus % scoring 0–6. A weak predictor of future growth. See referral-programs.
- **NRR (net revenue retention)**: how much revenue last period's customers bring now, extra sales to them included. (Start revenue + expansion − contraction − churn) ÷ start revenue; excludes new customers and can exceed 100%. See metrics-and-measurement.
- **Objective-and-task budgeting**: set the goal, cost the work needed to reach it, and add it up. Instead of copying a % of revenue. See marketing-budget-and-team.
- **ODI (outcome-driven innovation)**: a survey method that ranks what customers want done by importance and satisfaction. Finds unmet needs. See customer-research.
- **Open core**: free, open-source base product with paid features on top. Usually team and enterprise features; the hard part is where to draw the line. See developer-tools.
- **Opportunity**: a potential deal that sales has accepted as real and is actively working. Pipeline is the total value of open opportunities. See b2b-saas-sales-led.
- **Overlay**: a product that adds a layer on top of an existing community or platform. A bot in a chat server, a tool on a game's data; its value comes from the community's activity. See community-and-hobby-products.

## P–Q

- **P&L (profit and loss statement)**: a report of money earned, money spent, and what is left. Covers a period, usually a month, quarter or year. See competing-with-incumbents.
- **Paper process**: the steps a buyer's company needs before signing, such as legal and purchasing review. Ask about it on the first serious call. See founder-led-sales.
- **Payback**: how many months a customer takes to earn back what you spent winning them. Short for CAC payback. See metrics-and-measurement.
- **Paywall**: the point where a product asks you to pay before going further. A hard paywall blocks use until you pay; a soft one limits a free version. See consumer-apps, pricing.
- **Peeking**: checking a test early and stopping when it looks like a win. This inflates false positives. See experimentation.
- **Penetration**: the share of all buyers in your category who bought from you in a period. Growth mostly comes from raising it. See brand-and-demand.
- **Performance Max (PMax) / Advantage+**: Google's and Meta's automated ad campaigns, which choose audiences and placements themselves. They also set bids. See paid-acquisition, ecommerce-dtc.
- **Pilot**: a short trial of your product in a customer's real work, ending in a buy decision. Also called a proof of concept (POC). Write the success test, length, price and what turns it into a contract before it starts; charge for it. See founder-led-sales, first-customers.
- **Pipeline**: the total value of the potential deals your sales team is working on. Open, qualified opportunities; work it backwards from the revenue target via win rate. See b2b-saas-sales-led.
- **Platform risk**: the danger that a company you depend on changes rules, fees or access. Or copies you; see absorption and sherlocking. See platform-and-feature-risk.
- **PLG (product-led growth)**: the product itself attracts, sells to and grows customers, with little sales help. See self-serve-saas, channel-strategy.
- **PMF (product-market fit)**: a clear group of people keeps using your product and would hate to lose it. Signals: a retention curve that flattens above zero, and 40% of users "very disappointed" without it (Sean Ellis's threshold, from his experience, never published as data). See metrics-and-measurement.
- **Positioning**: how you make the right buyers see why you are their best choice. Built from competitive alternatives, unique attributes, value and proof, target customers and category. See positioning.
- **Power (statistical)**: the chance a test detects a real effect of a given size. 80% is the usual default. See experimentation.
- **PQL (product-qualified lead)**: a user or account whose use of the product shows they are ready to buy. See self-serve-saas.
- **Pre-mortem**: imagining a project has failed and listing why, before starting. See startup-risk-and-opportunity.
- **Procurement**: the buying department and its process at a larger customer. Adds security review, legal terms and vendor setup; often weeks on its own. See founder-led-sales.
- **Product marketing**: the role that owns positioning, messaging, launches and sales materials. See marketing-budget-and-team, launches-and-gtm.
- **Product-led sales**: salespeople contact only the accounts whose product use shows they are ready to buy. They offer help rather than a cold pitch. See self-serve-saas, retention-and-expansion.
- **Productized service**: a service sold like a product: fixed scope, fixed price, fixed timeline. "Website accessibility audit, 10 days, $8,000". See professional-services.
- **Programmatic SEO**: many web pages built from one template and a dataset. "Plumbers in Lyon". See seo-content-and-architecture, marketplaces.
- **Quota**: the sales target a salesperson must hit in a period. Set a reachable first quota for a first hire. See b2b-saas-sales-led, founder-led-sales.

## R

- **Ramp**: the time a new salesperson takes to reach full productivity. See b2b-saas-sales-led.
- **RCT (randomised controlled trial)**: an experiment where people are randomly split into groups given different treatments. Differences in results can then be credited to the treatment. See startup-risk-and-opportunity, metrics-and-measurement.
- **Retainer**: a monthly fee a client pays for ongoing access or a set amount of work. Smooths cash flow; define what it includes and track hours to check it is profitable. See professional-services.
- **Retargeting**: ads shown to people who already visited your site or app. Attribution often over-credits it. See paid-acquisition.
- **Retention**: the share of customers or users who keep using or paying over time. The opposite of churn; read it by cohort, not as one average. See metrics-and-measurement, retention-and-expansion.
- **Retention curve**: a chart of the share of a group still active over time. A curve that flattens above zero is a key product-market fit signal. See metrics-and-measurement, consumer-apps.
- **Reverse trial**: new users get the full paid plan for a while, then drop to a free plan. Instead of being locked out when the trial ends. See pricing, self-serve-saas.
- **ROAS (return on ad spend)**: sales credited to ads for every dollar spent on them. Revenue attributed to ads ÷ ad spend; platform ROAS is not proof of extra sales. See paid-acquisition.
- **ROI (return on investment)**: what you got back compared with what you put in. (Return − cost) ÷ cost.
- **Runway**: how many months the company can keep going on the cash it has. Cash in the bank ÷ monthly burn rate. See startup-risk-and-opportunity.

## S

- **S&M**: sales and marketing costs together. Often given as a % of revenue or ARR. See marketing-budget-and-team.
- **Sales cycle**: how long a deal takes, from first serious talk to signed contract. Measured from a qualified opportunity being created to the deal closing. See b2b-saas-sales-led, events-and-webinars.
- **Sales velocity**: how fast your sales process turns into revenue. (Opportunities × win rate × deal size) ÷ cycle length; shows which lever moved. See b2b-saas-sales-led.
- **SAM / SOM**: the part of a market you can serve, and the part you can realistically win. See TAM / SAM / SOM. See market-sizing-and-timing.
- **Scaled content abuse**: Google's rule against mass-producing low-value pages to rank in search. Whatever tool made them, AI included. See seo-and-ai-search, seo-content-and-architecture.
- **SDR (sales development representative)**: a salesperson who books meetings for account executives. Often through outbound. See outbound-and-abm.
- **Segment**: a group of customers who share a need, size or situation. Positioning, pricing and channels are chosen per segment; results often differ sharply between segments. See positioning, customer-research.
- **Self-reported attribution**: asking customers where they heard of you, on forms or calls. "How did you hear about us?" catches word of mouth, podcasts and communities that click tracking misses. See metrics-and-measurement.
- **SEO (search engine optimisation)**: making pages easier to find, understand and rank in search engines. See seo-and-ai-search.
- **SERP (search engine results page)**: the page of results for a search. See seo-and-ai-search.
- **Share of search**: your brand's share of all brand searches in your category. Your branded search volume ÷ total branded search in the category; a cheap stand-in for how often people think of you. See brand-and-demand.
- **Share of voice (SOV)**: your share of all advertising, or mentions, in your category. See brand-and-demand.
- **Sherlocking**: a platform owner builds a feature that copies a popular app on its platform. Apple and Google have done it. See platform-and-feature-risk.
- **SKU (stock-keeping unit)**: one specific product variant you stock and sell. Size, colour, flavour. See retail-cpg, ecommerce-dtc.
- **SLA (service-level agreement)**: a written promise of a level of service, such as response time. Also used inside a company: marketing commits to a number of qualified leads, sales commits to follow-up times. See outbound-and-abm.
- **Small bet**: a cheap marketing test you can run in days and judge on its own. Run a few, keep what works, drop the rest; each needs a written stop rule. See small-bets.
- **SMB / mid-market / enterprise**: small, medium and large customer companies. Each needs a different sales approach, price and payback target. See b2b-saas-sales-led, metrics-and-measurement.
- **SOC 2**: an independent audit report on a software company's security controls. Type 1 checks design at one date; Type 2 checks they worked over months. Leave it until a buyer asks. See founder-led-sales, first-customers.
- **Social proof**: showing that other people use and like something, to make it feel safe to choose. Reviews, customer counts, logos, testimonials; must be real. See behavioral-science, landing-pages-and-cro.
- **SPIN**: a way of asking questions in a sales call to size the buyer's problem. Situation, problem, implication, need-payoff (Rackham). See founder-led-sales.
- **SQL (sales-qualified lead)**: a potential buyer that the sales team agrees is worth pursuing. See b2b-saas-sales-led.
- **SRM (sample ratio mismatch)**: the groups in a test differ in size from the planned split. It shows a broken setup; do not trust the result. See experimentation.
- **Stage**: how far a business has got, from 0 (no users yet) to 3 (something newsworthy). 0 no users; 1 first users (about 1–20); 2 steady use (around 100 active users or a couple of thousand monthly visits); 3 something newsworthy. Judged from traction, not from how the founder describes it. See small-bets.
- **Statistical significance (p-value)**: how surprising a result would be if nothing had really changed. "Not significant" does not mean "no effect". See experimentation.
- **Structured data (schema)**: code on a page that describes its content to search engines. Usually JSON-LD using schema.org terms. See seo-and-ai-search, local-seo.
- **Sustaining vs disruptive innovation**: a better product for the leader's best customers vs a cheaper, simpler one they ignore. Leaders usually win sustaining fights (Christensen). See competing-with-incumbents.
- **Switch interview**: an interview that rebuilds, step by step, how a recent customer chose to switch. From first thought to purchase; reveals the forces of progress. See customer-research.
- **Switching costs**: the money, effort or risk a customer faces in changing supplier. See competitive-analysis.

## T

- **Take rate**: the share of each sale a marketplace keeps as its fee. Marketplace revenue ÷ GMV; Gurley's examples range from about 2% to 70%, so no single benchmark fits. See marketplaces.
- **TAM / SAM / SOM**: the whole market, the part you can serve, and the part you can realistically win. Total addressable market (all demand for the category), serviceable addressable market (the part you can serve today), serviceable obtainable market (what you can win in a set time). Size bottom-up first. See market-sizing-and-timing.
- **Time-to-value**: how long a new customer waits before getting the result they came for. From sign-up or purchase. See retention-and-expansion, activation-and-analytics.
- **Topic cluster**: one main page on a subject linked to several detailed pages on its sub-topics. See seo-content-and-architecture.
- **Trust page**: a public page explaining what data you handle, where it goes and who can see it. Also how long you keep it and how to report a security problem; it shortens security reviews. See founder-led-sales.

## U–W

- **UGC (user-generated content)**: content made by customers or creators rather than the brand. Often reused in ads. See ecommerce-dtc, pr-and-influencers.
- **Unit economics**: whether you make or lose money on each single customer or order. Revenue and costs per customer or order: CAC, LTV, payback, contribution margin. See metrics-and-measurement.
- **Upsell / cross-sell**: selling an existing customer a bigger plan, or a second product. Offer it after the first purchase has delivered value, not during onboarding. See retention-and-expansion, ecommerce-dtc.
- **Utilisation**: the share of available hours that are booked or billed. Billable hours ÷ available hours. See local-services, professional-services.
- **UTM parameters**: tags added to links so analytics can record which campaign a visit came from. See small-bets, launches-and-gtm.
- **Value metric**: what you charge by, chosen to grow as customers get more value. Seats, contacts, usage. See pricing.
- **Value proposition**: the main benefit you promise a buyer, and why they should believe it. Comes from positioning. See messaging-and-copy, landing-pages-and-cro.
- **Van Westendorp**: a pricing survey asking at which prices a product seems too cheap or too expensive. Four questions: too cheap, cheap, expensive, too expensive. See pricing.
- **Venture capital (VC)**: money from investors who buy part of a young company, hoping for a huge sale later. Suits products that can grow very large; most venture-backed companies never return investors' money. See market-sizing-and-timing, startup-risk-and-opportunity.
- **Win rate**: the share of real potential deals that become customers. Share of qualified sales opportunities that are won. See b2b-saas-sales-led.
- **Win/loss interview**: asking recent buyers and lost prospects why they chose you or not. See competitive-analysis, customer-research.
- **WTP (willingness to pay)**: the most a customer would pay. See pricing.
