This playbook is about using generative AI (large language models and image/video generators) to do marketing work. How AI assistants and AI Overviews change search traffic and citations ("GEO") is in seo-and-ai-search. Scaled content and site quality are in seo-content-and-architecture and content-marketing.

## Productivity: what the experiments show [research]

- **Writing tasks get faster and, on average, better.** Noy & Zhang (Science, 2023): professionals doing mid-level writing tasks (press releases, short reports, analysis plans, sensitive emails) with ChatGPT took about 40% less time and their output was rated about 18% higher in quality. Weaker writers gained most, so the gap between workers narrowed. Tasks were short and self-contained; this is the best case for AI.
- **The "jagged technological frontier".** Dell'Acqua et al. (HBS working paper 24-013, 2023; 758 BCG consultants, pre-registered): on tasks inside AI's capability, consultants with GPT-4 completed 12.2% more tasks, 25.1% faster, at more than 40% higher rated quality. On a task chosen to be just outside the frontier (a business problem that needed careful reading of interview notes and numbers), consultants using AI were 19 percentage points less likely to reach the correct answer. The two kinds of task looked similarly hard. Lesson: you can't tell by looking which side of the frontier a task is on; you have to check.
- **Gains are largest for less experienced people.** Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond (QJE, 2025; 5,000+ customer-support agents, staggered rollout): issues resolved per hour rose about 15% on average (14% in the 2023 working paper). The working paper reports about 34% for novice and low-skilled agents and little effect for the most experienced, whose quality fell slightly. The tool seems to spread top performers' know-how to new staff.
- **What this means for a marketing team**: expect real time savings on drafting, rewriting, summarising and first-pass analysis; expect the biggest lift for junior staff; and expect confident wrong answers on tasks that need careful judgment of messy evidence. None of these studies measured campaign outcomes (sales, pipeline); they measured task speed and rated quality.

## Quality risk: everyone's output starts to look the same [research]

- Doshi & Hauser (Science Advances, 2024): writers who got story ideas from an LLM wrote stories rated more creative and better written, especially the less creative writers. But AI-assisted stories were more similar to each other than stories written without AI. The authors call it a social dilemma: each writer is better off, but the group produces a narrower range of ideas.
- For marketing, distinctiveness is the point (see positioning and brand-and-demand). If you and your competitors prompt similar models with similar briefs, you get similar headlines, angles and visuals. AI raises the floor and pulls the ceiling toward the average.
- Countermeasures [practitioner]: write the point of view, the customer insight and the brief yourself before prompting; feed the model your own material (call transcripts, reviews, win/loss notes; see customer-research); ask for many very different options and reject the obvious ones; have one person own the final voice.

## AI-generated ad creative

- **Platform tools [first-party]**: Meta Advantage+ creative generates image variations, backgrounds, text variations and format changes (for example, turning media into 9:16). Meta says background generation for Advantage+ catalog ads gave a 2–3% lift in conversions. Google Performance Max can build an asset group from your website and generate text, images, logos and video; Google's help pages say generated assets aren't guaranteed to pass ads policy and that you must review them for accuracy before they run. Treat platform performance claims as the platform marking its own homework.
- **Independent evidence [research]**: Hartmann, Exner & Domdey (International Journal of Research in Marketing, 2025) compared AI-generated and human-made marketing images. In a field test with more than 173,000 banner-ad impressions, some AI-generated images reached up to 50% higher click-through than human-made stock photos. One study, CTR only (not sales), and newer image models have changed since. Little independent evidence yet on AI-generated video or copy at scale.
- **How to use it**: AI makes the "creative volume and diversity" that automated campaigns need cheaper (see paid-acquisition). Use it to produce more *different* concepts, not 40 near-copies of one ad. Test generated creative against your best human creative in a proper split or lift test (see experimentation), and judge on cost per acquisition, not CTR.
- **Labels**: since 2025 Meta adds an "AI info" label to ads made or heavily edited with its generative tools and says it will try to detect third-party AI edits [first-party].

## Synthetic personas and "silicon samples" for research

- **The promise [research]**: Argyle et al. (Political Analysis, 2023) showed that GPT-3, given demographic backstories, reproduced many patterns in US survey answers ("algorithmic fidelity"). Brand, Israeli & Ngwe (HBS working paper 23-062) found GPT gave answers consistent with economic theory (downward-sloping demand) and willingness-to-pay estimates similar to human studies, and that fine-tuning on earlier survey data improved fit.
- **The limits [research]**:
  - Bisbee et al. (Political Analysis, 2024): ChatGPT personas matched average survey answers fairly well but with **smaller variance** than real people, gave different regression results, and changed with prompt wording and over time. Averages can look right while the spread is wrong.
  - Wang, Morgenstern & Dickerson (Nature Machine Intelligence, 2025; human studies with 3,200 participants): LLMs tend to misrepresent and "flatten" demographic groups, showing an outsider's stereotype rather than the range of views inside the group.
  - Brand et al. themselves found weak reproduction of differences between demographic groups.
- **Rule**: synthetic respondents are useful for drafting and stress-testing a survey or interview guide, generating hypotheses, and quick sanity checks. They are not evidence of demand, price acceptance or segment size. Segmentation and pricing decisions depend on variance and on subgroup differences, which is exactly what LLM samples get wrong. Confirm with real customers (see customer-research and pricing).

## AI content and search [first-party]

- Google (Search Central, Feb 2023): it rewards helpful content "however it is produced"; using automation to create content mainly to manipulate rankings breaks its spam policies.
- Since March 2024, "scaled content abuse" covers mass-produced, low-value pages by any method, including AI. See seo-and-ai-search and seo-content-and-architecture (programmatic pages). The test is value added, not the tool used.

## Disclosure and law

Not legal advice; see privacy-and-marketing-law and pr-and-influencers for wider coverage.
- **FTC, United States [first-party]**: Operation AI Comply (25 Sep 2024) brought five actions over deceptive AI claims and AI-enabled deception, including DoNotPay ("robot lawyer" claims; $193,000 settlement) and Ascend Ecom (AI income claims). The order against Rytr (an AI tool that generated reviews) was set aside in December 2025 under a new FTC majority, so enforcement priorities shift. What has not changed: claims about what your AI product does need evidence, and the Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (in force 21 Oct 2024) bans fake reviews and testimonials, explicitly including AI-generated ones.
- **EU AI Act Article 50 [first-party; details not re-verified]**: transparency duties apply from 2 August 2026, including labelling deepfakes and AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest (unless a human reviewed it and holds editorial responsibility). Check how this applies to your ads with counsel.
- **Consumer reaction to "made with AI" labels [research, mixed; not re-verified]**: several experiments report that disclosing AI involvement lowers perceived authenticity, trust and purchase intent, more for high-involvement products. Wang, Sturgis & de Kadt (Telematics and Informatics, 2026) found AI labels lowered perceived accuracy of content but had limited wider effects. An agency study (TBWA with Ideally, 2026) reported trust penalties that disclosure made worse [vendor]. Effects are measured on stated attitudes, not real purchases. Don't hide AI use where the law or platform requires a label; do avoid AI-generated people presented as real customers.

## Workflow: where AI helps and where it doesn't

| Task | Use AI? | How |
|---|---|---|
| Summarising interviews, reviews, support tickets | Yes | Ask for themes with verbatim quotes and source IDs; spot-check quotes against the originals |
| Competitor and market desk research | With care | Use it to find and organise sources; verify every number at the source |
| First drafts of copy, emails, briefs | Yes | Give it your positioning, customer language and point of view first |
| Ad and headline variants | Yes | Ask for very different angles; test with real spend |
| Survey and interview guide drafts | Yes | Pilot with real people; synthetic answers are not data |
| Data analysis and formulas | With care | Ask for the code or formula, run it yourself, check totals |
| Translation and localisation | With care | Native-speaker review for ads and legal text |
| Final factual claims, statistics, prices | No | A human checks every claim against a source |
| Customer quotes, reviews, testimonials | No | Only real, attributable words from real customers |
| Positioning, strategy, "what's our point of view" | Not as the author | Use it to challenge your draft, not to write it |
| Regulated claims (health, finance, legal) | No | Legal or compliance review |

## Common mistakes

- Assuming AI helps on every task; the "jagged frontier" means it can make careful analytical work worse while looking confident.
- Publishing AI drafts without adding first-hand data, examples or a point of view; the result reads like everyone else's.
- Treating synthetic personas as market research, especially for pricing or segment sizes.
- Letting AI invent statistics, citations, customer quotes or reviews.
- Generating many near-identical ad variants and calling it "creative diversity".
- Trusting platform claims about AI creative without your own holdout or split test.
- Measuring AI adoption by hours saved instead of by business outcomes.
