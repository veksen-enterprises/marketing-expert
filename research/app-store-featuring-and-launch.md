# App Store and Google Play featuring, charts and launch mechanics — Research Notes

Scope: how editorial featuring works on Apple's App Store and Google Play; what featuring and chart position do to downloads, and for how long; Apple In-App Events and Google Play Promotional Content (formerly "LiveOps"); launch tools (pre-orders, pre-registration, phased release); how charts are computed; burst campaigns and timing; awards and small-developer programs; and 2025–2026 policy changes (EU, Japan, US) as they affect discovery for a small app. Extends `research/local-and-consumer-apps.md` §2, which already covers product-page limits, ratings prompts, AdAttributionKit and the US Epic v. Apple anti-steering case; those are not repeated here. Researched 2026-10-05.

**Access caveat.** Most Apple and Google pages were read in full through a page fetcher on 2026-10-05 (tagged `[read 2026-10-05]`). Three paper PDFs were read as text (Ju/Zhao/Aral; Ifrach & Johari short paper; Apple's DSA report). Peer-reviewed journal pages (Wiley, INFORMS, SSRN) blocked fetching, so several papers are known from their abstracts only (`[read 2026-10-05; abstract]`). Items seen only in search-result summaries are tagged `[snippet-only]`. Vendor figures are tagged [vendor] and carry their sample and date.

## §1 Apple editorial featuring

1. **Apple, "Getting featured on the App Store"**. https://developer.apple.com/app-store/getting-featured/ — [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]. Nominate through "Featuring Nominations" in App Store Connect. Lead time: "please give our team a minimum of two weeks notice"; "for wider featuring consideration, we recommend submitting a nomination up to three months in advance". What can be nominated: new apps and games, significant updates, In-App Events, app launches. Editors look at user experience, UI design, innovation, uniqueness, accessibility, localization, and the product page ("compelling screenshots, app previews, and descriptions, as well as positive ratings and reviews"); for games also gameplay, art, controls, story, replayability, sound, technical performance, value. Placements on iPhone/iPad Today tab: App of the Day and Game of the Day ("handpicked, refreshed daily"), developer stories, collections, lists, In-App Events, pre-orders. Also: Mac Discover tab, Vision Pro Apps & Games tab, search results, product pages, collections on the Apps and Games tabs. "All apps and games are eligible" for App of the Day/Game of the Day regardless of age. Editors' Choice badge appears on the product page. "Featured selections are localized and regionally curated." When featured in some Today placements you get an App Store Connect notice and ready-made social media assets.
2. **App Store Connect Help, "Nominate your app for featuring"**. https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-featuring-nominations/nominate-your-app-for-featuring/ — [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]. Roles allowed: Account Holder, Admin, App Manager, Marketing. Nomination types: New Content, App Enhancements, App Launch. Fields: name, description, publish date or date range; optional related apps (up to 10), platforms, countries/regions, localizations, attached approved In-App Events, up to 5 supplemental URLs (documents, assets, TestFlight links), "helpful details" (accessibility, inclusivity, priority, team). Bulk CSV import (submitted on upload, no drafts). **This page says "a minimum lead time of 3 weeks"**, which differs from the two weeks on item 1. No guarantee: editors "consider these plans for potential featuring opportunities"; the page describes no acceptance or rejection notice.
3. **TechCrunch, "Apple now lets app developers apply to be featured on the App Store"**, 13 Nov 2024. https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/13/apple-now-lets-app-developers-apply-to-be-featured-on-the-app-store — [read 2026-10-05]. Featuring Nominations announced at WWDC June 2024, live in App Store Connect from Nov 2024; selected apps get "Promote Your App" assets. (Before this, developers used a web form; the in-console nomination is new since late 2024.)
4. **Apple Distribution International, "App Store – Second Report on Risk Assessment and Risk Mitigation Measures" (EU Digital Services Act), 27 Aug 2024, non-confidential version.** https://www.apple.com/legal/dsa/20241212_app-store_risk-assessment-report_non-confidential.pdf — [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]. Today tab editorial content "is drafted by human App Store editorial teams" (original stories, tips, how-to guides, interviews, App of the Day, Game of the Day, Now Trending, Collections, Our Favourites, Get Started); editors decide which categories to "pin" in which vertical positions; stories can be **personalised** using a user's purchase and download history. On the Games and Apps tabs "App Store editors look for high-quality apps across all categories, with a particular focus on new apps and apps with significant updates." Algorithmic recommendations use (i) app quality, (ii) app popularity, (iii) app sensitivities, (iv) context, drawing on downloads, transactions, browse/search signals and "fraud trust signals". **Charts: "Apps are selected for charts based on the most downloads in the App Store within approximately the past 24-hour period."** Search: main parameters are text relevance of title, keywords and category, plus user engagement such as number and quality of ratings and reviews.

## §2 Google Play editorial featuring and programs

5. **Google Play for business, "Getting featured on Google Play"** (redirects from play.google.com/console/about/guides/featuring/). https://google.play/business/guides/featuring/ — [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]. "We consider the quality of both your app or game, and its promotional assets." Four quality pillars: core value, user experience, technical quality, privacy and security. The only submission link on the page is a Google Form for **premium (paid) titles** with a proposed discount, judged on "app quality, historical performance, and the depth of the proposed discount"; 2026 seasonal deal windows (September Sale, Cyber Week, Year End Deals) are configured in Play Console. No general nomination form or lead time is stated there.
6. **Play Console Help, "Grow your startup app with help from Google Play" (Apps Innovation Corner).** https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16412111 — [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]. Eligibility: team of 1–30 people, "self-funded or has a small outside investment", rating 4.0 or higher, app launched no more than 2 years before submission, **developer based in the United States**. Placement: a collection "on the apps tab on the U.S. homepage", "refreshed at least quarterly". Apply through https://support.google.com/googleplay/contact/indie_corners.
7. **Play Console Help, "Create promotional content".** https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12932541 — [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]. Promotional content is "available for all games, and for apps that meet our eligibility criteria for Premium growth tools". Submit no earlier than 60 days before start; approval can take "up to four days"; **if you request featuring, submit at least 14 days before start**. Max duration four weeks; major updates fixed at 28 days; pre-registration announcements fixed at one week. Types: offers, time-limited events, major updates (sub-types: discount, value add, free reward, subscription trial, competition/challenge, real-time, special event). Featuring request quotas per quarter: games for a broad audience 4, apps for a broad audience up to 6, specific audiences 2. Content may appear on the store listing and on "the homepage and search results".
8. **Google Play for business, "Premium growth tools".** https://google.play/business/guides/premium-growth-tools/ — [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]. Apps (not games) must meet one of: ≥1.6M daily MAU over 3 months and ≥1.6M active installs; ≥2.0M daily MAU last month and ≥2.0M installs; ≥$40,000/month consumer spend in each of the last 3 months; ≥$50,000 last month. Lower thresholds for developers with $5M+ annual spend. Only some app categories are supported. Tools: promotional content, promotional content featuring, deep links, custom audience targeting, YouTube video. **Consequence: most small non-game apps cannot use Play promotional content at all; all games can.**
9. **Google Play for business, "Promotional content".** https://google.play/business/programs/promotionalcontent/ — [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]. Titles "can see over twice as many explore acquisitions during a featuring window": ~6,000 "high-quality" events from 2,000+ titles, Nov 2023–Jan 2024; "just over half" showed statistically significant uplift, with median uplift 106% among those. Note the selection: only events Google judged high quality, and the median is among the significant half.
10. **Android Developers Blog, "Supporting and rewarding great Apps and Games on Google Play"**, 2 Nov 2022. https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2022/11/supporting-and-rewarding-great-apps-and-games-on-google-play.html — [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]. LiveOps users saw "+3.6% increase in revenue and a +5.1% increase in 28DAU versus similar titles" (the 90th percentile of 70+ LiveOps vs control groups; "0.9 confidence interval"). LiveOps renamed Promotional Content. **Minimum 3.0-star rating to appear in Top Free charts from Feb 2023**; store-listing warnings for user-perceived crash/ANR rates above 8%.
11. **Play Console Help, "Evaluate your promotional content performance".** https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12932124 — [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]. "Featuring uplift is based on a holdback where each promotional content card is held back from a very small portion of users"; uplift is shown only when statistically significant. Useful: Google measures this with a real holdout, so a developer who qualifies gets a causal number for their own events.
12. **Android Developers Blog, "Introducing the Google Play Games Level Up program"**, Sept 2025. https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2025/09/introducing-google-play-games-level-up.html — [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]. Open to all games; requirements by July 2026 (Play Games Sidekick, achievements) and Nov 2026 (cloud save, seamless restore). Games meeting the guidelines get "featuring opportunities and Play Points boosters and quests" and more recommendations on Games home; new "You" tab (only where Play Points exists).
13. **Play Console Help, "Enroll in the Apps Experience program or revamped Level Up program"** and **Android Developers Blog, "A new era for choice and openness"**, 4 Mar 2026. https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/18018581 ; https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/03/a-new-era-for-choice-and-openness.html — [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]. Apps Experience Program: enrollment from 1 Sept 2026, requires **at least $1M earnings over the last 12 months**; benefit is a lower fee rate card (15% new installs / 20% existing installs for one-off purchases, before the billing fee). The help page says nothing about ranking or featuring; a search summary of developer.android.com/distribute/aep said "the program does not affect store ranking" `[snippet-only]`.
14. **Google Play for business, "Indie games"** and **Android Developers Blog, Indie Games Fund posts** (Latin America July 2025; Africa July 2026). https://google.play/business/programs/indiegames/ ; https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2025/07/google-plays-indie-games-fund-latin-america-returns-2025.html ; https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/07/Indie-Games-Fund-Africa.html — [first-party] programs page [read 2026-10-05]; fund posts [snippet-only]. Indie Games Fund Latin America: $2M to 10 studios (4th year, 2025), non-equity. Indie Games Fund Africa: $1M to 10 Sub-Saharan studios, $50k–$200k each, applications closed 31 July 2026. Indie Games Accelerator: online education and mentorship. These are funding/education, not guaranteed featuring. The Indie Games Festival (Europe, Japan, Korea) prize has historically included "promotion campaigns" `[snippet-only]`; no 2025–2026 festival page was found.
15. **Google Play, "The Latest from Google Play: Q2 2026".** https://google.play/the-latest/2026-q2/ — [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]. "Ask Play" AI assistant in the store; app discovery inside the Gemini app; large-screen badges; a new "reach" metric in Play Console showing app visibility; keyword recommendations that generate custom store listings for search trends; editorial spotlights (World Cup, NBA Finals, an anime campaign across 23 markets).
16. **Play Console Help, "App Discovery and Ranking".** https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9958766 — [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]. Main parameters: user relevance, app quality, editorial value (including Editors' Choice), ads (labelled), user experience. "Apps shown on Top Charts are heavily influenced by popularity"; search results "heavily influenced by relevance to the user's query"; "apps that have strong technical performance and a good user experience are generally favored". No formula and no time window for charts.

## §3 What featuring and chart position do to downloads

Research (causal or quasi-causal designs):

17. **Carare, O., "The Impact of Bestseller Rank on Demand: Evidence from the App Market"**, *International Economic Review* 53(3), 2012. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-2354.2012.00698.x — [research] [read 2026-10-05; abstract]. Daily top-100 paid-app ranks on Apple's App Store; today's rank causally raises tomorrow's demand. Willingness to pay "about $4.50 greater for a top ranked app than for the same unranked app"; effect "decline[s] steeply with rank at the top ranks, but remain[s] economically significant" for the first half of the top 100. Old data (App Store's early years), paid apps only.
18. **Garg, R. & Telang, R., "Inferring App Demand from Publicly Available Data"**, *MIS Quarterly* 37(4), 2013. https://doi.org/10.25300/misq/2013/37.4.12 — [research] [read 2026-10-05; abstract]. Rank-to-downloads curve for paid apps: the #1 paid iPhone app gets about **150 times** the downloads of the #200 app; on iPad about 120 times. Describes the steepness of the curve, not the causal effect of rank.
19. **Ifrach, B. & Johari, R., "Pricing a Bestseller: Sales and Visibility in the Marketplace for Mobile Apps"**, NetEcon 2013 (short paper); full version "The Impact of Visibility on Demand in the Market for Mobile Apps", SSRN 2444542, 2014. https://netecon.seas.harvard.edu/NetEcon13/Papers/Johari_netecon13.pdf ; https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.2444542 — [research] [short paper read 2026-10-05; SSRN version snippet-only]. Uses rank changes caused by other apps as a natural experiment. The indirect "visibility" effect is significant for ranks 1–20 of the overall top-paid chart: dropping one position within the top 5 cuts downloads by **5.4% on average (95% CI 2.6–8.2%)**. The SSRN summary says moving from #20 to #1 "can nearly double" demand `[snippet-only]`. Also found developers cycling prices to buy visibility.
20. **Ju, H., Zhao, M. A. & Aral, S., "Advertising Spillovers in Mobile Apps: Evidence from Ad Shutoffs and Store Rankings"**, arXiv 2504.16151 v2, 10 July 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16151 — [research, working paper] [read 2026-10-05]. Six free-to-play games from one US developer, iOS and Android, US storefront, 500 days in 2018–2019, spend across 85 ad networks; a global ad shutoff (days 75–98) **cut organic installs by 20–30%**. Panel estimate: every $100 spent ≈ 32 paid installs and 2.2 organic installs. Mechanism: paid installs lift **category chart rank**, which drives organic installs; once rank is controlled for, the spend–organic link disappears. Days with featuring spikes were removed from the sample. Caveats: one developer, games, pre-ATT data (2018–19), category charts not overall charts.
21. **Li, X., Bresnahan, T. & Yin, P.-L., "Paying Incumbents and Customers to Enter an Industry: Buying Downloads"**, working paper, SSRN 2834564, 2016. — [research, working paper] [snippet-only abstract; figure as cited in item 20]. Models developers paying users to download ("incentivized installs") to climb top lists, using one download-buying platform's private data; item 20 summarises the finding as $100 of purchased downloads improving rank by about 2.2%.
22. **Deng, Y., Lambrecht, A. & Liu, Y., "Spillover Effects and Freemium Strategy in the Mobile App Market"**, *Management Science* 69(9):5018–5041, 2023. https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2022.4619 — [research] [snippet-only abstract]. Launching a free version raised the paid version's daily ratings (a demand proxy) by 8.9% at the mean; driven partly by "enhanced app discovery" through chart visibility.
23. **Liang, C., Shi, Z. & Raghu, T. S., "The Spillover of Spotlight: Platform Recommendation in the Mobile App Market"**, *Information Systems Research* 30(4):1296–1318, 2019. https://doi.org/10.1287/isre.2019.0863 — [research] [read 2026-10-05; abstract via ASU repository]. Editor recommendations (featuring) spill over positively to the same developer's other apps and to the same app on the other platform (mainly through exposure); spillover to functionally similar apps from other developers is minimal. Strength depends on the featured app's price and rating. The abstract does not give the store, sample or effect sizes.
24. **Gianola, B., "Demand in mobile application markets and the role of top charts ranking"**, *Journal of European Competition Law & Practice* 16(3), 2025. https://doi.org/10.1093/jeclap/lpaf045 — [research] [read 2026-10-05; abstract]. Instrumental-variables study on Google Play; some reorderings of rank would raise consumer welfare, suggesting Google's ranking is not purely based on what users value. No effect size in the abstract.
25. **Askalidis, G., "The Impact of Large Scale Promotions on the Sales and Ratings of Mobile Apps: Evidence from Apple's App Store"**, arXiv 1506.06857, 2015/2016. https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.06857 — [research, working paper] [read 2026-10-05; abstract]. Four store-run promotions: free full-price promotions with tap-to-redeem drove the largest download increases; partial discounts hurt ratings in the short term; high-friction promotions favoured already-popular apps ("rich-get-richer").
26. **Sällberg, H., Wang, S. & Numminen, E., "The combinatory role of online ratings and reviews in mobile app downloads"**, *Journal of Marketing Analytics*, 2022. https://doi.org/10.1057/s41270-022-00171-w — [research] [read 2026-10-05]. Checked because search results described it as a featuring study; it is not. It studies ratings and reviews for 341 App Store apps and only uses rank as a control. Do not cite it for featuring effects.

Vendor and practitioner before/after data:

27. **Apptopia via TechCrunch, "Apple's 'App of the Day' featuring boosts downloads by 1747%, games by 792%"**, 24 Oct 2017. https://techcrunch.com/2017/10/24/apples-app-of-the-day-featuring-boosts-downloads-by-1747-games-by-792/ — [vendor] [read 2026-10-05]. 30 days of featured apps right after the iOS 11 redesign; compares downloads the day before vs after. App of the Day +1,747% average, Game of the Day +792%; weekday features larger (apps +2,172%) than weekend (+580%). Averages, single-day comparison, 2017.
28. **Sensor Tower via Marketing Dive, "Study: Apple's App Store redesign boosts downloads by up to 800%"**, 2018. https://www.marketingdive.com/news/study-apples-app-store-redesign-boosts-downloads-by-up-to-800/521916/ — [vendor] [read 2026-10-05]. Six months after the Sept 2017 redesign: Game of the Day ~800% average increase; App of the Day +685% (to ~16,800 weekly downloads); stories and lists ~200% median. 29% of featured publishers had under 10,000 iPhone downloads in the period (featuring is not only for big publishers).
29. **Phiture (Maggie Ngai), "Just How Impactful is Being Featured on the App Store?"**, 19 Mar 2019. https://phiture.com/asostack/just-how-impactful-is-being-featured-on-the-app-store-cb2185fb2e32/ — [vendor/practitioner; one app] [read 2026-10-05]. Headspace: App of the Day +125% browse conversion; relevant editorial stories ×2–3.5 units; less relevant stories +15–50%; position in a collection matters (2nd +180–220%, 4th +35%, 5th +8%).
30. **AppTweak case study, "How Pixel Federation grew +470% in organic app installs"**. https://www.apptweak.com/en/case-studies/pixel-federation-featurings — [vendor; one app] [read 2026-10-05]. Train Station 2, Game of the Day, US, 17 Oct 2023: +470% organic installs, +540% organic revenue. Comparison window not stated.
31. **Vendredi (Sylvain Corvaisier), "Getting Featured on the App Store: What It Is Actually Worth"**, 27 Aug 2026. https://vendredi-app.fr/blog/featured-by-apple/ — [practitioner] [read 2026-10-05]. Uplift lasts "roughly a week" with "most of it landing in the first day or two"; warns that 685% vs 1,747% reflects different samples, windows and median vs mean; the author's own featured app is no longer on the store.
32. **Sensor Tower, "The Best Days To Launch Or Promote Your iOS App — By Category (Q1 2015)".** https://sensortower.com/blog/best-days-launch-promote-ios-app-2015 — [vendor] [read 2026-10-05]. US iOS estimated downloads, Jan–Mar 2015: most categories peak at weekends; Business best Wed/Thu; Finance downloads midweek but revenue at weekends; Productivity flat.

## §4 In-App Events and Promotional Content

33. **Apple, "In-App Events".** https://developer.apple.com/app-store/in-app-events/ — [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]. Shown on the product page, in search results (for the app's name or searches for events), and in editorial and personalised recommendations on Today, Games and Apps tabs. Limits: up to **10 published** at once; up to **15 approved** in App Store Connect; each up to **31 days**; promoted up to **14 days before start**. Badges: Challenge, Competition, Live Event, Major Update, New Season, Premiere, Special Event. App Analytics reports event impressions, page views, opens, downloads by event, and notification opt-ins. **No Apple figure on effect.**
34. **AppTweak, "What Are In-App Events & How Do They Impact ASO?"**, 18 Sept 2024. https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/what-are-in-app-events-how-do-they-impact-aso — [vendor] [read 2026-10-05]. Contains no measured effect on downloads, conversion or keyword rank. Searches of AppTweak, Sensor Tower, Appfigures, SplitMetrics and Phiture found no published, sample-described study of In-App Event uplift. For Google Play the only effect data is Google's own (items 9–11).

## §5 Launch mechanics

35. **Apple, "Offering your apps for pre-order".** https://developer.apple.com/app-store/pre-orders/ — [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]. Release date 2–180 days after the pre-order is published (new apps); 2–365 days for existing apps entering new regions. Pre-orders show in search, the Today tab and the Games/Apps tabs (when featured). On release the app auto-downloads and the user is notified. Apple does **not** tell pre-order customers about date changes or removal.
36. **App Store Connect Analytics Help, "Pre-orders".** https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect-analytics/acquisition/pre-orders — [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]. "After launch, fulfilled pre-orders automatically flow into download reporting." "Pre-order volume can drive early visibility and contribute to stronger chart placement." Net pre-orders = placed minus cancelled; at least 5 pre-orders before data shows. (Apple does not say whether all fulfilled pre-orders count on day one in the 24-hour chart window of item 4, but the two statements together suggest they do.)
37. **App Store Connect Help, "Release a version update in phases".** https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/update-your-app/release-a-version-update-in-phases/ — [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]. Updates only, to users with automatic updates: day 1 1%, day 2 2%, 3 5%, 4 10%, 5 20%, 6 50%, 7 100%. Pause up to 30 days in total. Anyone can still download the update manually. It is a risk-control tool, not a discovery tool. (Google Play's equivalent is staged rollouts by percentage; not re-verified this session.)
38. **Play Console Help, "Build awareness for your apps with pre-registration".** https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859047 — [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]. Max **90 days**, then you must launch. Up to two titles in pre-registration at once. One pre-registration reward (a free in-app product) per campaign. At launch "all pre-registered users receive a push notification"; auto-install on eligible devices (Android M+, Play Store 19.2+; ≤200MB any network, 200MB–2GB Wi-Fi only, >2GB not eligible; not for under-13 or managed enterprise accounts). Conversion is defined as pre-registered users who installed within 14 days of availability.
39. **Google Play for business, "Pre-registration".** https://google.play/business/pre-registration/ — [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]. Recommends turning pre-registration on "3-6 weeks before your planned launch date". Only case figure: League of Legends: Wild Rift "nearly 40M pre-registrations". No published pre-registration-to-install conversion benchmark was found from Google or vendors.
40. **Apple, App Review Guidelines (introduction and §3.2.2; §5.6 Developer Code of Conduct).** https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/ — [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]. "If you attempt to cheat the system (for example, by trying to … manipulate ratings or App Store discovery) your apps will be removed from the store and you will be expelled." Prohibits attempts to "inflate your chart rankings with paid, incentivized, filtered, or fake feedback, or engage with third-party services to do so". **Paid ads are allowed; paying users to install (incentivized installs) to climb charts is not.**
41. **TechCrunch, "Google gets better at flagging apps trying to fake their way into the Play Store's top charts"**, 31 Oct 2016. https://techcrunch.com/?p=1409382 — [read 2026-10-05]. Google rolled out a detection and filtering system against illegitimate ways to boost chart rank; no formula disclosed.
42. **Apple Ads, "News".** https://ads.apple.com/app-store/news — [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]. 17 Dec 2025: "Early next year, we'll begin running additional ads in search results — where 65% of downloads start"; new ads appear further down the results. Press reports give 3 March 2026 as start, UK then Japan, all markets by end of March `[snippet-only]`. 14 Apr 2025: Apple Search Ads renamed Apple Ads. 8 Jun 2026: custom creative for Today tab and search ads "later in 2026".
43. **Apple WWDC25, "What's new in App Store Connect".** https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/328/ — [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]. **App Store Tags**: generated by Apple's large language models from metadata and other sources, human-reviewed; developers can deselect tags; tapping a tag opens a curated collection; tags show beside categories on the Search page and with the app in results. **Custom product pages can now have keywords** and replace the default page in search for those keywords, without App Review.

## §7 Policy changes 2025–2026 that affect discovery

EU (Digital Markets Act):
47. **Apple Newsroom, "Apple announces changes for apps in the European Union"**, 18 Aug 2026, effective 1 Oct 2026. https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/08/apple-announces-changes-for-apps-in-the-european-union/ — [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]. EU App Store commission: 26% standard / 15% reduced with Apple IAP; 20%/10% with alternative payment; 15%/10% when linking out to the web; 5% "Core Technology Commission" for apps distributed through alternative marketplaces or the web; developers may now offer IAP alongside other payment options. Alternative marketplace or web distribution open to firms meeting a financial-stability test, public companies, VC-backed firms, audited firms, governments, schools and non-profits. ## What this means for a small app team

- **Nominate every launch and major update, early.** Apple's nomination is free and inside App Store Connect; Apple's own pages give two different minimums (two weeks on the featuring page, three weeks in the help page), and recommend up to three months for wider consideration. Plan on three weeks minimum and aim for 6–12 weeks. No reply is promised. [first-party; 1, 2]
- **Featuring is human editorial and regional; the product page and quality are judged.** Editors weigh design, UX, accessibility, localization and the product page; Today-tab stories may be personalised by download history, so a feature reaches people who already download similar apps. [first-party; 1, 4]
- **A feature is a spike, not a baseline.** Vendor before/after numbers range from roughly +200% (lists and stories) to +700–1,700% (App/Game of the Day) on the day, with the gain mostly in the first one or two days and largely gone within about a week. These come from 2017–2023 samples and single-day or undisclosed windows; no peer-reviewed study measures Today-tab featuring directly. Plan to capture the spike (ratings prompt, onboarding, email capture), not to live on it. [vendor; 27, 28, 30, 31]
- **Featuring helps your other apps and your app on the other store.** Exposure from editor recommendations spills over to the same developer's other apps and the same app on the other platform, but barely to similar apps from other developers. [research; 23]
- **Chart rank matters and is steep at the top.** Apple says charts use the most downloads in about the past 24 hours. Research shows a causal rank effect (one position in the top 5 ≈ 5% of downloads; #20 to #1 ≈ double demand) and a very steep curve (#1 ≈ 150× #200 for paid apps). But all of this comes from top-20 to top-100 paid charts in the App Store's early years (papers published 2012–2014); a small app far down the charts gets little from rank itself. [first-party; 4] [research; 17, 18, 19]
- **Paid installs can raise organic installs through category rank, and a concentrated launch fits Apple's 24-hour window.** One field study found an ad shutoff cut organic installs 20–30%, working through category rank (6 games, 2018–19, pre-ATT). That supports concentrating paid, PR and community pushes into the same one or two days, especially to reach a category chart. Buying incentivized installs to climb charts breaks Apple's rules and Google filters it. [first-party; 4, 40, 41] [research; 20, 21]
- **Pre-orders (iOS) and pre-registration (Play) bank launch-day installs.** Apple says fulfilled pre-orders count as downloads and "can … contribute to stronger chart placement"; Play pushes a notification to all pre-registrants and auto-installs on eligible devices. Limits to plan around: Apple 2–180 days for a new app; Play max 90 days, one reward, two titles at once; Google suggests starting 3–6 weeks before launch. No independent pre-registration conversion data exists. [first-party; 35, 36, 38, 39]
- **Phased release is for safe updates, not for launch or discovery.** [first-party; 37]
- **In-App Events are free on iOS and appear in search and on editorial surfaces; on Play, promotional content is open to all games but only to large apps.** Apple: 10 live, 31 days max, 14 days of pre-promotion. Play: submit 60 days to 4 days before; 14+ days ahead if you want featuring; quarterly featuring quotas. Google's own holdout data shows a median 106% uplift among the roughly half of high-quality events that showed any significant uplift; there is no independent data for either store. [first-party; 7, 8, 9, 10, 33]
- **On Google Play the editorial routes for a small non-game app are narrow.** The public featuring form is for discounted premium titles; the Apps Innovation Corner takes US-based teams of 1–30 people with a 4.0+ rating and an app under two years old; the Apps Experience Program needs $1M a year and gives fee cuts, not reach. Keep at least a 3.0 rating, the Top Free chart minimum since Feb 2023. [first-party; 5, 6, 10, 13]
- **More ads in Apple search push organic results down.** Since March 2026 Apple runs several ad slots in search results, where Apple says 65% of downloads start. For a small app, rank #1 organic for a keyword may now sit below more than one ad; budget for Apple Ads on your own brand name and core keywords. [first-party; 42]
- **Use the new free surfaces.** Check and prune App Store Tags (they put you in tag collections and appear in search results) and add keywords to custom product pages. [first-party; 43]
- **Opt in or out of US third-party catalog sharing on purpose.** By default since 22 July 2026 your Play listing appears in enrolled rival US Android stores, with installs still going through Play on Play's terms. For most small apps this is extra reach at no cost; opt out if a brand or partner reason requires it. [first-party; 52]
- **Alternative marketplaces (EU, Japan) are not a discovery channel yet for a small app.** None publishes audited iOS user numbers, one EU store (Setapp Mobile) closed in Feb 2026, and apps distributed outside the App Store lose App Store search and featuring. Their value is mainly fees and control, and only if you already bring your own audience. [first-party; 47, 50] [practitioner/press; 49]
- **Fees, briefly:** Small Business Program 15% on iOS (no discovery benefit); Google Play 10% on the first $1M per year (plus 5% billing fee in major markets) from mid-2026; EU and Japan have separate tiered rates. [first-party; 45, 47, 50, 54]

## Folklore and weak claims

- **"Apple updates the store / features new apps on Thursday, so launch Wednesday."** This dates from the pre-2017 weekly "New & Noteworthy" refresh. Apple's own pages say App of the Day is "refreshed daily" and featuring is regionally curated; no current Apple source names a weekday. The only data found on weekdays is Sensor Tower Q1 2015 (most categories download more at weekends) and Apptopia 2017 (weekday features gave bigger relative lifts). Treat day-of-week launch advice as [rule-of-thumb]. [1, 27, 32]
- **"Burst campaigns of ~120,000 US downloads in 2–3 days get you into the charts" (Moburst, SplitMetrics and others).** No sample or date given; vendors sell these campaigns. The direction is supported (item 20) but the thresholds are not. [rule-of-thumb]
- **"Google Play charts use a 7–14 day install window weighted by recency / day-1 retention / uninstall rate."** Repeated by several ASO blogs; Google only says charts are "heavily influenced by popularity" and adds a 3.0-star minimum. No primary source for the window. [rule-of-thumb; 10, 16]
- **"Review velocity beats average rating for ranking."** No source found. [rule-of-thumb]
- **"Google Play has a Featuring Nomination form for apps launched in the last ~120 days."** Stated by AppTweak (Jan 2026) and ASOMobile; no Google page with this form or the 120-day rule was found. The only form on Google's featuring guide is for discounted premium titles. [vendor, unverified; 5]
- **"95% of App Store featured apps and 96% of Play featured apps have ratings of 4.0+."** AppTweak blog, no sample or date. Plausible but unsourced. [vendor]
- **"Featuring lifts downloads 1,747%."** A 2017 average from one month after the iOS 11 redesign, day-before vs day-after. Often quoted with no date. Later Sensor Tower numbers (+685% App of the Day) are lower; the two differ in sample, window and mean vs median. [vendor; 27, 28, 31]
- **"In-App Events boost keyword rankings / conversion by X%."** No published, sample-described study found from any vendor. [none]
- **"Winning an Apple Design Award guarantees featuring."** Apple's 2026 announcement mentions no featuring or prize. [44]

## Open questions

- No peer-reviewed study with a causal design measures the effect of today's Apple editorial formats (Today-tab stories, App of the Day) or of Google Play featuring on downloads, or how fast the effect decays. The academic chart-rank evidence uses early App Store paid-app charts (papers from 2012–2014) and may not carry over to free-app category charts.
- Do pre-order fulfilments all land inside Apple's ~24-hour chart window on release day? Apple says only that they "can … contribute to stronger chart placement".
- No independent data on Play pre-registration conversion rates or on how much the pre-registration reward adds.
- No independent data on In-App Event or Play promotional-content uplift for small apps; Google's numbers are from its own holdouts on large or high-quality events.
- How much did Apple's extra search ad slots (March 2026) reduce organic taps for non-advertisers? No vendor study found yet.
- Does sharing a Play listing with third-party US stores (from July 2026) produce meaningful installs? Too new; no data.
- Will Apple's alternative-marketplace apps in the EU or Japan ever be discoverable inside the App Store? Nothing in Apple's 2025–2026 announcements says so.
- Status of the Epic–Google settlement after the summer 2026 hearing, and whether Registered App Stores reach the US. Recheck.
- Ifrach & Johari full paper (SSRN 2444542) and Liang, Shi & Raghu (ISR 2019) full texts were not read; effect sizes beyond the abstracts are unverified.
