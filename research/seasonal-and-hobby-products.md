# Seasonal and hobby products: one-peak demand, crafter channels, and paying for a free hobby tool

Research date: 2026-10-05. **Not legal advice.**

**Scope.** Products whose demand peaks once or twice a year and whose users are hobbyists or crafters: a free or freemium planning tool, pattern or template library, colour or supply database, project gallery, hobby newsletter or group. Covers: planning the year around a peak and when search interest starts rising; keeping a seasonal audience between peaks; Pinterest, Facebook groups, YouTube and TikTok creators, Reddit, Etsy and craft fairs as channels; earning money from a free hobby tool (freemium, supply-brand and marketplace affiliate programmes, kits and templates, sponsorship); user galleries as content and search pages; research on hobbyist motivation.

**Access caveat (2026-10-05).** Pages were fetched directly with a generic browser User-Agent (curl or a fetch tool). `[read 2026-10-05]` = the page was read; `[read 2026-10-05; abstract]` = only the abstract or summary; `[snippet-only]` = only search-engine text was seen. Etsy's www.etsy.com legal pages returned bot checks (403 / JavaScript stub); Etsy Help Center articles were read through the Help Center's public article API, and the 10-K through Etsy's investor site. Google Trends' data API returned 429 (rate limited), so **no first-hand Trends curves were pulled for craft terms**. OpenAlex was rate-limited; ACM Digital Library returned 403. The Crafts Council web page was blocked but its full PDF report was read. TikTok's support page did not render. Reddit's rules API returned no data.

## What this means

- **Plan backwards from the peak.** Etsy's own filing shows craft commerce peaks in Q4 every year (5). Pinterest's tool and guides say interest "builds, peaks, and cools" and advise being ready "a couple months ahead" (9, 15); NRF says about two in five holiday shoppers start before November (6). For a planning tool, people plan before they buy supplies, so the useful window opens earlier still. **No study measured the lead time for craft-planning searches**; check your own terms in Google Trends and Pinterest Trends, year over year (1, 2, 9).
- **Read Trends curves as shapes, not counts.** Both Google and Pinterest index each curve to its own peak (1, 9). Google samples and adds noise to low-volume terms (1). Compare the same term across years and look at when the rise starts, not the absolute level.
- **Keep seasonal pages on fixed URLs.** One evergreen URL per season keeps its links and history (3). Google's own debugging guide treats seasonal drops as normal and says to compare year over year before changing anything (2).
- **Pinterest rewards being saved to boards.** Related Pins, over 40% of engagement in 2017, mainly recommends pins saved to the same boards as the one being viewed (10). New pins with few saves rely on text matching from their description and link (10). Good descriptions, a claimed site and Rich Pins help the first saves happen (11). Idea Pins are gone as a format (13).
- **Galleries are search pages only if they carry text and are moderated.** Google's image guide wants descriptive alt text, file names and nearby text (32). Its spam guide wants moderation, `ugc` links and `noindex` for new users (31). Research suggests feedback on first posts keeps newcomers posting (35, snippet-only), and crafters join communities mostly for inspiration, learning and feedback, not reputation (33).
- **Two kinds of user.** Serious hobbyists have a "leisure career" and come back all year; project-based users do one holiday project and leave (34). Plan retention and pricing separately for each.
- **Facebook groups make the admin responsible.** Approving a violating post counts against the group (17). Pages can join or run groups only where the admin allows (18). Membership questions and rules give a light filter (19, 20).
- **Affiliate income comes with rules that hit seasonal tools.** Amazon's links can't go in unsolicited emails or printed material, a qualifying purchase window is short, and new accounts must make sales within a probation period (27). Etsy's creator cookie is only 7 days and payouts wait for the return window (25). FTC disclosure must sit next to the link (28). The Etsy API requires a trademark notice and a review for commercial use (26).
- **Selling templates is a known path.** A free community with a pattern store reached 100,000 patterns sold in 2009 (29). On Etsy, digital items must be designed by the seller (23), and every listing costs $0.20 plus 6.5% of the sale (22).
- **Craft fairs matter for makers who sell.** UK buyers still prefer buying finished craft from the maker at fairs and markets, including younger buyers (24). That is about the tool's users' customers, not about the tool; fairs are where sellers who might use a planning tool gather.

## Folklore and weak claims

- **"Post seasonal content 45 days / 90 days / 3 months ahead on Pinterest."** Widely repeated by Pinterest consultants. Pinterest's own guidance says "a couple months ahead" for campaigns (15); no published test compares lead times. [rule-of-thumb]
- **"Pinterest users start Christmas planning in April / September."** From one 2020 Pinterest post in a pandemic year, with no method (14). [vendor]
- **"Pinterest Predicts is 80% accurate."** Self-graded by Pinterest with its own definition of "came true" (16). [vendor]
- **"Fresh pins are favoured."** A 2017 Pinterest paper discusses activating fresh content (10), and many guides say Pinterest prefers new pins; no current Pinterest document states a ranking boost. [practitioner]
- **Pinterest keyword placement rules** ("primary keyword in the first 35–45 characters", "board titles weigh most"). From third-party guides; Pinterest only says the first 40 title characters are most visible (12). [practitioner]
- **"Craft fair visitors convert at 10%"** and booth sales ranges. From vendor-directory blogs with no method. Not used. [snippet-only; weak]
- **Craft market size reports** (paid "market research" sites). Methods not disclosed. Not used.
- **Etsy affiliate commission of 4%.** Repeated by third-party sites; Etsy's page does not state a rate (25).

## Open questions

- No study measures how many weeks before a holiday craft-**planning** searches start rising, separately from gift-shopping searches. A first-hand Google Trends pull for the business's own terms would answer it for that case.
- No published data on freemium conversion or churn in seasonal consumer tools; whether a "season pass" or annual plan beats monthly billing for one-peak products is untested in public data.
- No primary data on Facebook group reach for brand-run vs member-run groups, or on how much traffic a group sends to an outside tool.
- Etsy's current affiliate commission rate and API Terms of Use text (403 from etsy.com) were not read on the primary page.
- Amazon category commission rates and the 180-day rule were not read on a primary page (JavaScript).
- TikTok's branded content rules were not read (page did not render); YouTube's were.
- Burke et al. (35) and Stebbins 1982 (34) were seen only through summaries.
- No evidence was found on whether supply-brand sponsorships of free hobby tools affect user trust.
