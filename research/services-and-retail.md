## Part A: Professional services

1. **Hinge Research Institute, *Referral Marketing for Professional Services Firms*** (Hinge Marketing; PDF https://hingemarketing.com/uploads/hinge-research-referral-marketing.pdf ; blog https://hingemarketing.com/blog/story/study-reveals-the-role-of-reputation-and-relationships-in-referral-marketin). Survey of **523 professional services firms**. **81.5%** of firms received referrals from people who had never worked with them. Firms said referrals came from general reputation (**46.4%**) and expertise (**48.1%**) more than from client relationships (**23.1%**). A CNBC article on the study dates it to 2016 or earlier (https://www.cnbc.com/2016/04/01/...). Exact publication year not confirmed (likely 2015–2016). [vendor] [snippet-only] — Hinge sells marketing to professional services firms; these are firm self-reports, not buyer behaviour.
2. **Hinge, *Professional Services: How Buyers Buy*** (https://hingemarketing.com/library/article/how_buyers_buy_professional_services_buyers_study). Surveyed **822 buyers and 533 providers** across technology, accounting/finance, AEC, management consulting and government contracting. Buyers find a new firm by asking another person **71%** of the time; searching online **11%**. Year of the survey not confirmed in snippet (older study, likely early 2010s) [vendor] [snippet-only].
3. **Hinge, *High Growth Study 2024: Executive Summary*** (https://hingemarketing.com/library/article/high-growth-study-2024-executive-summary). 9th edition, **824 firms** in six professional services industry groups. "High growth" = at least **20% compound annual growth over three years**. Hinge says high-growth firms grow "4X faster" and are "almost 2X as profitable" [vendor] [snippet-only]. Correlational; firms self-report; the definition selects winners, so the "advantages" are not shown to cause growth.
4. **Enns, B. *The Win Without Pitching Manifesto*** (2010 [publisher/year not re-verified]; https://www.winwithoutpitching.com/books/the-win-without-pitching-manifesto). Twelve "proclamations" for creative firms. Defines a pitch as trying to win approval "by giving [ideas] away for free". Advises specialisation, diagnosing before prescribing, refusing free creative/strategy work, and avoiding long speculative proposals [practitioner] [snippet-only + secondary summaries]. Enns also wrote *Pricing Creativity* (2018) on value pricing with priced options [not re-verified].
5. **Baker, D. C. *The Business of Expertise*** (2017 [publisher/year not re-verified]). Positioning for expert firms: **vertical** (one industry; easier to find prospects, follow clients between jobs, charge more) vs **horizontal** (one discipline across industries; more variety, harder to find prospects and get referrals). Lead with one dimension [practitioner] [secondary: book summaries, shortform/heliosdesign].
6. **Baker, R. J. *Implementing Value Pricing: A Radical Business Model for Professional Firms*** (Wiley [edition year not re-verified]) and VeraSage Institute. Argues the billable hour prices effort, not value; fixed prices agreed before the work transfer scope risk to the firm, which buyers will pay a premium for [practitioner] [secondary: book reviews, interviews].
7. **SPI Research, *Professional Services Maturity Benchmark*** 2025 and 2026 (via Kantata/Workday PDF hosts and Certinia/Deltek/Rocketlane blogs). Billable utilisation fell from **73.2% (2021) to 68.9% (2024)** and to **66.4% (2025)**, the lowest in SPI's history; SPI treats 70–75% as a healthy target; top-maturity firms exceed 80% [vendor: SPI's sponsors sell PS automation software] [snippet-only + secondary]. Sample is mostly tech/consulting service organisations, not agencies or law firms.
8. Practitioner ideas not looked up this session [not re-verified]: Maister, *Managing the Professional Service Firm* (1993) and *The Trusted Advisor* (2000) on leverage and trust; Weiss, *Value-Based Fees*; Jonathan Stark on productised services and pricing; Philip Morgan, *The Positioning Manual for Technical Firms*.

## Part B: Retail / CPG

9. **Ehrenberg-Bass** (Sharp 2010; Romaniuk & Sharp 2016; Romaniuk 2018) — penetration, mental and physical availability, distinctive assets. Full notes in research/brand-growth-channels.md.
10. **Ehrenberg, A., Hammond, K. & Goodhardt, G. "The after-effects of price-related consumer promotions." *Journal of Advertising Research* 34, 1994, 11–22.** Study of large-scale price promotions in many grocery categories: no measurable after-effect on later sales or loyalty. About **70%** of promotion-period buyers had bought the brand in the prior six months, **80%** in the prior year, **93%** in the prior 2.5 years; promotions rarely draw buyers of untried brands [research] [snippet-only: Semantic Scholar].
11. **Mela, C., Gupta, S. & Lehmann, D. "The Long-Term Impact of Promotion and Advertising on Consumer Brand Choice." *Journal of Marketing Research*, 1997.** Over eight years of panel data, frequent promotions made consumers more price- and promotion-sensitive [research] [title from search; finding not re-verified this session].
12. **Nielsen (2014)**, reported by FoodNavigator (https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2014/10/09/Three-quarters-of-CPG-promotions-don-t-break-even-Nielsen/): almost **three-quarters** of CPG promotions fail to break even, from >1 million UPCs and 39 million promotional events. Later NielsenIQ material: about **two-thirds (67%)** don't break even. Causes named: diminishing returns to promotion frequency and overuse of deep discounts [vendor] [secondary + snippet-only]. McKinsey: **72%** of US trade promotions lose money [vendor: consultancy] [snippet-only].
13. **Trade spend share**: Strategy& (PwC) reported US trade spending above **$200 billion** a year and about **20% of gross sales** for most CPG companies [vendor: consultancy] [secondary, quoted on Wikipedia/blogs; year not confirmed]. Broker/consultant blogs say **15–25%** of gross sales, higher (20–27%) for emerging brands [rule-of-thumb] [secondary].
14. **FTC staff, *Report on the Use of Slotting Allowances in the Retail Grocery Industry*, November 2003** (https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2003/11/ftc-releases-grocery-industry-slotting-allowance-report). Case studies in five categories (bread, hot dogs, ice cream, shelf-stable pasta, salad dressing). Wide variation; average slotting fee per item per regional chain for initial ~6 months about **$6,819–$10,625** in some categories (snippet via secondary); national launch slotting from just under **$1 million to over $2 million** (Supermarket News). FTC says small, possibly unrepresentative sample [first-party: regulator] [snippet-only + secondary]. 20+ years old; do not use as current prices.
15. **Tropicana 2009 redesign.** Bloomberg (April 2009) citing Information Resources Inc.: Pure Premium unit sales fell **20%** from 1 January to 22 February 2009; dollar sales down **$33 million** to $137 million; old pack restored within about two months [secondary: Packaging Digest, teaching cases quoting Bloomberg]. Confounded by the recession and competitor pricing; one brand, not a study.
16. **Heilman, C., Lakishyk, K. & Radas, S. "An empirical investigation of in-store sampling promotions." *British Food Journal* 113(10), 2011, 1252–1266.** Field data from a US grocery chain sampling programme, six products over six weekends. Sampling raised same-day sales strongly (snippet: up to ~300% on the day); interaction with the person giving samples or with other samplers increased buying [research, single chain, quasi-experimental] [snippet-only].
17. **Velocity definitions**: SPINS, *Mastering Sales Velocity* (https://www.spins.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/SPINS-Mastering-Sales-Velocity.pdf): units/dollars per store per week, per TDP (total distribution points), and per $MM ACV (all-commodity volume, which adjusts for store size) [vendor: data provider; definitions only] [snippet-only]. Broker blogs quote "8–12 units per store per week" as healthy for natural snacks/beverages [rule-of-thumb] [secondary] — category-dependent, do not generalise.
18. **Retail media size**: eMarketer forecast US retail media ad spending **$58.8B (2025)** and **$69.3B (2026)**; US "commerce media" $83B in 2026 [vendor: forecaster] [snippet-only]. Amazon advertising revenue **$68.6B in 2025 (+22%)**; Walmart global advertising **$6.4B in 2025 (+46%)**, Walmart Connect US +41% in Q4 [secondary: AdExchanger, Marketplace Pulse summarising company earnings; Walmart's total likely includes VIZIO after its acquisition — not checked].

## Open questions

- Hinge referral study and How Buyers Buy: exact fieldwork years and question wording not confirmed. Is there a newer Hinge referral dataset?
- No independent (non-vendor) research found on referral rates or marketing ROI for professional services firms.
- No controlled evidence found that value pricing beats hourly pricing on profit; current support is case and practitioner argument.
- Utilisation benchmarks (SPI) cover mostly tech services organisations; agency and law-firm benchmarks not found this session.
- Current slotting fee levels are undocumented publicly; the FTC 2003 report is the last government data found.
- Trade spend share: primary Strategy&/PwC report and year not located.
- Retail media incrementality: no independent study found on how much retail media spend is incremental vs paying for sales that would happen anyway.
- Sampling: long-term repeat effect of in-store sampling not confirmed from the Heilman et al. abstract.
