# Can a new solo practitioner message friends, family and former colleagues under Canada's anti-spam law? (checked 2026-10-28)

Question: a new solo practitioner in Canada wants to tell people they know, by email, text or DM, that they are taking clients. Do CASL's family and personal-relationship exemptions cover friends, family, former colleagues and LinkedIn connections? What does the referral exemption allow? What changes in the US?

## 1. A "taking clients" message is a CEM, and DMs and texts count

- A message saying you are taking clients encourages commercial activity, so it is a commercial electronic message (CEM). [law, 3, fetch summary; s.1(2); in force 2026]
- "Electronic message" includes "text, sound, voice or image"; an "electronic address" includes email, instant messaging and telephone accounts. [law, 3, fetch summary; s.1(1)]
- CRTC staff: SMS is covered, and so are DMs on "Facebook Messenger and LinkedIn messaging". A "one-way general broadcast" on social media, such as a wall post, is not. [first-party, 4, fetch summary; staff view, 2026]
- A message asking for consent is itself a CEM, so "may I tell you about my practice?" needs an exemption too. [law, 3, fetch summary; s.1(3)] Consent cannot be gathered by an opt-out or a pre-ticked box. [first-party, 7, fetch summary; 2012]
- A live two-way voice call between individuals is outside s.6. [law, 3, fetch summary; s.6(8)]

## 2. The family exemption is narrow and needs past contact

- "Family relationship": related "through a marriage, common-law partnership or any legal parent-child relationship", and the two "have had direct, voluntary, two-way communication". [law, 1, fetch summary; verbatim fragments; SOR/2013-221 s.2(a)]
- The RIAS aims to keep it "limited to close family or personal relationships" and names no siblings, grandparents or cousins. [law, 5, fetch summary; 2013]

## 3. The personal exemption is read strictly

- "Personal relationship": "direct, voluntary, two-way communications" and it "would be reasonable to conclude that they have a personal relationship", weighing "the sharing of interests, experiences, opinions and information evidenced in the communications, the frequency of communication, the length of time since the parties communicated or whether the parties have met in person". [law, 1, fetch summary; verbatim fragments; s.2(b)]
- No time limit is set. Time since last contact is one factor among several. [law, 1 and 5, fetch summary]
- CRTC staff "intends to strictly interpret" the term. The other person must know your real identity. Clicking "like", accepting a "Friend" or clicking "Follow" "will generally be insufficient". [first-party, 4, fetch summary; staff view, 2026]
- Only individuals have one. "Someone who sends a CEM on behalf of a corporation may not claim to have a personal relationship with the recipient." [first-party, 4, fetch summary] So a practitioner who has incorporated may lose the exemption for messages sent for the company. [derived]
- Former colleagues are not named in any source read. A colleague you talk with by choice, recently, plausibly fits; one you have not spoken to in years, or a LinkedIn connection you never wrote to, likely does not. [derived from 1, 4]
- For loose contacts, the remaining routes: a phone call or meeting, then express consent; or, for B2B only, implied consent where the person gave you their work address and the offer fits their job. [law, 3, fetch summary; s.10(9)(c)] A consumer service such as a dietitian's rarely fits "business, role, functions or duties". [derived]

## 4. Referrals: one message, full name, say why

- After a referral, the first CEM needs no consent if the referrer has a business, non-business, family or personal relationship with both sender and recipient, the message gives the referrer's full name, and it says it was sent because of the referral. [law, 1, fetch summary; s.4(1); in force 2026]
- "Only one CEM may be sent without obtaining the consent of the recipient." It still needs sender details and an unsubscribe. [first-party, 4, fetch summary] Sender details include a mailing address; an SMS can unsubscribe by reply ("STOP") or a link. [first-party, 6, fetch summary; 2012]
- If the friend writes to their own friend, that is the friend's message to someone they know. [derived from 3, s.6(5)(a)] The RIAS tells "forward-to-a-friend" promoters to pass the relationship limits on to forwarders to reduce liability. [law, 5, fetch summary; 2013]

## 5. United States: much looser

- Email: CAN-SPAM needs no consent; a commercial email still needs an opt-out and postal address (`small-bets-direct.md` item 2). [first-party, cited note]
- Texts: the Do Not Call rules apply to "text messages to wireless telephone numbers". A caller is not liable for messaging a registered number if they have a "personal relationship", defined as "any family member, friend, or acquaintance". That covers the loose contacts CASL leaves out. [law, 8, fetch summary; US federal; current 2026-10-23] Autodialer consent rules were not checked.
