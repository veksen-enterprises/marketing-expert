# Is seeding a dating app with fake, staff, imported or AI profiles treated as deception? (checked 2026-10-27)

Question: `knowledge/marketplaces.md` tells a cold-start founder to "act as the supplier yourself" and to try "importing existing listings (with permission)". A dating app gets that playbook, but its supply is people. Do regulators and courts treat profiles or chats run by the company (staff, bots, AI), or copied profiles, as deception, and with what outcome? Kept apart: (a) fake profiles that message users, (b) a disclosed house profile, (c) real people's profiles or photos reused without consent.

## 1. Company-run fake profiles that message users: deception, with money paid back

1. JDI Dating ran 18 sites. Messages "were almost always from fake, computer-generated profiles" ("Virtual Cupids") made to "closely mimic the profiles of real people". A small "v in a C" icon was the only sign. The FTC got $616,165 in redress. The order bars failing to disclose clearly "that they will receive communications from virtual profiles who are not real people". [legal, 1, read; fetch summary; FTC settlement, no court finding; one operator]
2. Ashley Madison: the FTC and 13 states plus DC alleged messages were presented as "from actual women" but came from "fake engager profiles". Staff created 24,414 female engager profiles "to entice users to upgrade". [unverified: the FTC release (ftc.gov/node/45098) quotes "fake engager profiles" and gives $8.75 million suspended on $1.6 million, but no profile count and not "to entice users to upgrade"; the complaint was not read] The $8.75 million judgment was suspended on payment of $1.6 million in total. [legal, 2–3, read; fetch summary; settlement bundled with a data-breach case; profile count from a law-firm summary of the complaint]
3. Germany, two final or unappealed first-instance rulings. LG Berlin (2022): profiles created and run by the operator ("iNutzer"), disclosed only in the terms, were misleading under § 5 and § 5a UWG; the fact must appear at least on the home page or somewhere as obvious. [corrected 2026-10-27: vzbv's own case page (vzbv.de/urteile/dating-portal-muss-deutlich-auf-fake-profile-hinweisen) dates the judgment 27 January 2022, not 17 February, marks it "rechtskräftig", and asks for disclosure clearly before registration; it does not cite § 5 or § 5a UWG] LG Flensburg (2022): paid chats with hired staff playing invented characters made the advertised promise of meeting someone false; the operator withdrew its appeal. [legal, 9–10, read; fetch summary; two regional courts; brought by the consumer federation]
4. UK: the CMA's advice says don't "mislead customers by creating your own dating profiles to communicate with them", and that profiles "generated by you, or on your behalf" must be "identified as such", with customers told "in a timely and clear manner". A clause in the terms making customers accept such profiles is not enough. [first-party, 8, read; fetch summary; guidance, not a ruling] [unverified: a second read of the page found no sentence on terms clauses; it says not to require customers to accept such profiles unless told "in a timely and clear manner" and the profiles are "identified as such"]
5. EU: Annex I point 22 bans in all cases "falsely representing oneself as a consumer". A trader's profile posing as a member fits this. [derived from 11; no dating ruling under point 22 was found]

## 2. Fakes made by outsiders: a different legal question

6. The FTC's 2019 Match complaint was about scammers' accounts, not Match's own. It said hundreds of thousands subscribed after messages from accounts Match had flagged as likely fake. Match said it blocks "96% of fake accounts and bots within a day". [press, 6, read; fetch summary; allegations]
7. In March 2022 the court dismissed those counts under Section 230: the deceptive content came from third-party users, and Match's emails were "content-neutral tools". The 2025 $14 million settlement covers guarantees, billing and cancellation only. [legal, 4–5, 7, read; fetch summary; one district court]
8. So the line is who wrote the profile. Section 230 did not help JDI or Ashley Madison, because they made the profiles themselves. [derived from 1, 2, 7]

## 3. Real people's profiles or photos reused without consent

9. Korea's privacy regulator fined Techlabs (Amanda and two other apps) about 220 million won in 2024. It allegedly created 276 fake accounts with members' photos, matched them with real users for about a year, and had staff take part. [unverified: a second press report (datingnews.com, June 2025) confirms the PIPC fine of about 220 million won and the police referral but says "over 270" profiles, photos taken from a sister app in Taiwan, and October 2021 to April 2022; the PIPC decision was not read] It was also referred to police. [legal, 12, read; fetch summary; press report; one regulator]
10. The CMA advice asks for a list of sites where a customer's data may appear before sharing it with other dating sites, even ones the provider controls. [first-party, 8, read; fetch summary]

## 4. Disclosed house profiles and AI

11. No source condemns a profile that is clearly labelled where users see it. The JDI order and the CMA advice both allow provider profiles if disclosed. LG Berlin set where the disclosure must be: not only in the terms. [derived from 1, 8, 10]
12. From 2 August 2026, AI systems that talk with people must tell them they are AI, unless obvious (AI Act Art. 50(1); dates in `privacy-and-marketing-law.md` item 28). An AI "member" chatting unlabelled breaks this as well as consumer law. [legal, 14, read; fetch summary; no enforcement yet seen]
13. The 2024 FTC review rule does not reach dating profiles directly. Its "fake indicators of social media influence" covers followers, likes and views made by bots or by accounts "not associated with a real individual". [legal, 13, read; fetch summary] A bot-inflated "members near you" count is not clearly covered. [derived]
14. Apple's guidelines name dating only under 4.3(b) (saturated categories). No rule on fake or bot profiles was seen in the part read. [first-party, 15, read; fetch summary; last 21% of the page unread]

## What this changes

1. `knowledge/marketplaces.md`, line "act as the supplier yourself…". Add: "In dating and social apps the supply is people. Profiles or chats run by staff, bots or AI must be labelled where users see them, not only in the terms. Unlabelled ones were treated as deception and settled with refunds (FTC 2014, 2016) or banned by German courts (2022) [legal; two FTC settlements, two German regional courts, UK guidance; not legal advice]."
