# App store search and product page conversion: App Store and Google Play (checked 2026-10-05)

Scope: how people find an app through search inside the Apple App Store and Google Play, and how the store page turns a visit into an install. Covers which metadata each store indexes, the 2025–2026 changes (Apple's generated tags, keywords on custom product pages, more ad slots in App Store search, Gemini-made custom store listings on Play), product page tests, ratings rules, academic evidence on rank and demand, paid search inside the stores, and the share of installs that comes from search. It extends `research/local-and-consumer-apps.md` (items 16–17), which already holds the basic character limits, screenshot counts and rating-prompt rules.

Access caveat: `[read 2026-10-05]` = the page or abstract was fetched and read on that date. `[read 2026-10-05; abstract]` = only the abstract was read. `[snippet-only]` = only a search-engine summary was seen; re-check before quoting numbers. ScienceDirect, SSRN, Wiley, SAGE, Springer and the Appfigures site returned 403 to the fetcher; for those papers the abstract was read through RePEc, AISeL, AEA or Crossref instead. OpenAlex hit its daily limit partway through, so the paper search is not exhaustive.

## Sources

### A. How each store's search works (first-party)

1. **Apple, "App Store search"**. https://developer.apple.com/app-store/search/ [first-party][read 2026-10-05]. "Search results are based on a number of factors, including text relevance (matches for your app's title, subtitle, keywords, and primary category), as well as user behavior (downloads, ratings and reviews, and more)." Ratings and reviews "can influence how your app ranks in App Store search." Up to three screenshots or previews may show in results. Results "may also include app tags … generated using large language models (LLMs) based on the metadata you've provided in App Store Connect." The page does not mention screenshot text, developer name or In-App Purchase names as ranking inputs.
2. **Apple, "Creating your product page"**. https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/ [first-party][read 2026-10-05]. Name 30 characters, subtitle 30, keywords 100 (comma-separated, no spaces needed; don't repeat plurals, category names, the word "app", or competitor names). Promotional text 170 characters: "Promotional text doesn't affect your app's search ranking so it should not be used to display keywords." Description: "Don't add unnecessary keywords to your description in an attempt to improve search results" (the description is not listed as a ranking input in source 1). Up to 10 screenshots; "The first one to three images will appear in search results when no app preview is available." Up to 3 previews, max 30 seconds, autoplay muted.
3. **Apple, App Review Guidelines 2.3.7, 3.2.2(x), 5.6.1, 5.6.3, 5.6.4**. https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/ [first-party][read 2026-10-05]. 2.3.7: don't pack metadata "with trademarked terms, popular app names, pricing information, or other irrelevant phrases just to game the system"; "Apple may modify inappropriate keywords at any time." 3.2.2(x): apps must not force users to rate or review to get functionality. 5.6.1: use the provided API; "we will disallow custom review prompts." 5.6.3 Discovery Fraud: "Manipulating any element of the App Store customer experience such as charts, search, reviews, or referrals to your app … is not permitted." 5.6.4: "negative customer reviews, and excessive refund requests" are indications of low quality that may matter to Developer Code of Conduct decisions.
4. **Apple, "Promoting your In-App Purchases"**. https://developer.apple.com/app-store/promoting-in-app-purchases/ [first-party][read 2026-10-05]. Promoted In-App Purchases "can display in search results"; "consumable Apple In-App Purchases will not appear in search results." Up to 20 promoted at a time; display name 30 characters, description 45. No statement that IAP names help the *app* rank.
5. **Apple, "App Store localizations" (App Store Connect Help)**. https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-store-localizations/ [first-party][read 2026-10-05]. 54 localizations. Table of default and "additional" languages per storefront, e.g. United States: English (US) plus Arabic, Chinese (Simplified), Chinese (Traditional), French, Korean, Portuguese (Brazil), Russian, Spanish (Mexico), Vietnamese; Canada: English (Canada) + French (Canada); UK: English (U.K.) only; Mexico: Spanish (Mexico) + English (U.K.); Germany: German + English (U.K.); Japan: Japanese + English (US); Switzerland: German + English (U.K.), French, Italian; India: English (U.K.) + 11 Indian languages. The page says the language shown depends on store location, device language and the languages you added. **It does not say these extra locales are indexed for search** in that storefront; that claim comes from ASO vendors (see Folklore).
6. **Apple, WWDC25 "What's new in App Store Connect" (session 328)**. https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/328/ [first-party][read 2026-10-05; transcript]. Tags are "generated by our large language models using a range of data sources, including your app's metadata", "all human reviewed"; developers can deselect tags, which removes the app from that tag's page. Tags appear "along-side app categories on the search page and with your app in search results." Keywords for custom product pages: "Whenever your app shows up in search results for the keywords you've assigned, your custom product page shows in place of your default product page"; assigning keywords alone needs no review; search traffic per custom page is in App Analytics. Apple does not say tags affect ranking.
7. **Apple Machine Learning Research / arXiv 2602.23234: Christakopoulou, Patel, Velaga, Gaikwad, Suchter, Sundaranatha, "Scaling Search Relevance: Augmenting App Store Ranking with LLM-Generated Judgments"** (26 Feb 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23234 ; https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/augmenting-app [first-party research by Apple staff][read 2026-10-05; abstract]. The App Store ranker optimises two objectives: "behavioral relevance (results users tend to click or download) and textual relevance (a result's semantic fit to the query)." Expert text-relevance labels are scarce, so Apple generated millions with a fine-tuned LLM. A worldwide A/B test of the ranker gave "a statistically significant +0.24% increase in conversion rate, with the most substantial performance gains occurring in tail queries" (rare queries where click and download data is thin). This is the clearest primary evidence that App Store search mixes text matching with click and download behaviour, and that semantic matching (meaning, not just exact keywords) now matters for rare queries.
8. **Google Play Console Help, "Get discovered on Google Play search"** (answer 4448378). https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/4448378?hl=en [first-party][read 2026-10-05]. Play search ranks apps on "a combination of ratings, reviews, downloads, and other factors"; weights are "a proprietary part of the Google search algorithm." Advice: keep the title focused, use SEO best practice in descriptions within the policy against "keyword spamming", avoid subtle misspellings, update regularly, encourage ratings, and use professional translation for localized descriptions.
9. **Google Play Console Help, "App discovery and ranking"** (answer 9958766). https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9958766?hl=en [first-party][read 2026-10-05]. Search results are "heavily influenced by relevance to the user's query"; metadata such as "title, description, category" decides which apps address the query. "Apps that have strong technical performance and a good user experience are generally favored over lower quality apps." Quality includes the pre-install experience: "the app's icon, title, screenshots, videos, description and links." Ads are always labelled "ads" or "sponsored". Google names "description" without separating short and full description.
10. **Google Play Console Help, "Best practices for your store listing"** (13393723) and **"Metadata" policy** (9898842). https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13393723?hl=en ; https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898842?hl=en [first-party][read 2026-10-05]. Title ≤30 characters, short description ≤80, full description ≤4,000. No emojis or repeated special characters in the title, no CAPS unless brand, no "Free"/"No Ads"/price promotions, no ranking claims ("#1", "App of the year", "Best of Play"), no unattributed testimonials, no "keywords that manipulate search rankings"; avoid "repetition of words" (example given: "car racing, car driving, race cars…").
11. **Google Play Console Help, "Choose a category and tags"** (9859673). https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859673?hl=en [first-party][read 2026-10-05]. Up to five tags, chosen by the developer; "Categories and tags help users to search for and discover the most relevant apps." (Contrast: Apple's tags are generated, the developer can only remove them, source 6.)
12. **Android Developers, "Android vitals"**. https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/vitals [first-party][read 2026-10-05]. Bad-behaviour thresholds: user-perceived crash rate 1.09% overall (8% per phone model), user-perceived ANR ("app not responding") rate 0.47% overall (8% per phone model). "If your app or game exceeds a bad behavior threshold, Play may reduce the visibility of your title. Play may also show users a warning on your store listing." Assessed on the last 28 days. New thresholds for memory use and code optimisation "may see store visibility impact starting from February 2027."
13. **Android Developers Blog, "Improved app quality and discovery on Google Play"** (Kosuke Suzuki, 4 Jun 2019). https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2019/06/improved-app-quality-and-discovery-on.html [first-party][read 2026-10-05]. "App quality will impact where and how prominently you're eligible to surface in the store." 42% of users who leave a 1-star review mention stability or bugs. Rating recalculation weighted to recent ratings from August 2019; users who get a developer reply raise their rating "+0.7 stars on average."

### B. Product page conversion and testing (first-party)

14. **Apple, "Product page optimization"**. https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page-optimization/ [first-party][read 2026-10-05]. Up to 3 treatments vs the original; test icon, screenshots, previews (not text). Max 90 days. You set the traffic share (e.g. 40% split over two treatments = 20% each). Only iOS/iPadOS 15+; a user keeps the same treatment. Alternate icons must be in the app binary. Results report conversion rate, improvement and confidence, with 90% as the threshold. Alternate assets "may appear in search results", Today, Games and Apps tabs.
15. **Apple, "Custom product pages"**. https://developer.apple.com/app-store/custom-product-pages/ [first-party][read 2026-10-05]. Up to **70** custom pages per app (Phiture, source 37, says up from 35). Vary screenshots, previews, promotional text. Keywords can be assigned so a custom page shows in organic search for those keywords; each keyword set must be unique to one page. Deep links to a place in the app (iOS 18+). Usable in Apple Ads. Apple's claim: "Developers see a 2.5 percentage point increase on average when referring people to a custom product page. This is a 156% increase compared to the 1.6% average conversion rate on default product pages." No sample, period or definition of "referring" is given; treat as an Apple marketing figure, and note the 1.6% base cannot be compared with vendor conversion rates (source 33) that use other definitions.
16. **Apple, "What's new" (App Store, Developer)**. https://developer.apple.com/app-store/whats-new/ [first-party][read 2026-10-05]. Current items: review summaries now in the US plus 8 more storefronts; "coming this fall" (2026) new image and video creative assets that can show in product page headers, **search results** (App Store and Apple Games app), custom product pages, PPO and Apple Ads; an asset library with pre-approval; product page preview; personalised collections in Apps, Games and Search tabs in some countries.
17. **Apple, "Ratings, reviews, and responses"**. https://developer.apple.com/app-store/ratings-and-reviews/ [first-party][read 2026-10-05]. Summary rating is per territory. You can reset it with a new version, but Apple says "use this feature sparingly … having few ratings may discourage potential users," and a reset does not remove written reviews. Prompt up to 3 times in 365 days. You can reply to any review; reviewers are notified and can update. LLM review summaries are in the US plus Australia, Canada, India, Ireland, New Zealand, Singapore, South Africa, UK.
18. **Apple Machine Learning Research, "An LLM-Based Approach to Review Summarization on the App Store"**. https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/app-store-review [first-party][snippet-only]. Spam, profanity and fraud filtered out; summaries 100–300 characters; shown on iOS 18.4+.
19. **App Store Connect Analytics Help, "Acquisition"**. https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect-analytics/acquisition/acquisition/ [first-party][read 2026-10-05]. Source types: App Store search ("Includes views and downloads from ads that appear in App Store search results"), App Store browse (Today, Games, Apps), App referrer, Web referrer, Institutional purchase, Unavailable. So "search" downloads in your own analytics include Apple Ads search-result downloads.
20. **Apple Tech Talk 110365, "Measure and improve acquisition with App Analytics"**. https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/110365/ [first-party][read 2026-10-05; transcript]. Conversion rate = total downloads ÷ unique impressions; an impression is any time the icon is seen, "or in search results." Peer group benchmarks group apps by business model, category and download volume. Advice: wait for at least 90% confidence before acting on a PPO test. (So Apple's conversion rate counts search-result impressions, and differs from Play's store-listing conversion.)
21. **App Store Connect Analytics Help, "Peer group benchmarks"**. https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect-analytics/benchmarks/peer-group-benchmarks/ [first-party][snippet-only]. Benchmarks for conversion rate and, since 2025, download-to-paid conversion and proceeds per download. The benchmark values are visible only in your own account.
22. **Play Console Help, "Run A/B tests on your store listing"** (6227309). https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6227309?hl=en [first-party][read 2026-10-05]. Test icon, feature graphic, screenshots, video and descriptions; "you can choose up to 2 variants"; metrics: unique user install clicks (users who didn't have it on any other device), open clicks, pre-registration clicks; you set confidence level and minimum detectable effect; one default-graphics experiment or up to five localized experiments at a time; experiments stop after 6 months. Unlike Apple's PPO, Play tests text as well as graphics.
23. **Play Console Help, "Create custom store listings to target specific user segments"** (9867158). https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9867158?hl=en [first-party][read 2026-10-05]. Up to 50 custom store listings; target by country, pre-registration, **search keywords** ("choose from the available keywords known to bring you traffic" or enter new ones), URL, inactive (churned/lapsed) users, Google Ads campaigns. You can change name, icon, descriptions and graphics. No automatic translations.
24. **Android Developers Blog, "Updates to power your growth on Google Play"** (Oct 2024). https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2024/10/whats-new-in-google-play.html [first-party][read 2026-10-05]. Full-screen portrait store videos: "+7% increase in total watch time, a +9% increase in video completion count, and a +5% increase in conversions" (early results; no sample or period given).
25. **Android Developers Blog, "I/O 2025: What's new in Google Play"**. https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2025/05/io-2025-whats-new-in-google-play.html [first-party][read 2026-10-05]. Store listing asset library; hero content and YouTube playlist carousels for all apps from July 2025; Collections (Engage SDK) rolling out globally; topic browse pages.
26. **Android Developers, "I/O 2026: What's new in Google Play"** (May 2026). https://developer.android.com/blog/posts/i-o-2026-what-s-new-in-google-play [first-party][read 2026-10-05]. ### D. Research: rank, ratings and demand

30. **Garg, R. & Telang, R. (2013). "Inferring App Demand from Publicly Available Data." MIS Quarterly 37(4):1253–1264.** https://aisel.aisnet.org/misq/vol37/iss4/14/ [research][read 2026-10-05; abstract]. Paid iPhone apps: rank 1 gets "150 times more downloads" than rank 200; iPad: 120 times. Paid apps only, top charts (not search rank), data from about 2010.
31. **Carare, O. (2012). "The Impact of Bestseller Rank on Demand: Evidence from the App Market." International Economic Review 53(3):717–742.** https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/iecrev/v53y2012i3p717-742.html [research][read 2026-10-05; abstract]. Daily top-100 paid app ranks. Being top-ranked raises willingness to pay by "about $4.50" versus the same app unranked; the effect falls steeply with rank but stays "economically significant for the apps in the first half of the top 100." Evidence that chart rank itself causes more demand, not only reflects it.
32. **Engström, P. & Forsell, E. (2018). "Demand effects of consumers' stated and revealed preferences." Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization 150:43–61.** https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jeborg/v150y2018icp43-61.html [research][read 2026-10-05; abstract]. 500,000+ apps, US Google Play, 42 days; uses Google's rounded display of ratings and download brackets. "A 10 percentile increase in displayed average rating only increases downloads by about 3%, while a 10 percentile increase in displayed number of downloads increases downloads by about 25%." (The 2013 Uppsala working paper said "about 20 percent"; quote the published 25%.)
33. **Kübler, R., Pauwels, K., Yildirim, G. & Fandrich, T. (2018). "App Popularity: Where in the World are Consumers Most Sensitive to Price and User Ratings?" Journal of Marketing.** https://doi.org/10.1509/jm.16.0140 [research][read 2026-10-05; abstract via Crossref]. 60 countries, dynamic panel. Sensitivity to rating valence (average stars) is higher in individualist, uncertainty-avoiding countries; sensitivity to rating volume is higher in high power-distance, uncertainty-avoiding, richer and more equal countries. The authors compute how much price must fall to offset a negative review or no reviews.
34. **Leyden, B. T. "Platform Design and Innovation Incentives: Evidence from the Product Ratings System on Apple's App Store."** SSRN 3863816 (2021); International Journal of Industrial Organization (2024), https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167718724000882 [research][read 2026-10-05; abstract via Crossref, SSRN version]. Before iOS 11 (Sept 2017) every update reset the visible rating; for "nearly a decade" this discouraged updates, most for developers who relied on ratings. Shows rating display rules change developer behaviour.
35. **Ershov, D. (2024). "Variety-Based Congestion in Online Markets: Evidence from Mobile Apps." AEJ: Microeconomics 16(2):180–203.** https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Fmic.20200347 [research][read 2026-10-05; abstract]. Google Play's March 2014 split of 6 game categories into 18 is a natural experiment: "more apps in the market directly reduce per app usage/downloads"; 40% of the consumer gains from more variety are lost to congestion. How the store organises browsing changes who gets found.
36. **Lee, G. & Raghu, T. S. (2014). "Determinants of Mobile Apps' Success: Evidence from the App Store Market." JMIS 31(2):133–169.** https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2560677 [research][snippet-only]. Survival in Apple's top-300 grossing chart is helped by free versions, high initial rank, less competitive categories, continuous quality updates, and high volume and score of reviews.
37. **Wang, M. & Li, X. (2017). "Effects of the aesthetic design of icons on app downloads: evidence from an android market." Electronic Commerce Research 17(1):83–102.** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10660-016-9245-4 [research][snippet-only]. On a large Chinese Android store, more colourful icons, "proper" complexity and slight asymmetry go with more downloads. Observational, one store.

### E. Paid search inside the stores and the halo question

38. **Apple Ads, "Ads on the App Store"**. https://ads.apple.com/app-store [first-party][read 2026-10-05]. "70% of App Store visitors use search to discover apps" and "Almost 65% of downloads happen directly after a search" (footnote: App Store, worldwide, **2022**). Over 60% average tap-to-install rate for ads at the top of search results (all Apple Ads regions, Nov 2024–Oct 2025). 95% of ad-tap conversions within 1 minute (Aug 2024). Over 850 million weekly App Store visitors (2026).
39. **Apple Ads, "Ad placements – best practices"**. https://ads.apple.com/app-store/best-practices/ad-placements [first-party][read 2026-10-05]. Four placements: Today tab, Search tab, search results, product pages. "62% of surveyed users were more likely to check out apps that were highlighted across the App Store" (Apple-commissioned GWI survey, Jan–Feb 2022, 21k respondents, 38 countries). Search tab analysis: 4,439 advertisers, Jan–Oct 2023.
40. **Apple Ads Help, "Search results"** (0082). https://ads.apple.com/app-store/help/ad-placements/0082-search-results [first-party][read 2026-10-05]. Ads "can appear either at the top of their search results or further down the page"; advertisers cannot choose the position. Apple "considers both relevance and bids" and "If your app isn't relevant to what the user is searching for, it won't be displayed — regardless of how much you may be willing to pay." Maximize Conversions bidding is for post-launch campaigns. Nothing on this page says ads change organic rank.
41. **Apple Ads, "News"**. https://ads.apple.com/news [first-party][read 2026-10-05]. Rename "Apple Search Ads" → "Apple Ads" dated **14 April 2025** on this page (corrects the "4 April" date in `local-and-consumer-apps.md` 16d). Additional search-result ad positions announced 17 Dec 2025 (iOS/iPadOS 26.2+, live in 2026, per 9to5Mac report https://9to5mac.com/2025/12/17/apple-announces-more-ads-are-coming-to-app-store-search-results/ [read 2026-10-05]). Maximize Conversions (Search Match + auto-bidder) 25 Feb 2026. Custom creative for Today tab and search results ads announced 8 June 2026, "later in 2026".
42. **Google Ads Help, "About App campaigns"** (6247380). https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6247380?hl=en [first-party][read 2026-10-05]. One campaign serves across Google Search, Google Play (search results, related apps, home), YouTube, Display Network and Discover; Google builds ads from your text, images, video and HTML5 assets; the advertiser sets languages, locations, bid and budget, not keywords or placements. There is no Play-search-only campaign comparable to Apple Ads search results.
43. **Ju, H., Zhao, M. & Aral, S. "Advertising Spillovers in Mobile Apps: Evidence from Ad Shutoffs and Store Rankings."** arXiv 2504.16151 (v1 22 Apr 2025, revised 10 Jul 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16151 ; https://arxiv.org/html/2504.16151 [research, working paper][read 2026-10-05]. One large US mobile game developer, 6 games, iOS and Android, 500 days; a global shutoff of all paid ads across 85 publishers (search, social, display, in-app) for about 24 days. Organic installs "dropped by 20–30%, depending on bandwidth" (11-day window: −0.319 log points, p=.005). Ad spend moves **category top-chart (Free chart) rank**, and once rank is controlled the spend–organic link disappears, so the spillover runs through chart rank. "Every $100 of ad spend was positively associated with 32.3 paid and 2.2 organic installs" (organic coefficient p≈0.51, not significant). Limits: one developer, games only, ranks from Appfigures, top charts not search rank, all channels off together (not Apple Ads alone).
44. **SplitMetrics, "Apple Ads Search Results Benchmarks Report 2025"**. https://splitmetrics.com/apple-ads-search-results-benchmarks-2025/ [vendor][read 2026-10-05; landing page only]. 3.1M keywords, 4.44B impressions, 395.4M taps, 253.1M downloads, 15 categories, 61 markets, 2024 data: "67.2% average conversion rate in 2024 across top categories, up from 65.37% in 2023." The 2026 edition (2025 data) is reported in search summaries as TTR 9.7%, CR 66.2%, average CPT $2.25 [snippet-only]. SplitMetrics sells Apple Ads management.
45. **SplitMetrics case study, "How Apple Ads helps to increase organic traffic" (Deep Town, Rockbite Games)**. https://splitmetrics.com/customers/how-apple-search-ads-helps-to-increase-organic-traffic-with-splitmetrics/ [vendor][read 2026-10-05]. US, Jan–Jun 2022, generic and competitor keywords: keywords in the top 10 went from 26 to 112; organic installs 1.88K in a 30-day window vs 1.24K the month before. A before/after comparison with no control group; the vendor ran the campaign.
46. **"Halo effect" claims: Mobile Action ("20–30% boost in organic installs"), AppsFlyer ("40–70% incrementality")**, as repeated by AppSamurai https://appsamurai.com/blog/apple-search-ads/ and agency blogs [vendor][snippet-only]. No primary report with sample and method was found for either figure.

### F. Where installs come from, and practitioner evidence

47. **Android Developers Blog, "Exclusive new organic acquisition insights on the Google Play Console"** (Aug 2018). https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2018/08/new-organic-acquisition-insights-now.html [first-party][read 2026-10-05]. Play Console splits organic traffic into "Google Play search" and "Google Play explore" (home page, category lists, related apps). No market-wide share is given. No first-party Google figure for the share of Play installs from search was found.
48. **Google/Ipsos, "Mobile App Marketing Insights: How Consumers Really Find and Use Your Apps"** (US, May 2015). https://think.storage.googleapis.com/docs/mobile-app-marketing-insights.pdf [first-party survey][snippet-only]. 40% of smartphone users discover apps by browsing the app store; 27% through search engines. Self-reported and 11 years old.
49. **AppTweak, "Average App Conversion Rate per Category [2025]"** (published 18 May 2026). https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/average-app-conversion-rate-per-category [vendor][read 2026-10-05]. US, 2025: average conversion rate App Store 8.56%, Google Play 16.15%; App Store "install rate" (download straight from search/browse results without opening the page) 3.8%. Sample size and app selection not disclosed. The two stores define conversion differently (Apple: per unique impression incl. search results, source 20; Play: per store-listing visitor), so the gap is not a like-for-like comparison.

Claims Apple began extracting text from screenshot captions in June 2025 and using it for ranking; later described as AI extraction rather than character-by-character OCR.

Repeats the screenshot-indexing claim but notes Apple denied OCR use; custom product pages appear in organic search after the July 2025 keyword update; limit 70 (from 35); tags "can potentially influence browse placement" (no data).

[vendor-aggregate][snippet-only]. No original report found; the 65% matches Apple's own 2022 "downloads directly after a search" figure (source 38), so the split looks derived, not measured.

## What this means for a small app team

1. **On the App Store, rank comes from text match plus behaviour.** Only name, subtitle, keyword field and primary category are named text inputs; downloads, ratings and reviews are named behaviour inputs. Promotional text does not count and Apple tells you not to stuff the description. [first-party: 1, 2] Apple's own 2026 paper confirms the ranker blends "behavioral relevance" (clicks, downloads) with "textual relevance", and that semantic matching now carries rare queries where behaviour data is thin. [first-party research: 7] For a new app with little download history, that means: write a name and subtitle that state plainly what the app does in the words users search, because for niche queries text relevance does more of the work.
2. **On Google Play there is no keyword field; title and description carry the text match, and quality and ratings count openly.** Google names "ratings, reviews, downloads" and technical quality, and says it may reduce visibility for apps above a 1.09% crash rate or 0.47% ANR rate. [first-party: 8, 9, 12] Fixing crashes is a ranking task on Play, not just an engineering one.
3. **Since mid-2025 the store page can change per search keyword on both stores.** Apple: up to 70 custom product pages, keywords assigned to one page show it in organic search; Google: up to 50 custom store listings targeted by search keyword, and since I/O 2026 Gemini drafts one from a keyword recommendation. [first-party: 6, 15, 23, 26] For a small team, 2–3 pages that match the main intents (e.g. "budget planner" vs "split bills") is a practical start; Apple's "+2.5 percentage points" uplift has no published method. [first-party claim: 15]
4. **Test what you can, but treat a 90% result as a lead.** Apple PPO tests icon, screenshots, previews (up to 3 treatments, 90 days, iOS 15+). Play tests graphics and text (up to 2 variants). Both report at 90% confidence by default, so roughly 1 in 10 "wins" is noise; re-test big changes. [first-party: 14, 20, 22; practitioner: 54] Low-traffic apps may never reach significance; Apple notes that testing fewer localizations speeds results. [first-party: 14]
5. **Chart rank causes demand, not only reflects it.** Top-ranked paid apps sell far more (rank 1 ≈150× rank 200) and being ranked raises willingness to pay. [research: 30, 31] Data is from paid-app charts around 2010; search rank effects are not measured in these papers.
6. **Displayed popularity moves installs more than displayed stars.** On Google Play a 10-percentile rise in displayed downloads raised downloads ~25%; the same rise in displayed rating ~3%. [research: 32] How much stars matter also varies by country. [research: 33] Ratings still count for Play placement and Apple ranking [first-party: 1, 28], so prompt for ratings after a success moment, but don't expect stars alone to drive growth.
7. **Don't reset the App Store rating casually.** It is per territory, the reset removes the count that signals popularity, and written reviews stay. Play has no reset; it weights recent ratings and shows country- and device-specific ratings. [first-party: 17, 27, 28] Reply to bad reviews: Google reports +0.7 stars on average after a reply (2019). [first-party: 13]
8. **Apple Ads gives you a spot at the top of search, but Apple does not claim it lifts organic rank.** Relevance gates the auction, and since 2026 there are more ad slots lower in results, which also means more paid listings above organic ones. [first-party: 40, 41] Evidence for a "halo": one 2025 working paper found shutting off *all* ad channels for one game company cut organic installs 20–30%, through category chart rank. [research: 43] Apple-Ads-specific halo figures are vendor case studies without controls. [vendor: 45, 46] Treat a halo as plausible for chart-sensitive apps, unproven for Apple Ads search alone, and test with a holdout before counting on it.
9. **Search is the main door on iOS, by Apple's own figure.** "Almost 65% of downloads happen directly after a search" and 70% of visitors use search, App Store worldwide, 2022. [first-party: 38] Your own App Store Connect "App Store search" source includes Apple Ads downloads, so separate paid from organic before judging ASO. [first-party: 19] There is no comparable first-party figure for Google Play.

## Folklore and weak claims

- **"Apple indexes screenshot captions for search since June 2025."** Started from Appfigures' June 2025 analysis [vendor: 50]; a 64-phrase test found no strong evidence [practitioner: 51]; Phiture reports Apple denied OCR use [practitioner: 52]; Apple's search page lists no screenshot text [first-party: 1]. Press reports (TechCrunch, June 2025, not read) say tags draw on screenshots among other inputs; tags are not the same as keyword ranking. Write captions for the human reader; don't move keywords there.
- **"The US App Store also indexes your Spanish (Mexico) keywords" (and similar per-country "extra locale" tables).** Apple's page lists which languages each storefront *supports* [first-party: 5] but says nothing about indexing. The indexing claim is vendor testing; widely used, not confirmed by Apple.
- **"Apple Ads boosts organic rank 20–30%" / "4× ASO".** No primary data with method; the only case study read is a before/after with no control [vendor: 45, 46]. The one research paper measured all-channel ads, games only, through top charts [research: 43].
- **"Preview videos lift conversion 20–40%" / "screenshots drive 80% of install decisions".** Repeated in many ASO blogs; no traceable source found. The only first-party video figure is Google's +5% conversion for full-screen portrait video on Play, early results, no sample given [first-party: 24].
- **"65% search / 18% browse / 12% web / 5% ads" iOS install split.** Appears to be built from Apple's 2022 "65% after a search" figure, not measured [vendor-aggregate: 53].
- **Apple's "1.6% average conversion rate on default product pages."** No definition, sample or date [first-party: 15]; vendor averages (8.56% App Store, 16.15% Play) use other definitions [vendor: 49]. None of these numbers can be compared.
- **"Download velocity" and "retention" as named App Store rank factors.** Apple names "downloads" and "user behavior", not velocity or retention; Google names downloads, ratings, quality and technical performance. Retention as a direct ranking input is plausible but stated by neither store.
- **The "20%" vs "25%" download effect in Engström & Forsell.** The working paper said ~20%; the published JEBO paper says ~25% [research: 32].

## Open questions

- Does Apple index the description, developer name or In-App Purchase names for app ranking? Apple's search page names only title, subtitle, keywords and category; vendor tests disagree. Not resolved from primary sources.
- Do Apple's generated tags affect ranking or only browse pages? Apple describes display and tag pages only.
- No first-party Google figure for the share of Play installs from search vs explore; Play Console shows it per app only.
- No controlled study of Apple Ads (search only) on organic search rank. A geo or time holdout on your own app is the only clean answer.
- No peer-reviewed study of custom product pages, Play custom store listings by keyword, or PPO results; all uplift figures are platform claims.
- Effect of the 2026 extra search-result ad slots on organic click-through has not been measured publicly.
- How AI answers (Ask Play, Gemini app discovery, chat assistants) change the share of installs that start in store search; Google says Ask Play exists, gives no traffic data [first-party: 26].
- Papers not read in full: Lee & Raghu 2014 and Wang & Li 2017 (snippet-only); Leyden read via SSRN abstract only.
