# How does a designer in Argentina get paid through freelance platforms, and do Latin American freelancers win foreign clients on time zone or price? (checked 2026-10-28)

Question: a freelance designer based in Argentina, with zero clients, wants buyers in the US, Spain and other Latin American countries. Which freelance platforms accept and pay someone in Argentina, and by which rails? Is there evidence that time-zone overlap ("nearshore": selling to a nearby country in similar hours) or a lower price wins foreign clients? Does any Argentine rule block a marketing step?

## 1. Which platforms pay out in Argentina, and how

- Upwork pays to a local bank in Argentina in pesos (ARS). The fee is $0.99 per withdrawal; the Argentina limit is $3,000 per withdrawal. The page warns that AFIP (now ARCA) may withhold income tax by tax category, and ARBA (Buenos Aires province) 0.20%–3.5% of gross income. [first-party, 1, 2, fetch summary; undated]
- Upwork also pays through Payoneer for freelancers outside India and the US, for "a small per-transfer fee", amount not stated. Payoneer adds its own conversion and transfer fees. [first-party, 3, fetch summary]
- Fiverr: PayPal ($0 fee, $1 minimum), bank transfer via Payoneer ($1 fee, $20 minimum), Payoneer account ($3 fee, $10 minimum). Each method "may have location restrictions"; the page names no countries. [first-party, 4, fetch summary; undated]
- Contra: local bank transfer, PayPal, Payoneer and USDC crypto ("most countries"); debit card is US only. Fees and the country list sit on linked pages not read. [first-party, 6, 7, fetch summary]
- Malt accepts freelancers registered abroad only from "open countries", and asks for proof of business registration dated within three months, plus a passport for non-Europeans. [unverified: the step page that would hold the document list (2511434) failed at the site on 2026-10-28; the overview step confirms only "open" and "blocked" countries] Whether Argentina is open was not seen. [first-party, 5, fetch summary; country list missing]
- Toptal pays by bank transfer to a USD account; if the account is in another currency, Toptal converts "for which they will charge a fee", amount unstated. [vendor, 8, fetch summary; not Toptal's page]
- Workana: no first-party page found.
- So no platform checked refuses Argentina outright. Upwork is the only one whose page names Argentina. Upwork's local route pays pesos, so a designer who wants dollars needs Payoneer or another USD rail. [derived from 1–8]

## 2. Do time zone or price win foreign clients?

- Time zone: on one IT platform, 11,541 software projects, buyers were less likely to pick providers in other time zones. Good ratings softened language and culture gaps, but not time-zone gaps. [research, 11, abstract-only; observational with an instrument; one platform; software, not design] The press office gives no effect size. [secondary, 12]
- Argentina is 1–2 hours from US Eastern time and 4–5 hours from Spain. [derived; standard time zones, US daylight saving varies] So the time-zone finding favours US buyers over Spanish ones for this designer, if it carries to design.
- Country of origin: on oDesk (now Upwork), employers in rich countries were less likely to hire workers from less developed countries, with controls. [corrected 2026-10-28: the abstract says contractors from less developed countries "are disadvantaged relative to those from developed countries"; it does not say the gap comes from employers in rich countries] Verified work history narrowed the gap more for those workers, also in wage bids, interviews and shortlists. [research, 9, abstract-only; observational; one platform; c. 2009–10 data per the 2013 WP] This matches the first-rating finding in `freelance-platforms-first-clients.md` §2.
- Price: no source tested whether a lower price wins work. Ghani, Kerr & Stanton found ethnic-Indian employers 9 percentage points (32%) more likely to hire in India, on 168,060 contracts, 2005–Aug 2010, and paying about 14% less. [corrected 2026-10-28: no 14% figure in the NBER PDF; descriptively, US ethnic-Indian employers hiring in India paid about $7.80 an hour against $8.40 for non-Indians (about 7%); the regression estimate is about 2.4% lower ($9.76 on a $10 contract) and not statistically different from zero] The paper reads the gain as lower cost for the employer. [research, 10, fetch summary; observational; names used to infer ethnicity; India only] It says nothing about Latin America.
- Rates: Payoneer's 2020 survey gives a global average of $21 an hour, 7,000 freelancers in 150+ countries, with no regional figures on the page. [vendor, 13; self-reported; method not described] No rates gap for Argentina was found.

## 3. What Argentine rules add

- Since Comunicación "A" 8330 (18 Sep 2025), individuals exporting services may keep their dollars, with no annual cap, if they bring them into Argentina within 20 business days. [secondary, 14, fetch summary; regulation text not read] The 2022 regime with a $12,000 cap is superseded. [first-party, 15] This is about getting paid, not marketing. It blocks no marketing step.
- For marketing rules on Argentine prospects, see `knowledge/marketing-law-by-market.md`, "Argentina" (the No Llame register for phone calls). Selling to US and Spanish businesses, those rules mostly don't apply. [derived]
