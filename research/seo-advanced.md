## A. Local SEO

1. Google Business Profile Help — "Tips to improve your local ranking on Google" (answer 7091). https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091 — Local results are based mainly on relevance (how well a profile matches the search), distance (from the searcher, or Google's estimate of their location) and prominence (how well-known the business is: links, reviews and ratings, and other web information; more reviews and positive ratings can help). The page's summary sentence says "relevance, distance, and popularity" while the section heading reads "Prominence". Distance: if the searcher doesn't share a location, Google uses what it knows about it. Prominence is described as based on "how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have" (no mention of other web information on the current page). [first-party] [read 2026-10-05; corrected: "other web information" is no longer on the page]
2. GBP Help — "Guidelines for representing your business on Google" (answer 3038177). https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177 — Business name must be the real-world name used on storefront, website and stationery; no marketing taglines, service/product info, location info, phone numbers or URLs in the name; adding unnecessary information can lead to suspension. [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]
3. GBP Help — "Manage your business category" (answer 7249669). https://support.google.com/business/answer/7249669 — One primary category that best describes the business; add additional categories for departments or services, but do not add a category for every product or service. The page gives no maximum; the "up to 9 additional" limit comes from practitioner guides (BrightLocal and others). [first-party] [read 2026-10-05; corrected: the 9-category limit is not on Google's page]
4. GBP Help — "Manage your business attributes" (answer 9049526). https://support.google.com/business/answer/9049526 — Attributes (e.g. "Wi-Fi", "Has outdoor seating"); some only available in certain countries or categories. [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]
5. GBP Help — "Manage your service areas for service-area & hybrid businesses" (answer 9157481) and "Manage your business address" (answer 2853879). — A service-area business (SAB) visits or delivers to customers and does not serve them at its address; it should hide (clear) its address; overall service area should not extend beyond about 2 hours' driving time from the base; up to 20 service areas. [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]
6. GBP Help — "Verify your business with a video recording" (answer 14271705); "Verify your business on Google" (answer 7107242). — Video verification: live, unedited, continuous recording, at least 30 seconds, from a mobile device; must show location (street signs, landmarks), proof the business exists (signage, equipment) and proof of management (for storefronts: opening a cash register, kitchen, storage room or point-of-sale system; SAB requirements differ). Works for storefront, SAB and hybrid. [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]
7. GBP Help — "Create & manage posts on your Business Profile" (answer 7342169). — Post types: Update, Offer (title, dates and time required, adds "View offer" button), Event (title + start/end dates and times). Posts older than 6 months are archived unless dated. [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]
8. GBP Help — "Manage your Business Profile photos & videos" (answer 6103862) and photos/videos/posts content policy (answer 7213077). — JPG or PNG, 10 KB–5 MB, recommended 720×720 px, minimum 250×250 px; in focus, well lit, no significant alterations or excessive filters. [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]
9. GBP Help — "Understand your Business Profile performance & insights" (answer 9918094). — Metrics: searches (terms used), views (Search and Maps), calls, website clicks, directions, messages, bookings (via booking provider). Only for verified profiles; only metrics that apply appear. [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]
10. GBP Help — "Business links policies & guidelines" (answer 13769188). — Google crawls profile links (at most daily) to verify they lead to a valid, relevant page; unreachable links may be removed. [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]
11. Google Maps User Generated Content Policy — "Prohibited & restricted content" (answer 7400114, also under GBP Help). — Merchants must not discourage or prohibit negative reviews or selectively solicit positive reviews (i.e. review gating is banned); no incentives (payment, discounts, free goods) for reviews or for removing negative ones; must not pressure customers on premises or request specific content, including naming staff; soliciting genuine reviews without incentives is allowed. Violations of the Fake Engagement policy can lead to profile restrictions. [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]
12. GBP Help — "Business Profile restrictions for policy violations" (answer 14114287). — Restrictions on profiles that violate the Fake Engagement policy, besides removing the reviews: no new reviews for a set period, existing reviews unpublished for a set period, or a warning to consumers that fake reviews were removed; owners are notified by email. [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]
13. US Federal Trade Commission — "Federal Trade Commission Announces Final Rule Banning Fake Reviews and Testimonials", press release, Aug 2024. https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials — and "The Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule: Questions and Answers". 16 CFR Part 465. In effect 2024-10-21. Bans creating/selling fake reviews (including AI-generated, or by people with no real experience) and buying them when the business knew or should have known; bans unfounded legal threats, intimidation or false accusations to suppress negative reviews; bans claiming on-site reviews represent all or most reviews when they were suppressed by rating. Civil penalties up to $53,088 per violation (the 2025 inflation-adjusted maximum, effective 17 Jan 2025, from the FTC's release https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/02/ftc-publishes-inflation-adjusted-civil-penalty-amounts-2025; the Q&A itself gives no dollar figure). [first-party: regulator] [read 2026-10-05; corrected: source of the $53,088 figure]

## B. International SEO

21. Google Search Central — "Managing Multi-Regional and Multilingual Sites". https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites — URL structure table: ccTLD (clear geotargeting, server location irrelevant, easy separation; expensive/limited availability, more infrastructure, strict requirements, one country only); subdomain on gTLD (easy setup, allows different server locations, easy separation; users may not recognise geotargeting from URL); subdirectory on gTLD (easy setup, low maintenance, same host; users may not recognise geotargeting, single server location, harder to separate). Also: avoid automatic redirection based on perceived user language; let users switch. URL parameters for locale "Not recommended". The page also says "IP location analysis is difficult and generally not reliable". [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]
22. Google Search Central — "Localized Versions of your Pages" (hreflang). https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/localized-versions — Three methods: HTML link tags, HTTP headers, XML sitemap. Language code ISO 639-1, optional region ISO 3166-1 Alpha 2; other codes unsupported. Each version must list itself and all others; if A links to B, B must link back (return links) or annotations may be ignored. x-default = fallback when no language/region matches. [first-party] [read 2026-10-05] Self-reference requirement: each variation lists one link per variant "including itself"; the set is identical on every version (same for sitemap entries). [read 2026-10-05] "Region alone not valid" (e.g. hreflang="be") [not re-verified; not stated on the 2026-10-05 page read]. Unsupported example given: es-419.

## C. Content and architecture

30. Google Search Central — "SEO Link Best Practices for Google". https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable — Google can reliably crawl only `<a>` elements with an `href`; anchor text should be descriptive, reasonably concise and relevant; don't cram keywords; surrounding words matter; don't chain links; every page you care about should have a link from at least one other page on your site. [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]
31. Google Search Central — "Crawl Budget Management For Large Sites". https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/large-site-managing-crawl-budget (now also under developers.google.com/crawling/docs/crawl-budget) — Intended for sites with 1M+ unique pages changing about weekly, or 10k+ pages changing daily (plus sites with a large portion of their URLs classified as "Discovered – currently not indexed" [read 2026-10-05]); Google says crawl resources for Search depend on "popularity, overall user value, content uniqueness, and serving capacity". Crawl budget = crawl capacity limit + crawl demand; duplicate/unimportant URLs waste crawl time; robots.txt to block unimportant URLs. [first-party] [read 2026-10-05; corrected: the page no longer says serving capacity and content value are the only levers]
32. Google Search Central Blog — "Crawling December: Faceted navigation", Dec 2024; doc "Managing crawling of faceted navigation URLs" (developers.google.com/crawling/docs/faceted-navigation). — If filtered URLs needn't be indexed: disallow in robots.txt, or use URL fragments (#) for filters (Google generally ignores fragments). If they must be crawled: use standard `&` separators, consistent filter order, return 404 for empty combinations; rel=canonical and rel=nofollow (applied consistently) are weaker options; avoid redirecting empty results to a generic "not found" page. Blog URL: https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/12/crawling-december-faceted-nav [first-party] [read 2026-10-05: doc confirms fragments, `&` separators, consistent order, 404 for empty or nonsensical combinations, canonical/nofollow as less effective]
33. Google Search Central — "Pagination Best Practices for Google" (ecommerce). https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/pagination-and-incremental-page-loading — Google no longer uses rel=next/prev; link pages sequentially with `<a href>`; give each page a unique URL (not a # fragment); don't use page 1 as the canonical for the series, give each page its own canonical; paginated pages may share titles/descriptions. [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]
34. Search Console Help — "What are impressions, position, and clicks?" (answer 7042828) and "Performance report (Search results)" (answer 7576553, 17011364). — Clicks, impressions, CTR (clicks ÷ impressions), average position = topmost position of your link per query, averaged; position recorded only when an impression occurs. Dimensions: queries, pages, countries, devices, search appearance, dates. [first-party] [read 2026-10-05]
35. Search Engine Land — "Google warns against content pruning as CNET deletes thousands of pages", Aug 2023. https://searchengineland.com/google-warns-against-content-pruning-as-cnet-deletes-thousands-of-pages-430509 — Danny Sullivan (Google Search Liaison): deleting content because it is "old" is "not a thing"; removing pages may help a massive site get other content crawled, but doesn't make the whole site "so much better". [first-party via secondary reporting] [verified-search: searchengineland.com, 2026-10-04] (re-check 2026-10-05: searchengineland.com returns 403)

A patent is not evidence Google uses it in ranking. Search Engine Land explainers: "Information gain in SEO: What it is and why it matters" (2023), "Information gain: Here's what this new SEO 'buzzword' really means" (2024). ## Open questions

- Resolved 2026-10-05: answer 7091 now says "relevance, distance, and popularity" in its summary but keeps the "Prominence" heading.
- Is GBP public Q&A fully removed worldwide, and is "Ask Maps" Google's official name? API discontinuation is first-party (item 16); public removal still only in agency blogs.
- Do GBP posts or photo counts affect ranking at all? Google says complete info helps "relevance"; there is no first-party statement about posts as a ranking factor. Practitioner tests are anecdotal.
- How much does NAP consistency still matter? Practitioner surveys rate citations low and declining; no controlled study found.
- Does Google enforce the FTC rule-style ban on review gating proactively, or only on reports? No data on enforcement rates.
- Resolved 2026-10-04: crawl budget thresholds unchanged at developers.google.com/crawling/docs/crawl-budget (1M+ pages weekly; 10k+ pages daily; large share Discovered – not indexed).
- Market-share figures for Naver, Seznam, Yandex and Baidu differ by source by 5–10 points; find StatCounter or national sources directly.
- Machine translation: is lightly post-edited machine translation treated differently from raw output in practice? Google's policy is about value and scale, not the tool.
- Traffic numbers for Zapier/Wise/Tripadvisor are tool estimates; no company-published figures found.
