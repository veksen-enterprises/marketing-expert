## A. Local SEO

1. Google Business Profile Help — "Tips to improve your local ranking on Google" (answer 7091). https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091 — Local results are based mainly on relevance (how well a profile matches the search), distance (from the searcher, or Google's estimate of their location) and prominence (how well-known the business is: links, reviews and ratings, and other web information; more reviews and positive ratings can help). The snippet's summary sentence said "relevance, distance, and popularity" while the section heading still read "Prominence" — wording may have changed. [first-party] [verified-search: support.google.com, 2026-10-04]
2. GBP Help — "Guidelines for representing your business on Google" (answer 3038177). https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177 — Business name must be the real-world name used on storefront, website and stationery; no marketing taglines, service/product info, location info, phone numbers or URLs in the name; adding unnecessary information can lead to suspension. [first-party] [verified-search: support.google.com, 2026-10-04]
3. GBP Help — "Manage your business category" (answer 7249669). https://support.google.com/business/answer/7249669 — One primary category that best describes the business; up to 9 additional categories; do not add a category for every product or service. [first-party] [verified-search: support.google.com, 2026-10-04]
4. GBP Help — "Manage your business attributes" (answer 9049526). https://support.google.com/business/answer/9049526 — Attributes (e.g. "Wi-Fi", "Has outdoor seating"); some only available in certain countries or categories. [first-party] [verified-search: support.google.com, 2026-10-04]
5. GBP Help — "Manage your service areas for service-area & hybrid businesses" (answer 9157481) and "Manage your business address" (answer 2853879). — A service-area business (SAB) visits or delivers to customers and does not serve them at its address; it should hide (clear) its address; overall service area should not extend beyond about 2 hours' driving time from the base. [first-party] [verified-search: support.google.com, 2026-10-04]
6. GBP Help — "Verify your business with a video recording" (answer 14271705); "Verify your business on Google" (answer 7107242). — Video verification: live, unedited, continuous recording, at least 30 seconds, from a mobile device; must show location (street signs, landmarks), proof the business exists (signage, equipment) and proof of management (cash register, back rooms, branded van, business documents). Works for storefront, SAB and hybrid. [first-party] [verified-search: support.google.com, 2026-10-04]
7. GBP Help — "Create & manage posts on your Business Profile" (answer 7342169). — Post types: Update, Offer (title + dates required, adds "View offer" button), Event (title + start/end dates). [first-party] [verified-search: support.google.com, 2026-10-04]
8. GBP Help — "Manage your Business Profile photos & videos" (answer 6103862) and photos/videos/posts content policy (answer 7213077). — JPG or PNG, 10 KB–5 MB, recommended 720×720 px, minimum 250×250 px; in focus, well lit, no significant alterations or excessive filters. [first-party] [verified-search: support.google.com, 2026-10-04]
9. GBP Help — "Understand your Business Profile performance & insights" (answer 9918094). — Metrics: searches (terms used), views (Search and Maps), calls, website clicks, directions, messages, bookings (via booking provider). Only for verified profiles; only metrics that apply appear. [first-party] [verified-search: support.google.com, 2026-10-04]
10. GBP Help — "Business links policies & guidelines" (answer 13769188). — Google crawls profile links (at most daily) to verify they lead to a valid, relevant page; unreachable links may be removed. [first-party] [verified-search: support.google.com, 2026-10-04]
11. Google Maps User Generated Content Policy — "Prohibited & restricted content" (answer 7400114, also under GBP Help). — Merchants must not discourage or prohibit negative reviews or selectively solicit positive reviews (i.e. review gating is banned); no incentives (payment, discounts, free goods) for reviews or for removing negative ones; must not pressure customers on premises or request specific content, including naming staff; soliciting genuine reviews without incentives is allowed. Violations of the Fake Engagement policy can lead to profile restrictions. [first-party] [verified-search: support.google.com, 2026-10-04]
12. GBP Help — "Business Profile restrictions for policy violations" (answer 14114287). — Restrictions on profiles with fake engagement (e.g. review removal, warnings). Detail not visible in snippet. [first-party] [verified-search: support.google.com, title only]
13. US Federal Trade Commission — "Federal Trade Commission Announces Final Rule Banning Fake Reviews and Testimonials", press release, Aug 2024. https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials — and "The Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule: Questions and Answers". 16 CFR Part 465. In effect 2024-10-21. Bans creating/selling fake reviews (including AI-generated, or by people with no real experience) and buying them when the business knew or should have known; bans unfounded legal threats, intimidation or false accusations to suppress negative reviews; bans claiming on-site reviews represent all or most reviews when they were suppressed by rating. Civil penalties up to $53,088 per violation (inflation-adjusted figure as shown in the FTC Q&A). [first-party: regulator] [verified-search: ftc.gov, 2026-10-04]

## B. International SEO

21. Google Search Central — "Managing Multi-Regional and Multilingual Sites". https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites — URL structure table: ccTLD (clear geotargeting, server location irrelevant, easy separation; expensive/limited availability, more infrastructure, strict requirements, one country only); subdomain on gTLD (easy setup, allows different server locations, easy separation; users may not recognise geotargeting from URL); subdirectory on gTLD (easy setup, low maintenance, same host; users may not recognise geotargeting, single server location, harder to separate). Also: avoid automatic redirection based on perceived user language; let users switch. URL parameters for locale not recommended [not re-verified]. [first-party] [verified-search: developers.google.com, 2026-10-04]
22. Google Search Central — "Localized Versions of your Pages" (hreflang). https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/localized-versions — Three methods: HTML link tags, HTTP headers, XML sitemap. Language code ISO 639-1, optional region ISO 3166-1 Alpha 2; other codes unsupported. Each version must list itself and all others; if A links to B, B must link back (return links) or annotations may be ignored. x-default = fallback when no language/region matches. [first-party] [verified-search: developers.google.com, 2026-10-04] Self-reference requirement: each variation lists one link per variant "including itself"; the set is identical on every version (same for sitemap entries). [verified-search: developers.google.com, 2026-10-04] "Region alone not valid" (e.g. hreflang="be") [not re-verified].
23. Google Search Central Blog — "How x-default can help you", May 2023; "Introducing x-default hreflang", Apr 2013. [first-party] [verified-search: titles]
24. Google Search Central — "How Google Crawls Locale-Adaptive Pages". https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/locale-adaptive-pages — Googlebot's default IPs appear US-based and it sends requests without Accept-Language; it also crawls from some non-US IPs; IP location analysis is "difficult and generally not reliable"; recommends separate locale URLs with hreflang. [first-party] [verified-search: developers.google.com, 2026-10-04]
25. Search Engine Land — "Google Search Console to remove International Targeting report", Aug 2022. https://searchengineland.com/google-search-console-to-remove-international-targeting-report-387477 — Deprecated 2022-08-24, removed 2022-09-22; country targeting setting "determined to have little value… no longer supported"; hreflang still supported. [secondary reporting of first-party] [verified-search: searchengineland.com, 2026-10-04]
26. Google Search Central — "Spam Policies for Google Web Search" (scaled content abuse). https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies — Scaled content abuse = many pages generated mainly to manipulate rankings, not help users, however made (automation, humans, or both). Examples include scraping feeds/search results and generating pages via automated transformations such as synonymising or translating, where little value is added. [first-party] [verified-search: developers.google.com, 2026-10-04]
27. Google Search Central — "Google Search's Guidance on Generative AI Content on Your Website". — Review AI output for accuracy; generating many pages without oversight is low effort. [first-party] [verified-search: developers.google.com, 2026-10-04]

## C. Content and architecture

30. Google Search Central — "SEO Link Best Practices for Google". https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable — Google can reliably crawl only `<a>` elements with an `href`; anchor text should be descriptive, reasonably concise and relevant; don't cram keywords; surrounding words matter; don't chain links; every page you care about should have a link from at least one other page on your site. [first-party] [verified-search: developers.google.com, 2026-10-04]
31. Google Search Central — "Crawl Budget Management For Large Sites". https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/large-site-managing-crawl-budget (now also under developers.google.com/crawling/docs/crawl-budget) — Intended for sites with 1M+ unique pages changing about weekly, or 10k+ pages changing daily (plus sites with a large portion of their URLs classified as "Discovered – currently not indexed" [verified-search: developers.google.com, 2026-10-04]); only ways to increase crawl budget: more serving capacity and more valuable content. Crawl budget = crawl capacity limit + crawl demand; duplicate/unimportant URLs waste crawl time; robots.txt to block unimportant URLs. [first-party] [verified-search: developers.google.com, 2026-10-04]
32. Google Search Central Blog — "Crawling December: Faceted navigation", Dec 2024; doc "Managing crawling of faceted navigation URLs" (developers.google.com/crawling/docs/faceted-navigation). — If filtered URLs needn't be indexed: disallow in robots.txt, or use URL fragments (#) for filters (Google generally ignores fragments). If they must be crawled: use standard `&` separators, consistent filter order, return 404 for empty combinations; rel=canonical and rel=nofollow (applied consistently) are weaker options; avoid redirecting empty results to a generic "not found" page. Blog URL: https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/12/crawling-december-faceted-nav [first-party] [verified-search: developers.google.com, 2026-10-04, re-checked]
33. Google Search Central — "Pagination Best Practices for Google" (ecommerce). https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/pagination-and-incremental-page-loading — Google no longer uses rel=next/prev; link pages sequentially with `<a href>`; give each page a unique URL (not a # fragment); don't canonicalise all pages to page 1 [snippet ambiguous; consistent with long-standing Google guidance, not re-verified]; paginated pages may share titles/descriptions. [first-party] [verified-search: developers.google.com, 2026-10-04]
34. Search Console Help — "What are impressions, position, and clicks?" (answer 7042828) and "Performance report (Search results)" (answer 7576553, 17011364). — Clicks, impressions, CTR (clicks ÷ impressions), average position = topmost position of your link per query, averaged; position recorded only when an impression occurs. Dimensions: query, page, country, device, search appearance, date [not re-verified: full list]. [first-party] [verified-search: support.google.com, 2026-10-04]
35. Search Engine Land — "Google warns against content pruning as CNET deletes thousands of pages", Aug 2023. https://searchengineland.com/google-warns-against-content-pruning-as-cnet-deletes-thousands-of-pages-430509 — Danny Sullivan (Google Search Liaison): deleting content because it is "old" is "not a thing"; removing pages may help a massive site get other content crawled, but doesn't make the whole site "so much better". [first-party via secondary reporting] [verified-search: searchengineland.com, 2026-10-04]
36. Google patent US11354342B2 — "Contextual estimation of link information gain", granted Jun 2022. https://patents.google.com/patent/US11354342B2 — Describes scoring how much new information a document adds relative to documents a user has already seen. A patent is not evidence Google uses it in ranking. Search Engine Land explainers: "Information gain in SEO: What it is and why it matters" (2023), "Information gain: Here's what this new SEO 'buzzword' really means" (2024). [first-party patent; practitioner interpretation] [verified-search: patents.google.com, searchengineland.com, 2026-10-04]
37. Google Search Central — "Creating Helpful, Reliable, People-First Content". — Self-assessment questions: original information/research/analysis, substantial value compared with other pages in results, first-hand expertise, clear authorship ("Who, How, Why"). [first-party] [verified-search: title; question details not re-verified]
38. Programmatic SEO examples — madx.digital "Programmatic SEO Examples: 6 SaaS Teardowns"; austinheaton.com; tryvizup.com (2025–2026). — Zapier: 25,000+ app-integration pages, reportedly ~16% of organic traffic; Wise: currency-pair converter pages with live rates, "reportedly tens of millions of visits a month"; Tripadvisor: location × category pages built on reviews and listings. Numbers are third-party estimates (often from SEO tool traffic models). [anecdote] [verified-search: practitioner blogs, 2026-10-04]
39. Topic clusters / hub-and-spoke pages — HubSpot popularised the "pillar page + cluster" model (~2017). No controlled evidence of a ranking effect beyond internal linking and coverage. [practitioner] [not re-verified]

## Open questions

- Has Google changed the local ranking help page wording from "prominence" to "popularity"? Read answer 7091 directly.
- Is GBP public Q&A fully removed worldwide, and is "Ask Maps" Google's official name? API discontinuation is first-party (item 16); public removal still only in agency blogs.
- Do GBP posts or photo counts affect ranking at all? Google says complete info helps "relevance"; there is no first-party statement about posts as a ranking factor. Practitioner tests are anecdotal.
- How much does NAP consistency still matter? Practitioner surveys rate citations low and declining; no controlled study found.
- Does Google enforce the FTC rule-style ban on review gating proactively, or only on reports? No data on enforcement rates.
- Resolved 2026-10-04: crawl budget thresholds unchanged at developers.google.com/crawling/docs/crawl-budget (1M+ pages weekly; 10k+ pages daily; large share Discovered – not indexed).
- Market-share figures for Naver, Seznam, Yandex and Baidu differ by source by 5–10 points; find StatCounter or national sources directly.
- Machine translation: is lightly post-edited machine translation treated differently from raw output in practice? Google's policy is about value and scale, not the tool.
- Traffic numbers for Zapier/Wise/Tripadvisor are tool estimates; no company-published figures found.
