## Open questions

- Scarcity: no large preregistered field test of fake vs real countdown timers on revenue and on later trust/returns was found. Regulators treat fake timers as illegal regardless of effect.
- Anchoring: Many Labs shows strong anchoring in estimation questions; how large anchoring is in real price negotiation and list-price displays, after publication bias, is less clear.
- Social proof: the towel-reuse case shows that "most people do X" can beat generic appeals in one setting and not another; when it works is not well mapped.
- Loss aversion: researchers agree it has moderators but not on how big it is at small, everyday stakes (the typical marketing context).
- Nudges: Maier et al. found no average effect after bias correction, but large heterogeneity; which nudge types survive (defaults are the best candidate) is still debated.
- Free samples and reciprocity: field evidence is mostly about trial and habit, not a felt obligation to repay; the mechanism behind sample-driven sales is unclear.
