# Does message match between ad and landing page raise conversion, and can a tool check it? (checked 2026-10-27)

Question: "Message match" (the page's headline and offer continue what the ad or link promised) is tagged practitioner consensus in `knowledge/landing-pages-and-cro.md` and `research/cro-landing-pages.md` §7, which found no controlled study. Is there controlled or field evidence that ad–page congruence raises conversion? What does Google say its "landing page experience" measures? And is word overlap between the ad text, UTM values or search query and the page's title and h1 a defensible proxy for a check in `audit_page`? This would change teardown step 2 in `landing_page_teardown` (`src/prompts.ts`), the playbook's "Message match" line, and whether a check gets built.

## 1. No controlled study isolates message match

- No peer-reviewed or large-platform experiment was found that changes only the match between ad and page and measures conversion. This repeats `cro-landing-pages.md` §7 after a wider search (sponsored search, display, native ads, dynamic text, information scent). [derived]
- The tests that do exist are vendor A/B write-ups. Each is one organisation, chosen for publication, and most changed several things at once:
  - A seminary's Facebook ad was changed to reuse the landing page's image. Sign-up rate 0.49% → 0.73%; 239,607 in total, per-arm counts not given. [vendor; A/B anecdote, 19]
  - A newsletter's page was changed to reuse the Facebook ad's photo, and its copy was shortened in the same change. 25.0% → 34.8%; 3,687 in total. [vendor; A/B anecdote, 20; bundled change]
  - A non-profit's page changed headline, copy, image, testimonial emphasis and default gift together. Donations 39/1,319 (2.96%) → 54/1,185 (4.56%); no significance reported. My two-proportion check gives z ≈ 2.1, p ≈ 0.04 two-sided. [vendor; A/B anecdote, 21; bundled change; z derived]
  - The headline and button verb were swapped to the verb in the visitor's search query (dynamic text). "31.4% more conversions"; 1,274 visitors in total; conversion rates not reported. [vendor; A/B anecdote, 22; the vendor sells the feature]
- Two of these test visual match (same image), not word match. None tests word overlap as such. [derived]

## 2. Field data: page relevance goes with better outcomes, but not causally

- Across one retailer's Google keywords, "an increase in landing page quality scores is associated with an increase in conversion rates." [research, 8; observational; one retailer; abstract only] Google's score blends relevance with other things (section 3). The 2007 working paper found landing-page-type dummies (home, search, product, category) not significant in a robustness check. [research, 8; working paper]
- In Yahoo ads, homepages converted above the dataset average and search-results pages below it. But for related queries, search-results pages beat category pages in head-to-head conversion comparisons 824 to 263. [research, 5; observational; >31,000 query-URL pairs] The authors warn that conversion means different things to different advertisers, so this is correlation. [research, 5] They argue a search-results page carries the query's context only when the site actually has what was searched for; otherwise "the context is completely lost". [research, 6; argument, not measured]
- Expert ratings of landing pages agreed with bounce rate: ads rated excellent had less than half the normalised bounce rate of ads rated bad, and ads that followed Google's quality guidelines bounced 25.4% less. [research, 7; observational; ~7,000 rated ads]
- Ad copy changes who clicks. In a field experiment, one call-to-action wording lowered clicks but raised conversions; the authors read it as visitors self-selecting on the ad's promise. [research, 11; abstract; one advertiser] So the page has to serve the visitors the ad's promise attracted. [derived]
- Native ads placed in-feed got more clicks and more bounces; in a lab study, being sent to the advertiser's site from an in-feed ad raised annoyance, which raised bounce intentions and lowered purchase intentions. [research, 12; abstract; lab, intentions] This is expectation mismatch about where a click goes, not about page wording.

## 3. What Google says it measures

- Landing page experience: "How relevant and useful your landing page is to people who click your ad." [first-party, 1] Pages with higher ratings "are usually well organized and have text that relates to a person's search terms." [first-party, 2]
- Each status is "based on a comparison with other advertisers whose ads showed for the exact same search over the last 90 days". Google does not say how it is computed; crawler data "may be used". Quality Score "is not an input in the ad auction"; it is a diagnostic. [first-party, 1]
- Advice: "Keep messaging consistent from ad to landing page." "Ensure the page follows through on the ad's offer or call to action." [first-party, 3] An ad for 20% off shoes should lead to a page where people can buy the shoes at that price, and the page "should also mirror the call-to-action in your ad text." [first-party, 4]
- And: "the word-for-word phrase from a search term doesn't need to be on your landing page." [first-party, 3] Google itself does not treat exact word match as the test.

## 4. Is word overlap a good proxy for match? Mostly no

- People rarely pick the same word for the same thing: in five domains, "two people favored the same term with probability <0.20". [research, 13; abstract via a secondary copy] A page can match an ad in meaning and share few words with it.
- Using landing-page text to judge whether an ad fits a query helped (+8.5% DCG@1 for the best method). Using only the page words that already appeared in the ad gave no gain (+0.0%); the gain came from page words not in the ad. [research, 9; editor judgments, not users] The same paper's preliminary look found "over 30% of the landing pages are not at all, or very remotely, related to the ads". [research, 9; not quantified further]
- Word-similarity features between keyword, ad text and landing page (cosine, KL divergence, redundancy) predicted bounce rate on their own, but weakly: correlation 0.180, against 0.303 for the full model. [research, 7; observational; millions of ads]
- On Yahoo native ads, noun and entity overlap between ad and landing page were model features but ranked below page-structure and readability features in the importance plot. [research, 10; my reading of a figure through the summary, not a stated result]
- Scent models that work use weighted or semantic similarity, not raw overlap: TF.IDF-weighted word similarity with spreading activation [research, 14], or Latent Semantic Analysis between user goals and link text [research, 15]. Practitioner guidance says the same thing in words: scent is "the user's imperfect estimate of the value", and jargon or vague labels weaken it. [practitioner, 16]
- Two more problems for the proposed inputs. A UTM campaign value is usually an internal label (for example "q4_retarget"), not words the visitor saw, so overlap with it means nothing. [derived] And a query in `utm_term` need not appear on the page, by Google's own advice. [first-party, 3]
- Narrow exception: a concrete offer in the ad (a price, a percentage, "free trial", a product name) is something Google says the page must show. Whether that exact token appears on the page is checkable, and its absence is worth a human look. [first-party, 4; check design derived]

## What this changes

1. **Playbook line (`knowledge/landing-pages-and-cro.md`, "Message match"): keep it out of [research]; add the first-party tag.** No measured effect isolates match, so the tag is not promoted. Proposed text: "**Message match**: the page shows the offer and action the ad or link promised. Google advises it and says the exact search words need not appear on the page [first-party]. Vendor tests that made ad and page match report lifts, but each changed several things at once [vendor; A/B anecdote]. No controlled study isolates it." Same change in teardown checklist item 2. [first-party, 1–4; vendor, 19–22]

2. **Teardown step 2 (`landing_page_teardown` in `src/prompts.ts`): make it concrete.** Proposed: "2. Message match: put the ad text or the linking text next to the title, h1 and first screen. Does the page show the specific offer, price, product and action the ad promised? Same meaning in other words is fine; exact words are not needed. If the source is unknown, ask for the ad text or the link that brings most visitors." It replaces the current line, so the instructions do not grow. Evidence grade: [first-party] plus [practitioner].

3. **No word-overlap score.** Overlap between ad, query or UTM values and the title and h1 is not a defensible proxy: same-meaning pages share few words [13], overlap carried no signal for ad relevance [9] and little for bounce [7], and Google says the exact words are not needed [3]. A score would mislead in both directions (synonyms read as mismatch; keyword-stuffed pages read as match). Do not build it.

4. **Buildable narrow check (optional): "offer tokens not found".** Input: an optional `adText` (or `promise`) string on `audit_page`. Rule: extract concrete offer tokens from it (numbers with %, currency amounts, "free", "trial", "no card", and words in quotes), normalise spacing and case, and search the page's visible text (server HTML, or rendered text when `render` is on). Output, only for tokens not found: `The ad text promises "20% off"; this text was not found on the page. Check that the offer is shown. Not checked: synonyms, images, text added by scripts after load (unless render mode), and later pages in the flow.` Also return the ad text next to the title, h1 and lead text for the agent to compare. Never flag a missing search keyword. Threshold: none; it is exact presence of a stated offer. Evidence behind it: Google's first-party advice on offer follow-through [4]; no measured effect size. Test: ad "Save 20% on running shoes" with a page lacking "20%" gives one flag; the same page with "20 %" in the text gives none; an ad with no offer tokens gives no flag.

5. **`cro-landing-pages.md` §7** can cite this note for the search scope and the first-party wording.

## Open questions

- Read the full texts of sources 5 and 8 to confirm Becker's Table 3 values and how Ghose & Yang measured landing-page quality in the published version.
- Does Microsoft Advertising document how it judges landing page relevance? Not searched.
- Would a semantic similarity (embeddings) between ad and page predict bounce or conversion better than overlap? Sources 7 and 15 suggest semantic measures do better than raw words, but none measured conversion. A deterministic tool can't run one without a model, and its output would need a validated threshold.
- No experiment found that changes only the words of the headline to match the ad, with conversions as the outcome and a pre-set sample size. That is the test that would promote the tag.
