## 2. Referral programs and word of mouth

10. Schmitt, P., Skiera, B. & Van den Bulte, C. "Referral Programs and Customer Value." *Journal of Marketing* 75(1), 2011, 46–59. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jm.75.1.46 ; PDF https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2012/04/Schmitt-Skiera-vandenBulte-2011-Referral-Programs-Customer-Value.pdf (blocked) — ~10,000 customers of a German bank, ~3 years: referred customers have higher contribution margin (gap erodes), higher retention (gap persists), higher value short and long run; CLV at least 16% higher than comparable non-referred customers; differential varies by segment. [research][read 2026-10-05]
11. Van den Bulte, C., Bayer, E., Skiera, B. & Schmitt, P. "How Customer Referral Programs Turn Social Capital into Economic Capital." *Journal of Marketing Research* 55(1), 2018, 132–146. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1509/jmr.14.0653 ; MSI working paper https://thearf-org-unified-admin.s3.amazonaws.com/MSI/2020/06/MSI_Report_15-102.pdf — ~1,800 referred customers and referrers, same bank, €25 reward; tests better matching and social enrichment as mechanisms. The authors' MSI version (2015) summarises the 2011 study as CLV 16%–25% higher; data are 1,800 referrals, their 1,800 referrers and 3,663 non-referred customers, reward a €25 voucher. Results: better matching drives the margin gap (referrer–referral pairs share unobserved margin traits; more experienced referrers bring higher-margin referrals, and that gap shrinks over time); social enrichment drives retention (referrals churn less only while their referrer stays a customer). Authors suggest recruiting referrers acquired 6+ months ago with high margins and low churn risk. [research][read 2026-10-05: abstract via OpenAlex and MSI working paper]
12. Kumar, V., Petersen, J. A. & Leone, R. P. "How Valuable Is Word of Mouth?" *Harvard Business Review*, October 2007. https://hbr.org/2007/10/how-valuable-is-word-of-mouth — Customer lifetime value vs customer referral value; heaviest buyers are often not the best referrers; telecom and financial-services data. [research, practitioner outlet][snippet-only] (re-check 2026-10-05: HBR summary confirms the biggest buyers are often not the best marketers; body and industry detail are paywalled)
13. Berger, J. "Word of mouth and interpersonal communication: A review and directions for future research." *Journal of Consumer Psychology* 24(4), 2014, 586–607. doi:10.1016/j.jcps.2014.05.002 — WOM is goal-driven and largely self-serving: impression management, emotion regulation, information acquisition, social bonding, persuasion. [research][read 2026-10-05: abstract via OpenAlex]
14. Ryu, G. & Feick, L. "A Penny for Your Thoughts: Referral Reward Programs and Referral Likelihood." *Journal of Marketing* 71(1), 2007, 84–94. — Four experiments: rewards raise referral likelihood, especially for weak ties and weaker brands; for strong ties/strong brands, rewarding the receiver is more effective. Measures intentions. [research][read 2026-10-05: abstract via OpenAlex]
15. Reichheld, F. "The One Number You Need to Grow." *Harvard Business Review*, December 2003. — Introduced NPS. Critics (e.g. MeasuringU replication https://measuringu.com/nps-replication/ ; Wikipedia summary) note it correlated NPS with past growth. [secondary for the critique]
16. Keiningham, T., Cooil, B., Andreassen, T. W. & Aksoy, L. "A Longitudinal Examination of Net Promoter and Firm Revenue Growth." *Journal of Marketing* 71(3), 2007, 39–51. https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228660597 — 21 firms, 15,500+ interviews, Norwegian Customer Satisfaction Barometer; failed to replicate NPS's "clear superiority" vs other measures (e.g. ACSI). Won MSI/H. Paul Root Award. [research][read 2026-10-05: abstract via OpenAlex; award not re-checked]
17. Keiningham, T., Cooil, B., Aksoy, L., Andreassen, T. W. & Weiner, J. "The value of different customer satisfaction and loyalty metrics in predicting customer retention, recommendation, and share-of-wallet." *Managing Service Quality* 17(4), 2007, 361–384. https://www.emerald.com/jstp/article-abstract/17/4/361/302721 — 8,000+ US customers, two-year panel, three industries; recommend intention alone does not suffice as a single predictor of future loyalty behaviour; multi-indicator models predicted recommendation and retention better. [research][read 2026-10-05: abstract via OpenAlex]
18. Dropbox, Inc. Form S-1, 2018-02-23. https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001467623/000119312518055809/d451946ds1.htm — Users acquired efficiently via word of mouth, in-product referrals and content sharing; >90% of revenue from self-serve channels; users described as "best salespeople". [first-party][read 2026-10-05]
19. Houston, D. "Startup Lessons Learned" talk, April 2010. Slides: https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/dropbox-startup-lessons-learned-3836587/3836587 (blocked) — As relayed by ReferralCandy, Viral Loops and others: two-sided 500MB reward "permanently increased signups by 60%"; 100K (Sep 2008) → 4M users (Jan 2010); referrals ~35% of daily signups; 2.8M direct invites in 30 days. ## 3. Outbound: law, deliverability, benchmarks, AI

20. FTC, "CAN-SPAM Act: A Compliance Guide for Business." https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business — Honest headers/subject, identify as ad, valid physical postal address, clear opt-out, honour within 10 business days; penalties up to $53,088 per violating email. [first-party][read 2026-10-05][verified-search: ftc.gov, 2026-10-04] Penalty is normally inflation-adjusted yearly; no 2026 adjustment was made (OMB M-26-11 cancelled it), so $53,088 still applies as of 2026-10-04 [verified-search: federalregister.gov, ftc.gov, 2026-10-04].
21. ICO, "Business-to-business marketing." https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/ — PECR email consent rule does not apply to corporate subscribers; must identify sender and give opt-out address; sole traders and some partnerships are individual subscribers (consent or soft opt-in); if unsure, treat as individual. [first-party][read 2026-10-05]
22. CRTC, CASL guidance on implied consent and FAQ. https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/guide.htm ; https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm ; https://fightspam.gc.ca/eic/site/030.nsf/eng/00013.html — Consent (express or implied), identification, unsubscribe; conspicuous publication implied consent requires no "no CEMs" statement and relevance to business role; sender bears burden of proof; AMPs up to C$1M (individual) / C$10M (business) per violation. [first-party][read 2026-10-05: CASL s.10(9)(b), s.13 and s.20(4) on laws-lois.justice.gc.ca, penalties also on ISED's fightspam page; crtc.gc.ca returned 403][verified-search: crtc.gc.ca, 2026-10-04]

B2B): from vendor/legal blogs (e.g. https://www.kahlemadvisory.com/articles/cold-email-france-b2b/ ; https://itsalesaas.com/blog/is-cold-email-legal-in-europe). [secondary; not re-verified on cnil.fr or gesetze-im-internet.de]
24. Instantly, "Cold Email Benchmark Report 2026." https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026 — Average reply rate 3.43%, top quartile 5.5%, elite >10%; first email gets 58% of replies, follow-ups 42%; recommends 4–7 touches. [vendor][read 2026-10-05] Report updated 2026-01-12; "billions" of interactions across Instantly workspaces. Denominator: overall reply rate = all replies (including replies to follow-ups) divided by total emails sent, i.e. per email, the same basis as Belkins 2026.
25. Belkins, "What are B2B Cold Email Response Rates? (2026 Study)." https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates — 7.5M emails from Belkins client campaigns in 2025; average 0.45% replies per email sent (H1 0.50%, H2 0.40%; Feb peak 0.54%, Dec 0.35%); earlier Belkins studies used opened emails as denominator. [vendor][read 2026-10-05; corrected] Follow-ups (Belkins companion study on the same 7.5M emails, https://belkins.io/blog/sales-follow-up-statistics, updated 2026-06-26): the first email has the highest per-step reply rate (0.59%), but steps 2–6 together get 58.6% of replies; suggested 3–5 steps, with returns falling fast after step 5. (Not "first follow-up highest".) Belkins 2025 benchmarks report (with Reply.io, Expandi, Nooks; 16.5M emails) is a separate dataset: https://belkins.io/resources/b2b-cold-outreach-benchmarks
26. Jakesch, M., French, M., Ma, X., Hancock, J. & Naaman, M. "AI-Mediated Communication: How the Perception that Profile Text was Written by AI Affects Trustworthiness." CHI 2019. https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3290605.3300469 — "Replicant effect": in mixed sets, profiles believed AI-written are trusted less; in all-AI sets, not. [research][read 2026-10-05: authors' PDF, github.com/sTechLab/aimc-chi19]
27. Liu, Y. et al. "Will AI Console Me when I Lose my Pet? Understanding Perceptions of AI-Mediated Email Writing." CHI 2022. https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3491102.3517731 — Surveys and interviews: trust in email writers fell when people were told AI was involved; surprisingly, trust rose when AI was used for more interpersonal (vs transactional) emails. [research][read 2026-10-05: abstract via OpenAlex]
28. Claims that reply rates fell from "8.5% in 2019" to 3.43% (relayed by hausadvisors.com and others) compare different vendors' datasets. [secondary] Not used.

## Open questions

- Dropbox: confirm the 60% / 35% / 2.8M figures against Houston's original slides or a recorded talk; confirm whether "60%" was sign-ups per day or a sustained level.
- Van den Bulte et al. 2018: which mechanism (matching vs social enrichment) carries most of the effect, and how reward size interacts.
- Is there causal (randomized or quasi-experimental) evidence that partner involvement raises win rates, rather than partners joining likely winners?
- Any controlled study of AI-written cold email on reply rates in real B2B sending (not lab trust studies)?
- Instantly's 3.43%: per recipient or per email? Which replies count (auto-replies, negatives)?
- EU: confirm CNIL and UWG §7 positions on the regulators' and statute sites; check other large markets (Netherlands, Italy, Spain, Nordics).
- NPS: Baehre et al. (2022, *Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science*, "The use of Net Promoter Score (NPS) to predict sales growth") revisits NPS and growth with brand-level data; findings not re-verified this session.
- Affiliate economics: any independent data on what share of affiliate-credited sales is incremental (e.g. coupon-site holdout studies)?
- ABM: any study with matched control accounts, rather than practitioner self-reports?
