# Do freelance platforms get a new designer paying clients, and what do their terms forbid? (checked 2026-10-28)

Question: a freelance designer with zero clients could sign up to Upwork, Fiverr, Malt or Contra. ## 1. What it costs to bid

- Upwork: Connects cost "$0.15 each with a minimum of 10". A free Basic account gets 10 a month. A proposal's Connect price "varies by job". The freelancer fee on standard contracts runs from 0% to 15%. [first-party, 1, fetch summary; undated]
- Fiverr takes "a flat 20% commission on every sale", tips included. Buyers pay a separate service fee; the same page gives two different figures for it. [first-party, 7, fetch summary; marketing page]
- Malt: 10% on a project with a client met on Malt, 5% once the relationship passes six months, 2% for a client the freelancer invites. [first-party, 8, fetch summary] [corrected 2026-10-28: the rates are excl. VAT; sole traders not liable for VAT pay 12% and 6%, per the same page]
- Contra charges no commission. On the free plan a payment carries a flat platform fee: $15 for $1–499, $29 for $500 or more; Pro removes it. The page does not say who pays it. [first-party, 9, fetch summary] [corrected 2026-10-28: source 9 does not mention commission; "0% Commission" is on contra.com/commission-free (read 2026-10-28; fetch summary; promotional), which also gives a $19 contract fee charged to companies and Pro at $29 a month, figures that differ from source 9]

## 2. How often a new profile wins a first job

- No platform page seen gives this. The evidence is two oDesk studies (oDesk is now Upwork), both old.
- Stanton & Thomas, 83,029 non-US workers who applied for a job in 2008–09: about 27% of agency members and 10% of non-members found at least one job by Aug 2010. Non-members who were hired took a median of 13 days and 10 applications. [research, 11, fetch summary; observational; one platform; all job types]
- Good feedback on the first job raised a non-member's chance of a second job by 18–19 percentage points. [research, 11, fetch summary; observational]
- Pallais hired 952 randomly chosen data-entry workers out of 3,767 applicants. Of inexperienced workers not hired, "only 12% obtained any job in the next two months". Being hired, with a short public rating, raised that to 30%. A more detailed rating raised later hiring and earnings further. [research, 10, fetch summary; RCT; one platform; data entry at $3/hour or less] [corrected 2026-10-28: the detailed rating raised experienced workers' earnings ($101 to $187); for inexperienced workers it did not significantly improve employment beyond the coarse rating]
- Together: most new profiles never win a job, and the first job and its rating unlock the next ones. Both studies are low-wage, mostly non-US and 15 years old. [derived from 10, 11]

## 3. Moving clients off the platform

- Upwork: both sides must "keep all work and payments on Upwork for the first two years". Leaving earlier costs a conversion fee of 13.5% of the freelancer's hourly rate × 2,080 hours, paid in one go; the two sides decide who pays. [first-party, 2, fetch summary] At $30 an hour that is $8,424, one of the page's own examples. [derived from 2]
- Paying outside without the fee "will result in account suspension". [first-party, 3, fetch summary] The Terms of Use also bar sharing contact details before a contract. [first-party, 4, fetch summary] Older agreement copies give the same 24-month period. [secondary, 14] Enterprise clients have their own fee. [first-party, 5]
- Fiverr forbids offering or accepting payment outside Fiverr, steering users to other platforms, and sharing contact details "with the sole purpose of conducting business off-platform". Accounts "risk permanent suspension". The page gives no time limit. [first-party, 6, fetch summary; undated]
- Malt and Contra: not checked. Malt's falling commission rewards staying, which suggests long relationships are common there, but that is my reading, not a number. [derived from 8]

## 4. Do platform clients come back?

- Stanton & Thomas report that about 68% of workers are re-hired after their first job. The summary does not say whether that means the same client. [research, 11, fetch summary; wording unchecked]
- Gu & Zhu: once trust is high, the two sides tend to go around the platform to avoid fees. [research, 12, abstract-only] Interviews found platform contacts later trading work directly. [qualitative, 13; preliminary]
- No source gives a share of clients who rehire the same freelancer on the platform.
