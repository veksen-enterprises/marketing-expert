# Selling digital products across borders: sales taxes, merchants of record, app stores and pricing for a Quebec founder

Research date: 2026-10-06. **Not tax advice; check with an accountant.** Scope: a founder based in Quebec, Canada, working as a sole proprietor or through a small corporation, selling SaaS subscriptions, a hosted developer service, in-app premium tiers and freemium upgrades to customers in Canada, the United States, the European Union, Turkey, Armenia and Argentina. Covers indirect taxes (GST/HST, QST, provincial sales taxes, US sales tax, EU VAT, Turkish VAT and digital service tax, Argentine IVA and card perceptions, Armenian VAT), app stores and merchants of record, payment providers, country pricing, and income tax in brief.

Not repeated here (already in the repo): consumer-law rules on prices, subscriptions and cancellation in Quebec, Canada, the US, Turkey, Armenia and Argentina (research/marketing-law-canada-us.md, research/marketing-law-turkey-armenia-argentina.md); pricing method (value metric, packaging, willingness to pay) in knowledge/pricing.md.

Access marks: **[read 2026-10-06]** = the primary page or document was opened and the passage read (the Apple agreement and the Armenian Tax Code were downloaded and searched as text); **[read 2026-10-06; fetch summary]** = fetched, but read through a summarising fetch tool, not line by line; **[snippet-only]** = search-result snippets only. Sites that refused or could not be read: revenuquebec.ca (403 to every automated request, so all Revenu Québec items are snippet-only), digitalservice.gib.gov.tr (JavaScript-only portal, no text), oecd.org (403), the INDEC CPI PDF (read for monthly and year-to-date figures only).

Evidence quality: almost everything here is first-party law, regulator guidance or platform documentation. There is no controlled research on what tax setup or country pricing works best for a small software seller; the "what this means" section is our reading, not tested evidence.

## Canada: GST/HST, QST and provincial sales taxes

1. **CRA, "When to register for and start charging the GST/HST"** (updated 2026-06-16). https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html — The small supplier threshold is $30,000 for most businesses, measured over four consecutive calendar quarters or in a single calendar quarter. "Include the total amount of all revenues (before expenses) from your worldwide taxable supplies"; sole proprietors add revenue from all their businesses and from associates. Excluded: financial services, sales of capital property, goodwill. If you pass $30,000 in a single quarter, you must register and "start charging GST/HST on the supply that made you exceed $30,000". Below the threshold you "may choose to register voluntarily". [first-party][read 2026-10-06; fetch summary]
2. **CRA, GST/HST Memorandum 2-2, "Small suppliers"** (revised 3 June 2026). https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/2-2/small-suppliers.html — Four-quarter rule: when revenue over the last four consecutive quarters passes $30,000, you stop being a small supplier at the end of the month after that quarter; registration is effective from the first supply after that date, with 29 days to register. Single-quarter rule: you stop being a small supplier immediately before the supply that crosses $30,000 in that quarter. Zero-rated supplies count (they are taxable supplies); exempt supplies, goodwill, capital property and financial services do not. Associated persons include controlled corporations and partnerships or trusts over 50%. [first-party][read 2026-10-06; fetch summary]
3. **CRA, RC4022 "General Information for GST/HST Registrants"** (Rev. 25). https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/rc4022/general-information-gst-hst-registrants.html — Input tax credits (ITCs) let registrants "recover the GST/HST paid or payable on purchases and expenses related to your commercial activities". A voluntary registrant must "stay registered for at least one year" before asking to cancel. Annual filing is available when taxable revenue is "$1,500,000 or less". Exports of most goods and services are zero-rated. [first-party][read 2026-10-06; fetch summary]
4. **CRA, GST/HST Memorandum 4-5-3, "Exports – Services and Intellectual Property"** (December 2023). https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/4-5-3/exports-services-intangible-personal-property.html — A service supplied to a non-resident is zero-rated (taxed at 0%) if the recipient is not GST-registered and, for individuals, is not in Canada when the service is performed; supplies of intangible personal property (software licences, for example) to non-residents are zero-rated on similar conditions, except property that can only be used in Canada. The supplier should verify residency, for example by a self-declaration with supporting evidence. Exclusions include advisory and professional services (some still qualify elsewhere), telecommunications and services about Canadian property. [first-party][read 2026-10-06; fetch summary]
5. **CRA, "GST/HST and place-of-supply rules"** (updated 2026-07-15). https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-place-supply.html — General rule 1 for services and intangible property: "The place of supply is the province of the recipient's address if the supplier obtains that address in the normal course of business"; with several addresses, use the one most closely connected with the supply. General rule 2 applies when no address is obtained. [first-party][read 2026-10-06; fetch summary]
6. **CRA, GST/HST rates by province** (rates as of 1 April 2025). https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html — GST 5% in Alberta, BC, Manitoba, Saskatchewan, Quebec and the territories; HST 13% Ontario, 14% Nova Scotia (cut from 15% on 1 April 2025), 15% New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, PEI. Provincial taxes listed: Quebec QST 9.975%, BC PST 7%, Manitoba 7%, Saskatchewan 6%. [first-party][read 2026-10-06; fetch summary]
7. **Revenu Québec, "Small Suppliers"** and **"Registering With Revenu Québec"**. https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/citizens/consumption-taxes/taxable-zero-rated-or-tax-exempt-goods-and-services/gst-and-qst/other-situations/small-suppliers/ — Same $30,000 test for QST, counting worldwide taxable supplies "including zero-rated supplies" by you and your associates in a quarter or the four preceding quarters. ## United States: sales tax

15. **South Dakota v. Wayfair, Inc., 585 U.S. 162 (21 June 2018).** https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/17-494 — Overruled the physical-presence rule of Quill (1992) and National Bellas Hess (1967); states may require remote sellers to collect sales tax. South Dakota's law reached sellers with over $100,000 of sales or 200 separate transactions into the state a year. [first-party][read 2026-10-06; fetch summary]
16. **Texas Comptroller, "Remote Sellers."** https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/sales/remote-sellers.php — Remote sellers with Texas revenue under $500,000 in the preceding 12 months need no permit. Above it, collect "no later than the first day of the fourth month" after crossing. The measure includes taxable and non-taxable sales and marketplace sales. Sellers whose marketplace certifies it collects are exempt from the permit but keep records four years. [first-party][read 2026-10-06; fetch summary]
17. **Texas Comptroller, Publication 96-259, "Taxable Services"** (revised January 2026). https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/publications/96-259.php — "Twenty percent of the charge for data processing services is exempt from tax"; "Data processing services providers include sellers of software as a service and application service providers." So Texas taxes 80% of a SaaS charge. [first-party][read 2026-10-06; fetch summary]
18. **New York Department of Taxation and Finance, "Registration requirement for businesses with no physical presence in New York State"** (updated 2026-07-29). https://www.tax.ny.gov/pubs_and_bulls/publications/sales/nexus.htm — Register if, in the preceding four sales tax quarters, gross receipts from tangible personal property delivered into the state "exceeded $500,000, and" the seller "made more than 100 sales". Both conditions must be met. [first-party][read 2026-10-06; fetch summary]
19. **New York, "Computer Software" bulletin.** https://www.tax.ny.gov/pubs_and_bulls/tg_bulletins/st/computer_software.htm — Prewritten software is taxable "whether sold on a disk ... by electronic transmission, or by remote access"; a licence to remotely access software, sold to a New York purchaser, is subject to sales tax. New York treats prewritten software as tangible personal property, so SaaS sales count toward its threshold. [first-party][snippet-only]
20. **Washington DOR, "Digital products including digital goods"** (rules effective 1 October 2025) and **"Remote sellers."** https://dor.wa.gov/forms-publications/publications-subject/tax-topics/digital-products-including-digital-goods — Remote access software (SaaS) "is subject to sales or use tax"; digital automated services are subject to retail sales tax; the business-purpose exemption covers digital goods only, not SaaS. Remote sellers register above $100,000 of gross receipts sourced to Washington in the current or prior year. [first-party][read 2026-10-06; fetch summary; threshold snippet-only]
21. **California CDTFA, "Use Tax Collection Requirements Based on Sales into California Due to the Wayfair Decision."** https://cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/wayfair/ — Remote sellers register when sales of tangible personal property for delivery in California exceed $500,000 in the current or preceding calendar year (from 1 April 2019). Whether SaaS is taxable in California was not checked at a primary source. [first-party][snippet-only]
22. **Stripe Docs, "Collect tax in the United States."** https://docs.stripe.com/tax/supported-countries/united-states — Nexus can be physical or economic; each state sets its own threshold; you register state by state; Stripe can register for you and file through partners (Taxually, Marosa, Hands-off Sales Tax). "In the US, marketplace facilitator laws now exist in every state and territory with a sales tax, as well as the District of Columbia," requiring the marketplace to collect for third-party sellers. 24 states belong to the Streamlined Sales and Use Tax Agreement. [vendor][read 2026-10-06]
23. **Stripe Guide, "Introduction to SaaS taxability in the US"** (updated 24 November 2025). https://stripe.com/guides/introduction-to-saas-taxability-in-the-us — 25 states tax SaaS, and 7 more tax it if the customer must download software; some states tax it differently for business and personal use. ## European Union: VAT on digital services

24. **European Commission, VAT One Stop Shop, "Register."** https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/one-stop-shop/register-oss_en — "Any taxable person, not established in the EU, who supplies services to non-taxable persons taking place in the EU, can register in the non-Union scheme." A non-EU business "can choose any Member State to be the Member State of identification", gets a number in the form EUxxxyyyyyz used only for the scheme, and starts from the first day of the quarter after it applies. ## Armenia

41. **Tax Code of the Republic of Armenia**, official English text on ARLIS. https://www.arlis.am/en/acts/205620 — Article 2: an electronic service is a service over an information and telecommunication network, including the internet, "provision whereof is impossible without the use of information technology"; the Government publishes the list. Article 39(2.1): a B2C electronic service is supplied in Armenia when the individual's residence, bank or e-money operator, IP address, or phone country code is in Armenia (the state meeting more conditions wins). Article 288(9.1): a non-resident organisation without a permanent establishment (a fixed place of business in the country) that provides electronic services to an individual "shall be placed on record-registration with the tax authority". Article 70(2)(5): that organisation bears the VAT itself; Article 70(2)(3): the "VAT threshold shall not be considered" in these cases. Reports and payment are due by the 20th day of the month after each quarter. Article 63: VAT rate 20%. B2B: an Armenian VAT payer customer accounts for the VAT as tax agent (Article 70(2)). [first-party][read 2026-10-06; downloaded and searched]

[secondary][read 2026-10-06; fetch summary]

## What this means

- **The $30,000 GST/QST small-supplier test counts worldwide sales, including zero-rated exports and app-store and merchant-of-record revenue** (items 1, 2, 7). A founder selling mostly abroad can still cross it. Our reading: sales to a non-resident merchant of record such as Paddle are probably zero-rated exports that still count. [judgment]
- **Apple overrides the small-supplier option for Canadian developers.** Paid apps and in-app purchases require a GST/HST number from Canadian residents and a QST number from Quebec residents, plus the s.177(1.1) and s.41.0.1 elections so Apple Canada collects (item 45). Google instead collects GST/HST itself unless you give your number, and makes a Quebec developer who gives a QST number responsible for QST (item 46). These two rules point opposite ways.
- **Once registered, a Quebec seller selling direct charges GST/HST by the customer's province, plus QST to Quebec customers**, using the customer's address (items 5, 6). Sales to non-residents are generally zero-rated (item 4). BC, Saskatchewan and Manitoba have their own PST registration rules for out-of-province software sellers (items 13–14).
- **The US has no federal sales tax and no threshold that a small foreign seller crosses quickly**: most states use $100,000; Texas, California and New York use $500,000 (New York also needs 100 sales) (items 15–21). SaaS taxability varies by state; Texas taxes 80% of the charge; Washington and New York tax SaaS (items 17, 19, 20, 23). Marketplace facilitator laws exist in every sales-tax state (item 22), which is why app stores collect.
- **The EU gives a non-EU seller no threshold**: VAT is due on the first B2C sale, at the customer's country rate, reported quarterly in euros through the non-Union OSS in one chosen Member State (items 24–27).
- **Turkey and Armenia also have no threshold** for foreign B2C e-services; Turkey files monthly in lira (items 28–30), Armenia quarterly (item 41). Turkey's DST thresholds (TRY 20 million local and EUR 750 million global) are far above a small seller (item 31).
- **Argentina has no foreign-provider registration**: IVA (21%) on digital services is collected from the buyer by card issuers or paid by the buyer, and a separate 30% income-tax perception applies to card payments abroad (items 32–38). Neither Apple, Stripe Tax nor Stripe Managed Payments lists Argentina (items 45, 58, 59).
- **Merchants of record cost more per sale but remove registrations**: Paddle and Lemon Squeezy 5% + 50¢ (Lemon Squeezy adds up to 3.5% more for international, PayPal and subscriptions); Stripe Managed Payments +3.5% on top of 2.9% + CA$0.30; Stripe Tax alone 0.5% where registered, with the seller doing registrations (items 50–59).
- **App Store automatic price updates skip auto-renewable subscriptions** (item 48). In Türkiye and Argentina, with annual inflation near 30% and 33.5% (items 40, 61), subscription prices set in local currency lose value unless the developer updates them.

## Folklore and weak claims

- "Under $30,000 you can ignore sales tax." True for GST/QST collection on direct sales, but Apple makes registration a condition of selling paid apps for Canadian residents (item 45), and the threshold counts worldwide sales (items 1, 7).
- "Non-EU sellers get the EUR 10,000 threshold." No: that threshold is for EU-established sellers in one Member State (items 26–27).
- "SaaS is not taxable in the US." It depends on the state; at least Texas, Washington and New York tax it (items 17, 19, 20).
- "A merchant of record handles all my taxes." It handles the indirect taxes on sales it makes, in the countries it covers. Income tax stays with you (item 45 for Apple; same logic for Paddle and Stripe). Stripe Managed Payments leaves countries outside its list, Argentina included, with you (item 59).
- "PPP discounts raise revenue." No study was found that tests this for small software sellers (item 62). Vendor case stories exist; they are self-selected.
