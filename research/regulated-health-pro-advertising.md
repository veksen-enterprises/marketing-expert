# What advertising rules bind a regulated health professional in Quebec and Canada, and may they take online clients in other provinces or US states? (checked 2026-10-28)

Question: a registered dietitian in Quebec, selling video sessions, asks for first clients. `match_small_bets` offers `comparison-pages` now and `ask-happy-users` (reviews and testimonials) once there are users. Does the dietitians' code of ethics ban testimonials, comparisons, promised results or price ads? Is "nutritionniste" a reserved title, and since when? May the dietitian take online clients in Ontario or a US state? Ontario's standard is read alongside, so the answer isn't Quebec-only by accident.

## 1. Quebec dietitians: testimonials allowed unless false or misleading

- The code in force is C-26, r. 97.1, adopted by decree 38-2025. It replaced r. 97 (the draft was published 28 August 2024). [law, 1–2, read; Quebec; in force 2025, exact date not seen]
- s.76(3): a dietitian may not use or allow "un témoignage d'appui ou de reconnaissance le concernant qui est faux ou trompeur". This bans false or misleading testimonials, not testimonials. [law, 1, read; fetch summary; Quebec; consolidated 1 July 2026]
- No provision names comparisons or guarantees. The general rule is s.74: no ad that is "fausse, trompeuse, incomplète ou susceptible d'induire en erreur". s.43 bars false claims about "l'étendue et à l'efficacité" of services. [law, 1, read; fetch summary; Quebec; 2026]
- Price ads (s.79) must state what the fee includes and how long it is valid. [law, 1; Quebec; 2026]
- Also: no "insidious, pressing or repeated" solicitation (s.56); avoid a commercial image (s.76(4)); sponsored content may only educate (s.76); keep a copy of each ad for a year after its last run (s.81); the title may not appear on product packaging (s.82). [law, 1; Quebec; 2026]
- Quebec psychologists' code (r. 212) has no testimonial word in its advertising chapter either; claims of skill or effectiveness must be justifiable (s.77). [law, 5, read; fetch summary; Quebec; 2026; a summary can miss a clause]

## 2. Ontario dietitians: no asking for testimonials or reviews

- CDO Standard 5 (September 2024): dietitians "must not solicit clients, former clients or caregivers for testimonials or reviews". Ads they control must not include, link to or solicit reviews (Google reviews named). No incentives. They must take reasonable steps to remove testimonials, including those an agency posted. [first-party, 6, read; fetch summary; Ontario; approved 2024]
- Standard 4: no saying their services are better than other dietitians'; no implied guaranteed outcomes; no "specialist" title. Before-and-after photos need express consent, must show typical outcomes and carry no testimonial. [first-party, 6; Ontario; 2024] [corrected 2026-10-28: the photo lines are a guideline under Standard 5, not Standard 4; they say photos should not be manipulated, need express consent, carry no testimonial statement, and clients should be told outcomes may vary; "typical outcomes" was not in the text seen]
- BC's college says it "is moving to ban testimonials, including before/after photos", with no date. [first-party, 7; BC; undated; status in force not confirmed]
- So the rule depends on profession and province. Quebec alone would have suggested no restriction; Ontario's is close to a ban. [derived from 1, 6]

## 3. The reserved title

- "Diététiste", "diététicien" and "nutritionniste", their English forms and Dt.P., P.Dt. and RD are reserved to members by Code des professions s.36(c). [first-party, 3; Quebec; June 2026]
- "Nutritionniste" has been reserved "à partir de 1994", per the order's own legal column. The amending law is not named there, and s.36's notes were not read. [first-party, 4; one author; law not verified]
- Most other provinces reserve only "dietitian", so "nutritionist" is open there. [first-party, 4; 2015; may have changed]
- The order is now the Ordre des diététistes-nutritionnistes du Québec (ODNQ), not OPDQ. The rename date was not found. [first-party, 3, 8]

## 4. Online clients in another province or a US state

- The general principle: dietitians practising across a border "doivent connaître et respecter les exigences d'inscription dans la province où habite leur client". [first-party, 10; Canada]
- Registration in the client's province is required for virtual services in BC, AB, SK, MB, ON, QC and PEI (SK by a temporary licence). NB, NS and NL ask for disclosure instead, NB also for notice beforehand. [first-party, 8–9; Canada; 2022 and February 2024; "may change"] [corrected 2026-10-28: the 2022 Alliance chart (8) lists NL as disclosure, but the February 2024 CDBC table (9) says NL "must be registered in NL"; SK is registration in 2022 and a temporary licence in 2024; NS's 2024 wording is advice, not a requirement]
- So a Quebec dietitian with an Ontario client online needs Ontario registration. [derived from 8–9]
- US: practitioners must comply with licensure laws of "the states where the patients/clients are physically located at the time of service". The Academy advises holding a licence in every state where clients are. California, Colorado and Virginia protect titles only, without licensing. [first-party, 11–12; US; undated]
- The Dietitian Licensure Compact expects first privileges "sometime in 2027"; it rests on a US home-state licence, so it does not seem to cover a Canadian. [first-party, 13; derived on Canada]
