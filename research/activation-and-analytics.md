# Activation and product analytics for a small team

Research date: 2026-10-05. Scope: what to track first, event naming, a minimal tracking plan, defining activation (including products that need setup), privacy-friendly and self-hosted tools, consent for analytics, measuring with very small numbers, CLI and developer-tool telemetry, dashboards vs vanity metrics.

Builds on, and does not repeat: research/measurement.md (attribution, AARRR, north star, leading vs lagging), research/b2b-saas-models.md (Rachitsky activation method and benchmark, PQLs), research/privacy-and-marketing-law.md (cookie banner rules, CNIL fines, UK Data (Use and Access) Act analytics exception), research/small-bets-direct.md (founder emails, Retana et al. 2016 onboarding field experiment, Groove reply rates).

Access marks: **[read 2026-10-05]** = page or PDF read in full or in the relevant part; **[read 2026-10-05; abstract]** = abstract or press release only; **[snippet-only]** = only a search-result snippet. Evidence tags follow knowledge/_conventions.md. Vendor docs about naming conventions are tagged [first-party; vendor]: they document the vendor's own product but the vendor sells analytics, so "track more, structure it our way" is in its interest.

## 1. Event naming conventions (vendor documentation)

1. Twilio Segment, "Data Collection Best Practices" (Protocols, tracking plan). https://www.twilio.com/docs/segment/protocols/tracking-plan/best-practices (the segment.com URL returned 403). — Recommends the "Object (Blog Post) + Action (Read)" framework; "Segment recommends Title Case for event names and snake_case for property names"; most successful customers "limit their Tracking Plan to a minimal number of core events with rich properties that provide context"; avoid dynamic values in event names (example "User Signed Up (11-01-2019)") and dynamic property keys ("feature_1":"true"). Says Segment itself started by tracking three core events: User Signed Up, Source Data Sent, Subscription Started. [first-party; vendor][read 2026-10-05]
2. PostHog, "Product analytics best practices." https://posthog.com/docs/product-analytics/best-practices — Recommends `category:object_action` names, lowercase snake case, **present-tense** verbs ("submit", "create", not "submitted"); examples `signup_flow:pricing_page_view`, `account_settings:forgot_password_button_click`. Properties: object_adjective, `is_`/`has_` prefixes for booleans, `_date`/`_timestamp` suffixes. Warns that teams "rely on autocapture and miss instrumenting a proper user_signed_up event". Says "Backend analytics are more reliable than frontend analytics" (blockers, interrupted JavaScript); use frontend for journeys. Identity mistakes: catch-all IDs like "system", inconsistent ID formats, not linking anonymous and identified IDs. Recommends a reverse proxy to get past ad blockers and filtering out internal users. [first-party; vendor][read 2026-10-05]
3. Amplitude, "Data planning playbook." https://amplitude.com/docs/data/data-planning-playbook — Consistent capitalization ("Song Played" and "song played" are tracked as different events); "[Noun] + [Past Tense Verb]"; one consistent actor, the user's perspective; default events in Title Case. No fixed starting number of events; "plan and instrument your most important metrics, then iterate later". Steps: business objectives → key metrics → critical user actions → consolidate similar events, consistent property definitions. [first-party; vendor][read 2026-10-05]

Note: the three vendors disagree on tense (Segment and Amplitude past tense; PostHog present tense) and case (Title Case vs lowercase snake case). They agree on object then action, one convention applied everywhere, few events with properties, and no variable values inside names.

## 2. Activation: definitions and evidence that early activation predicts retention

4. Lenny Rachitsky, "How to determine your activation metric," Lenny's Newsletter, 2022. https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-determine-your-activation — Three steps: candidate aha moments, correlation with retention, experiment for causality; "A good activation metric is causal for your retention, not just correlative." Quotes a respondent with about 300 customers who would not decide on data alone when results were similar, because of noise. [practitioner][read 2026-10-05; paywalled after the opening] Method already recorded in research/b2b-saas-models.md §16.
5. Reforge, "Define your setup moment." https://www.reforge.com/guides/define-your-setup-moment — Three sequential moments: setup (user has done what is needed to experience the core value), aha (first experience of core value), habit (core action repeated within an initial period; Pinterest-style example "pinned 4 days out of the first 28 days"); "Setup is not activation." [practitioner][snippet-only; page body did not render]
6. Retana, Forman & Wu (2016), *Manufacturing & Service Operations Management* 18(1):34–50. Cloud provider field experiment: early guidance halved first-week churn, +46.57% usage over eight months. Already recorded in research/small-bets-direct.md §10. [research][read 2026-10-05; abstract] The closest causal evidence that helping new users reach first value raises retention.
7. Datta, Foubert & Van Heerde (2015), "The challenge of retaining customers acquired with free trials," *Journal of Marketing Research* 52(2):217–234, doi:10.1509/jmr.12.0160. Press release: https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/694422 — Household data from a large European digital TV provider: free-trial customers stayed about one third as long as regular customers, and usage intensity mattered more for their retention ("particularly strong" for trial-acquired customers). [research][read 2026-10-05; press release only; Tilburg repository PDF returned 403] Observational, one firm.
8. Halfaker, Geiger, Morgan & Riedl (2013), "The Rise and Decline of an Open Collaboration System," *American Behavioral Scientist* 57(5):664–688. https://stuartgeiger.com/abs-halfaker-rise-decline.pdf — Logistic regression on Wikipedia newcomers; "survival" = an edit 2–6 months after the first session. Newcomers reverted or deleted in their first session survived less; the share of good-faith newcomers reverted in their first session rose from 6.1% to 18.2% between 2006 and 2007 as survival fell. [research][read 2026-10-05; method and results sections] Observational; shows a bad first session predicts loss of new users.
9. Amplitude, "The 7% Retention Rule Explained," based on its 2025 Product Benchmark Report (over 2,600 companies). https://amplitude.com/blog/7-percent-retention-rule — "69% of top day seven performers were also top three-month performers"; for half of products "over 98% of new users are no longer active at the two-week mark"; 7% of a cohort returning on day 7 puts a product in the top 25%. [vendor; Amplitude customers only, product-level correlation, definitions vary][read 2026-10-05]
10. Zhang & Duan (2025), "Longer or shorter? A large-scale randomized field experiment on the impact of free trial duration…," *Frontiers in Psychology*. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12217587/ — Image-editing SaaS, 680,588 new users in about 190 countries, July 2022–July 2024; 7-day vs 3-day trial raised overall conversion 20.92% (delayed conversion +42.36%; immediate conversion not significant). Users who completed more tasks during the trial converted less immediately but more later: heavy trial use can satisfy the need. [research; RCT, n=680,588; one firm][read 2026-10-05]

## 3. Privacy-friendly and self-hosted tools

11. Plausible, "Data policy." https://plausible.io/data-policy — Daily visitor ID = hash(daily salt + domain + IP + user agent); salt deleted every 24 hours; raw IP and user agent never stored; "We do not generate persistent identifiers"; cannot recognise a visitor across days, devices or sites. Claims no cookie banner needed. [first-party; vendor][read 2026-10-05] Consequence: no multi-day user retention or cohorts of individuals.
12. Umami, FAQ. https://docs.umami.is/docs/faq — "Umami does not use any cookies in the tracking code"; anonymous session identifier; a person on a new device or browser is a new visitor unless you set a distinct ID for logged-in users; offers funnels, goals and session replays. [first-party; vendor][read 2026-10-05]
13. Matomo FAQ 157 (cookieless). https://matomo.org/faq/general/faq_157/ — `disableCookies` before `trackPageView`; disabling cookies does not stop JavaScript tracking; you lose reliable new vs returning visitor data; may still set security cookies. "Always check with your legal or privacy advisors." [first-party; vendor][read 2026-10-05]
14. PostHog, "Self-host." https://posthog.com/docs/self-host — Free Docker Compose "hobby" deployment under MIT licence; not recommended above roughly 300k events, 1k recordings or 300k flag calls per month; Cloud suggested above 1M events or 5k recordings; "We don't offer customer support" for self-hosted; only free-plan features; "no sort of guarantees", risk of data loss. [first-party; vendor][read 2026-10-05]
15. PostHog, "Session replay privacy controls." https://posthog.com/docs/session-replay/privacy — All inputs masked by default on web; general text is not masked by default; mobile SDKs mask text, inputs and images by default; masking happens in the browser so masked data is never sent. [first-party; vendor][read 2026-10-05]
16. Englehardt, Acar & Narayanan, "No boundaries: Exfiltration of personal data by session-replay scripts," Princeton CITP blog, 15 November 2017. https://blog.citp.princeton.edu/2017/11/15/no-boundaries-exfiltration-of-personal-data-by-session-replay-scripts — Seven replay vendors on 482 of the Alexa top 50,000 sites; recordings captured sensitive data including card details, passwords and medical information. ## 4. Consent for analytics (EU, France)

17. EDPB, Guidelines 2/2023 on Technical Scope of Art. 5(3) of ePrivacy Directive, adopted 14 November 2023 (consultation version), final version 2.0 adopted 16 October 2024. https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2023-11/edpb_guidelines_202302_technical_scope_art_53_eprivacydirective_en.pdf ; landing page https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/guidelines/guidelines-22023-technical-scope-art-53-eprivacy-directive_en — Para 50–51: tracking pixels and tracked URLs fall under Art. 5(3); with dynamically built pixels, distributing the JavaScript is the "instruction" and collecting identifiers is "gaining of access". Para 53: if client-side code sends locally produced information back to a server, that is "gaining of access to information already stored". Para 54–55: IP-only tracking can fall under Art. 5(3) unless the entity can ensure the IP does not originate from the user's equipment. [first-party][read 2026-10-05 the November 2023 version; version 2.0 text not read, only its landing page]
18. CNIL, "Sheet n°16: Use analytics on your websites and applications," 11 June 2020. https://www.cnil.fr/en/sheet-ndeg16-use-analytics-your-websites-and-applications — Analytics trackers can be exempt from consent if limited to audience measurement and A/B testing, tracker lifetime ≤ 13 months, last byte of IP truncated, no cross-checking with other processing, scope limited to one site or app publisher, users informed and able to object; "most large audience measurement offerings do not fall within the scope of the exemption". [first-party][read 2026-10-05]
19. CNIL, "Cookies : solutions pour les outils de mesure d'audience" and self-assessment tool (July 2025 PDF). https://www.cnil.fr/fr/cookies-solutions-pour-les-outils-de-mesure-daudience ; https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2025-07/outil_d_auto-evaluation_mesure_d_audience.pdf — Raw data retention ≤ 25 months; self-assessment replaces the earlier list of tools and is not a certification. Secondary sources list Matomo, Piano, Wysistat, Plausible and self-hosted Umami among configurable tools; Google Analytics excluded. [first-party for the conditions; secondary for the tool list][snippet-only]

## 6. Small numbers, qualitative backups, vanity metrics

32. Brown, Cai & DasGupta (2001), "Interval Estimation for a Binomial Proportion," *Statistical Science* 16(2):101–133. https://projecteuclid.org/journals/statistical-science/volume-16/issue-2/Interval-Estimation-for-a-Binomial-Proportion/10.1214/ss/1009213286.full — The textbook (Wald) interval has erratic coverage, worse than commonly believed; recommend Wilson or Jeffreys intervals for small n. [research][read 2026-10-05; abstract]
33. Hanley & Lippman-Hand (1983), "If nothing goes wrong, is everything all right? Interpreting zero numerators," *JAMA* 249(13):1743–1745. — Rule of three: with 0 events in n trials, the 95% upper bound is about 3/n. [research][snippet-only]
34. Own calculation (Wilson 95% interval, z = 1.96), 2026-10-05: 3/10 activated → 11–60%; 6/20 → 15–52%; 12/40 → 18–45%; 30/100 → 22–40%; 300/1000 → 27–33%; 0/20 → 0–16%. [own calculation]
35. Jakob Nielsen, "Why You Only Need to Test with 5 Users," NN/g, 18 March 2000. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/why-you-only-need-to-test-with-5-users/ — From Nielsen & Landauer (1993), average problem discovery rate 31% per user; five users find about 85% of usability problems; run several small rounds; quantitative studies need about 20 users; distinct user groups each need their own testers. [practitioner; based on 1993 model][read 2026-10-05]
36. Eric Ries, "Why vanity metrics are dangerous," Startup Lessons Learned, 23 December 2009. http://www.startuplessonslearned.com/2009/12/why-vanity-metrics-are-dangerous.html — "Hits" count "a technical process, not a number of human beings"; vanity metrics have no clear cause; use cohorts, split tests and per-customer metrics instead. [practitioner][read 2026-10-05]

## What this means

- **Start with a handful of server-side events.** All three vendors converge on few events with properties, one naming convention and no variable values in names [1–3]. Segment's own first plan had three events [1]. For a product with under 100 users, signup, the setup steps, the first value event, a repeat of it, and payment answer nearly every question; a vendor's interest is in more events.
- **Pick one naming convention and write it down;** the specific choice matters less than consistency [1–3]. Server-side events avoid ad blockers and consent-gated scripts for the core funnel [2], though they do not remove GDPR obligations.
- **Activation has decent evidence of a link to retention, mostly correlational.** A field experiment shows early help cuts first-week churn [6]; observational work shows usage predicts retention, especially for trial customers [7], and a bad first session predicts losing newcomers [8]; vendor data shows early activation and three-month retention move together across products [9]. Activity is not the same as conversion: in one RCT, heavy trial use delayed purchase [10]. Test causality before managing to a metric [4].
- **Setup-heavy products need a separate setup milestone** (database connected, CI installed, teammate invited) before the value milestone [5]; measure the drop between them.
- **Privacy-friendly tools trade away user-level analysis.** Plausible cannot follow a person across days [11]; Umami and cookieless Matomo cannot reliably tell returning visitors without an ID [12–13]; self-hosted PostHog has no support, free-plan features only and a low volume ceiling [14]. For activation and retention of logged-in users you need user-level events, which usually come from your own backend.
- **Consent:** the EDPB reads Art. 5(3) to cover pixels, client-side code that sends data back, and in some cases IP-only tracking [17]. Moving the tag to a server does not remove consent if browser code still reads and sends device data. France exempts narrow first-party audience measurement under strict settings [18–19]; the UK has its own analytics exception (research/privacy-and-marketing-law.md).
- **At n < 100, percentages are wide ranges.** 3 of 10 activated means anything from about 11% to 60% [32, 34]. Count people, name them, and use replays [15] and founder emails (research/small-bets-direct.md) to learn why.
- **CLI telemetry defaults:** large JavaScript and platform tools (Next.js, Gatsby, Homebrew, .NET, Turborepo) ship opt-out telemetry with a first-run notice and a tool-specific environment variable [20–21, 24–26]. Go chose opt-in after public debate and got a small, useful sample [27–28]. Backlash cases share features: third-party analytics services, little notice, and timing near an ownership or terms change [22, 29, 30]. DO_NOT_TRACK is a proposed convention honoured by some tools (Turborepo) and declined or ignored by others (Homebrew, Next.js) [23, 20, 26, 31].

## Folklore and weak claims

- "Track everything with autocapture and decide later": PostHog's own docs warn this leaves out the signup event needed for activation [2]. Autocaptured clicks are hard to interpret and change when the UI changes. [first-party; vendor]
- "Server-side tracking means you don't need consent": not supported by the EDPB reading in [17]; depends on what the browser is instructed to send.
- "Cookieless means GDPR-free": the cookie rule (ePrivacy) and GDPR are separate; CNIL's exemption has specific conditions [18]; hashing IPs still processes personal data at collection time. Vendor "no banner needed" claims [11–12] are the vendor's reading.
- "Famous activation thresholds" (for example Slack's 2,000 messages): company self-reports, not rules (research/b2b-saas-models.md §18).
- "Five users find 85% of problems" applies to qualitative usability testing with one user group, not to measuring rates [35].
- "Anonymous telemetry is harmless, so opt-out is fine": developers objected even to anonymous designs [22, 27]; Go's own author expected few would opt out if it were opt-in by default [27].

## Open questions

- No peer-reviewed study found that compares activation definitions across B2B SaaS or developer tools with a common definition; benchmarks remain vendor or survey data.
- EDPB Guidelines 2/2023 version 2.0 (October 2024) text was not read; only the November 2023 version. Whether final wording changed for IP-only tracking is unchecked.
- Whether purely server-side product events derived from requests the user makes to use the service need ePrivacy consent is not settled in the sources read; GDPR still needs a lawful basis (often legitimate interest). Ask counsel.
- Share of traffic or users lost to ad blockers for client-side analytics: no independent figure found.
- Opt-in telemetry participation rates outside Go: not found.
- CNIL's current list of compliant tools was read only through snippets; the self-assessment tool is not a certification.
