## Sources

### Marketplaces
1. Gurley, B. (2012). "All Markets Are Not Created Equal: 10 Factors To Consider When Evaluating Digital Marketplaces." Above the Crowd, 13 Nov 2012. https://abovethecrowd.com/2012/11/13/all-markets-are-not-created-equal-10-factors-to-consider-when-evaluating-digital-marketplaces/ — The 10 factors: (1) new experience vs status quo, (2) economic advantages vs status quo, (3) opportunity for technology to add value, (4) high fragmentation, (5) friction of supplier sign-up, (6) size of the market opportunity, (7) expand the market, (8) frequency, (9) payment flow, (10) network effects. Frequency: infrequent purchase cycles make brand awareness and word of mouth hard. Payment flow: being in the payment flow lets the marketplace set commission terms. [practitioner, read 2026-10-05]
2. Gurley, B. (2013). "A Rake Too Far: Optimal Platform Pricing Strategy." Above the Crowd, 18 Apr 2013. https://abovethecrowd.com/2013/04/18/a-rake-too-far-optimal-platformpricing-strategy/ — "the most dangerous strategy for any platform company is to price too high"; "High rakes are a form of friction"; high volume with a modest rake is the formula for a durable marketplace. Examples cited in summaries: take rates vary from roughly 1.9% (OpenTable) to 70% (Shutterstock); oDesk cut its rate from 30% to 10%. [practitioner, read 2026-10-05; OpenTable 1.9% and Shutterstock 70% are from the essay's rake table (Gurley's own estimates as % of GMS), oDesk 30%→10% from the text]

Includes the Airbnb "300 listings, 100 with reviews" tipping point attributed to Nate Blecharczyk. [practitioner, read 2026-10-05 (book excerpt on Lenny's Newsletter https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/atomic-network for the atomic-network definition; the 300/100 figures confirmed in Golden, item 4)]

7. Rachitsky, L. (2019). Marketplace growth series (Twitter threads and newsletter, interviews with early operators of ~17 marketplaces). https://x.com/lennysan/status/1197907129388027904 ; https://x.com/lennysan/status/1167088120132562945 — every marketplace starts supply-constrained; most of the biggest stay supply-constrained (Uber, Lyft); a few become demand-constrained when supply gets easy, accessible income (Rover, TaskRabbit). Roughly 80% (14 of 17) focused on supply first. Top early demand lever was word of mouth; top early supply lever was direct sales/outreach. [practitioner, read 2026-10-05 via https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-kickstart-and-scale-a-marketplace-9ee and Thread Reader copies; word of mouth was cited by 50% of companies for early demand, direct sales was the most common early supply lever; small, self-selected sample of successes]
8. Gu, G., Zhu, F. (2021). "Trust and Disintermediation: Evidence from an Online Freelance Marketplace." Management Science 67(2):794–807. https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2020.3583 — randomized trial on a freelance platform: features that increase trust help high-quality freelancers get hired, but once trust is high enough it also raises disintermediation (the two sides take later deals off-platform to avoid fees), offsetting the revenue gain. Tendency to disintermediate differs across clients and freelancers. [research, read 2026-10-05 (abstract)]
9. Hagiu, A., Wright, J. (2024). "Marketplace Leakage." Management Science 70(3):1529–1553. https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2023.4757 — models partial leakage (meeting on the platform, transacting off it) and compares counter-strategies: invest in transaction benefits, limit communication, charge referral (lead) fees instead of transaction fees, price-parity clauses, add seller competition on the platform, hide sellers who push leakage. [research (theory), read 2026-10-05 (abstract)]
10. Tadelis, S. (2016). "Reputation and Feedback Systems in Online Platform Markets." [research, read 2026-10-05 (abstract: trust via reputation systems, and bias in feedback); inflation detail from prior knowledge, not re-verified]

### SMS consent (US) — legal context, not legal advice

The court said consent must be "clear and unmistakable"; existing prior express written consent requirements for marketing texts using autodialers are unchanged. Opinion: https://media.ca11.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/files/202410277.pdf (vacated Part III.D of the 2023 Order) [verified-search: uscourts.gov, fcc.gov, 2026-10-04]. ## Derived rules used in the playbooks
- Contribution margin per order (after COGS, shipping, fulfilment, payment fees, discounts and expected returns) is the input to break-even ROAS/CPA; using gross margin overstates affordable CAC. [derived from paid-acquisition math]
- Leakage risk is highest where the transaction is large, repeated between the same pair, and trust is high once established (Gu & Zhu; Hagiu & Wright). [derived]
- State laws (e.g. Florida, Oklahoma "mini-TCPA" statutes, and state quiet-hours rules) add requirements; not researched this session. [not re-verified]
