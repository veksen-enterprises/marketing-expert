## Sources

### Marketplaces
1. Gurley, B. (2012). "All Markets Are Not Created Equal: 10 Factors To Consider When Evaluating Digital Marketplaces." Above the Crowd, 13 Nov 2012. https://abovethecrowd.com/2012/11/13/all-markets-are-not-created-equal-10-factors-to-consider-when-evaluating-digital-marketplaces/ — The 10 factors: (1) new experience vs status quo, (2) economic advantages vs status quo, (3) opportunity for technology to add value, (4) high fragmentation, (5) friction of supplier sign-up, (6) size of the market opportunity, (7) expand the market, (8) frequency, (9) payment flow, (10) network effects. Frequency: infrequent purchase cycles make brand awareness and word of mouth hard. Payment flow: being in the payment flow lets the marketplace set commission terms. [practitioner, snippet-only; factor list confirmed via two secondary summaries]
2. Gurley, B. (2013). "A Rake Too Far: Optimal Platform Pricing Strategy." Above the Crowd, 18 Apr 2013. https://abovethecrowd.com/2013/04/18/a-rake-too-far-optimal-platformpricing-strategy/ — "the most dangerous strategy for any platform company is to price too high"; "High rakes are a form of friction"; high volume with a modest rake is the formula for a durable marketplace. Examples cited in summaries: take rates vary from roughly 1.9% (OpenTable) to 70% (Shutterstock); oDesk cut its rate from 30% to 10%. [practitioner, snippet-only; confirm the example numbers in the original]
3. Chen, A. (2021). *The Cold Start Problem*. Harper Business. — "Atomic network": the smallest stable, self-sustaining network (a city, a campus, a team); build one, then adjacent ones. Includes the Airbnb "300 listings, 100 with reviews" tipping point attributed to Nate Blecharczyk. [practitioner, snippet-only via Lenny's Newsletter summary https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/atomic-network and book summaries]
4. Golden, J. "Lessons Learned Scaling Airbnb 100X." Medium. https://medium.com/@jgolden/lessons-learned-scaling-airbnb-100x-b862364fb3a7 — bookings growth showed a step change at about 300 listings in a city (observed in New York, Paris and others), when guests had enough choice for their dates and taste. [practitioner, snippet-only; single company's internal observation]
5. Jordan, J., Jin, L., Coolican, D., Chen, A. (2020). "13 Metrics for Marketplace Companies." a16z, 21 Feb 2020. https://a16z.com/13-metrics-for-marketplace-companies/ — marketplaces exist to match supply and demand, so measure the match rate (how often buyers find sellers and vice versa); also covers power-user curves (L7/L30 histograms) and cohort behaviour. [practitioner/investor, snippet-only; full metric list not confirmed this session]
6. a16z, "The Marketplace 100" (2020–2023 editions). https://a16z.com/marketplace-100/ — ranks private consumer marketplaces by GMV (2023 data from Consumer Edge). No median take rate figure could be confirmed. [vendor/investor, snippet-only]
7. Rachitsky, L. (2019). Marketplace growth series (Twitter threads and newsletter, interviews with early operators of ~17 marketplaces). https://x.com/lennysan/status/1197907129388027904 ; https://x.com/lennysan/status/1167088120132562945 — every marketplace starts supply-constrained; most of the biggest stay supply-constrained (Uber, Lyft); a few become demand-constrained when supply gets easy, accessible income (Rover, TaskRabbit). Roughly 80% (14 of 17) focused on supply first. Top early demand lever was word of mouth; top early supply lever was direct sales/outreach. [practitioner, snippet-only; small, self-selected sample of successes]
8. Gu, G., Zhu, F. (2021). "Trust and Disintermediation: Evidence from an Online Freelance Marketplace." Management Science 67(2):794–807. https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2020.3583 — randomized trial on a freelance platform: features that increase trust help high-quality freelancers get hired, but more trust also raises the risk that the two sides take later deals off-platform to avoid fees. Tendency to disintermediate differs across clients and freelancers. [research, snippet-only]
9. Hagiu, A., Wright, J. (2024). "Marketplace Leakage." Management Science 70(3):1529–1553. https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2023.4757 — models partial leakage (meeting on the platform, transacting off it) and compares counter-strategies: invest in transaction benefits, limit communication, charge referral (lead) fees instead of transaction fees, price-parity clauses, add seller competition on the platform, hide sellers who push leakage. [research (theory), snippet-only]
10. Tadelis, S. (2016). "Reputation and Feedback Systems in Online Platform Markets." Annual Review of Economics 8:321–340. https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-economics-080315-015325 — reviews the evidence that reputation systems create trust between strangers, and the biases in feedback (ratings inflated and mostly positive; dissatisfied buyers often stay silent). [research, snippet-only for the abstract; inflation detail from prior knowledge, not re-verified]
11. Zhu, F., Iansiti, M. (2019). "Why Some Platforms Thrive and Others Don't." HBR. — network effects local vs global; multi-homing. Already recorded in research/competition-and-defensibility.md. [research summary]

Popularised by CTC for DTC. e.g. https://www.northbeam.io/blog/marketing-efficiency-ratio-mer-roas [practitioner/vendor, snippet-only]
13. NRF & Happy Returns (2024). [vendor (Happy Returns is a UPS returns business) + trade association survey, snippet-only]
14. NRF (2025). "Consumers Expected to Return Nearly $850 Billion in Merchandise in 2025." https://nrf.com/media-center/press-releases/consumers-expected-to-return-nearly-850-billion-in-merchandise-in-2025 — 15.8% of 2025 sales expected returned; online ~19.3%; 9% of returns estimated fraudulent; 82% of consumers say free returns are a major purchase consideration. [vendor + trade association survey, snippet-only; retailer estimates, not audited]
15. Janakiraman, N., Syrdal, H., Freling, R. (2016). "The Effect of Return Policy Leniency on Consumer Purchase and Return Decisions: A Meta-analytic Review." Journal of Retailing 92(2):226–235. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jretai.2015.11.002 — leniency increases purchases overall. Five dimensions (money, time, effort, scope, exchange). Longer deadlines barely change purchase but *lower* return likelihood; lower effort raises purchase without raising returns. [research, meta-analysis, snippet-only]
16. Lewis, M., Singh, V., Fay, S. (2006). "An Empirical Study of the Impact of Nonlinear Shipping and Handling Fees on Purchase Incidence and Expenditure Decisions." Marketing Science 25(1):51–64. https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/mksc.1050.0150 — one online retailer varied shipping-fee schedules: shoppers are very sensitive to shipping fees; free shipping and free-over-threshold offers raised sales, but lost shipping revenue and non-responsive segments made them unprofitable for that retailer. [research, single retailer, snippet-only]
17. Spiegel Research Center, Northwestern (2017). "How Online Reviews Influence Sales." https://spiegel.medill.northwestern.edu/how-online-reviews-influence-sales/ — purchase likelihood with five reviews ~270% higher than with none; uplift larger for higher-priced items (380% vs 190%); diminishing returns after the first few reviews. Purchase likelihood reported to peak at ratings of about 4.0–4.7, not 5.0 [this detail not re-verified]. Data partly from PowerReviews. [research-centre analysis with vendor data, snippet-only]
18. FTC (2024). Trade Regulation Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials (16 CFR Part 465). Issued 14 Aug 2024, effective 21 Oct 2024. Q&A: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers — bans fake reviews and testimonials, buying positive or negative reviews (incentives conditioned on sentiment), and suppressing negative reviews (with limited exceptions); civil penalties for knowing violations (reported as up to $51,744 per violation in 2024; adjusted yearly for inflation). [first-party + legal summaries (WSGR, Morgan Lewis), snippet-only]

### SMS consent (US) — legal context, not legal advice
21. Eleventh Circuit, *Insurance Marketing Coalition v. FCC* (24 Jan 2025) — vacated the FCC's 2023 "one-to-one consent" rule before it took effect (27 Jan 2025). The court said consent must be "clear and unmistakable"; existing prior express written consent requirements for marketing texts using autodialers are unchanged. Summaries: https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/eleventh-circuit-vacates-tcpa-11-consent-rule ; https://www.wiley.law/alert-UPDATE-11th-Circuit-Vacates-FCCs-One-to-One-TCPA-Consent-Rule [legal, snippet-only]
22. FCC TCPA consent-revocation rules (effective 11 Apr 2025; part delayed by a one-year waiver) — consumers may revoke consent by any reasonable means; keywords such as stop, quit, revoke, opt out, cancel, unsubscribe, end must be honoured; process within 10 business days. Summaries: https://www.nixonpeabody.com/insights/alerts/2025/04/11/fcc-partially-delays-new-tcpa-consent-revocation-rules ; https://www.privacyworld.blog/2025/10/fcc-proposes-amendments-to-tcpa-consent-revocation-rules-proposed-changes-to-dnc-rules-deleted-from-final-text/ [legal, snippet-only; the FCC proposed further amendments in Oct 2025, status not checked]
23. CTIA (2023). "Messaging Principles and Best Practices." https://api.ctia.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/230523-CTIA-Messaging-Principles-and-Best-Practices-FINAL.pdf — carrier-industry rules: clear call to action at opt-in (who, what, frequency, "msg & data rates may apply"), confirmation message, opt-out in recurring programs, consent not transferable; 10DLC brand and campaign registration for business texting on standard numbers. [industry self-regulation, snippet-only]

## Derived rules used in the playbooks
- Contribution margin per order (after COGS, shipping, fulfilment, payment fees, discounts and expected returns) is the input to break-even ROAS/CPA; using gross margin overstates affordable CAC. [derived from paid-acquisition math]
- Leakage risk is highest where the transaction is large, repeated between the same pair, and trust is high once established (Gu & Zhu; Hagiu & Wright). [derived]
- State laws (e.g. Florida, Oklahoma "mini-TCPA" statutes, and state quiet-hours rules) add requirements; not researched this session. [not re-verified]
