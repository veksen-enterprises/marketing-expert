# Can a new dating app get approved to advertise on Google, Meta and TikTok? (checked 2026-10-27)

Question: `research/dating-ad-and-store-rules.md` found that Google, Meta and TikTok gate dating ads. Can an app that hasn't launched, or has few users, get approved? What does each application ask for: a live product, privacy policy, age checks, safety measures, business verification, users or a track record?

## Findings

### Google

1. **The form asks for no user count, revenue, launch date or account history.** Its fields are names, emails, a Google Ads customer ID, domains or app IDs, test log-ins, a screen recording, a service description and category boxes. The summary says no field asks about user counts or launch status. [first-party, 1, read; fetch summary; form fields can change without notice]
2. **But it needs a working product a reviewer can enter.** Only "Google Play apps, Apple App Store apps, and websites" are accepted. The help page asks for "Working test credentials or any other details to enable access to your service". The required screen recording must show the home screen, the login screen and "the post-login experience", with subscription plans if any. A waitlist page has no post-login experience to record. [first-party, 1, 2, read; fetch summary] So in practice the app must be built and installable from a store, or live on the web, before applying. [derived from 1, 2]
3. **Safety and age rules are promises, not proofs.** The form asks the company to confirm that the service does not "allow users under age 18" and does not "Contain synthetically generated dating chatbots or profiles without sufficient disclosures". It must also "Ask all users upon sign-up to your service to comply with guidelines that safeguard the community". Declining any box makes the applicant ineligible. No field asks how age is checked or for a privacy policy, though the form requires compliance with "privacy laws". [first-party, 1, read; fetch summary]
4. **A refusal costs 30 days.** Per the summary, once a form is submitted the same customer ID cannot apply again for 30 days. [first-party, 1, read; fetch summary only; this number was not seen on the page itself]
5. **No review time is published.** The form says only that it will be processed "as soon as possible". Neither help page gives a time. [first-party, 1, 2, 3, read; fetch summary]
6. **Advertiser verification is a separate gate, not named for dating.** Google says "all advertisers will eventually be required to complete advertiser verification". It is triggered early for some industries (financial services is the example), suspicious accounts and big account changes. Dating was not named in the summary. [first-party, 4, read; fetch summary]

### Meta and TikTok

7. **Meta: no application process could be read.** The earlier note's finding stands; how to ask and what is reviewed are unknown. [not read; see "Not fetched"]
8. **TikTok: a sales representative is the only stated step.** The adult content page lists no form, documents or eligibility rule. It requires 18+ audiences and a landing page that is "a Google Play, Apple App Store, or a website landing page". [first-party, 5, read; fetch summary] The North America handbook has no dating text. [first-party, 6, read; fetch summary]

### Agreement with the earlier note

9. This agrees with `dating-ad-and-store-rules.md` items 1, 4 and 5. Neither "after launch" nor "open to a new app with a site and policies" is quite right: Google's gate is a built app in a store or a live web product with log-ins, not users. [derived from 1, 2]

## What this changes

1. **The proposed `knowledge/paid-acquisition.md` "Channel notes" line.** Replace "Apply before you plan a paid test." with: "Dating apps: Google needs certification, Meta prior written permission and TikTok a sales rep before any ad runs; all limit ads to 18+. Google asks for no user count, but wants a live store app or site, test log-ins and a screen recording of the signed-in app, and a refused application waits 30 days. No platform publishes an approval time, so apply as soon as the build is live, not from a waitlist page. [first-party; checked 2026-10-27]". No test checks this line's facts.
2. **Small bets.** A pre-launch dating app can plan a paid test only after the app is in a store, with time left for an unknown approval. No paid-ads bet exists in the catalog, so nothing else changes.

## What this does not settle

- Whether Google accepts a TestFlight or closed-testing build; the form names only public store apps and websites.
- Meta's process; whether TikTok reps take small advertisers.
- Whether dating triggers Google's advertiser verification early.
- The 30-day wait, seen only in a fetch summary.
