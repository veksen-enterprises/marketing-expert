# Do dormant ties (people you lost touch with) give more useful help than current ties when you reconnect? (checked 2026-10-28)

Question: the `warm-network` bet tells a solo practitioner to write to "former colleagues, classmates, past employers and friends". The `build-a-network` bet says "stay in touch every few months". Does reconnecting with people you have not spoken to in years bring help as useful as asking people you talk to now? If so, should `warm-network` name them, and does it change the advice to stay in touch?

## 1. Executives who reconnected rated the advice as useful as, or more useful than, current contacts'

- Design: 224 executives in four Executive MBA classes (three US, one Canada); 129 answered both surveys. As a course task, each asked two people they had not talked to for at least three years (one once close, one once distant) for advice on a real work project. The comparison was two current contacts picked at random from those they had already asked about the same project. Usefulness was a six-item self-rating, 1–7, given about a month later. [research, 1, read; fetch summary; EMBA executives, n=129; self-reported]
- Result: once-close dormant ties scored about the same as current close ties in raw means (5.70 vs 5.63, not significantly different) and higher with controls (+0.27, p ≈ .02). Once-distant dormant ties scored higher than current distant ties (5.59 vs 5.20). No dormant group scored lower. [research, 1, read; fetch summary; self-reported; figures reconstructed by the tool]
- Why, as the authors measured it: dormant ties gave more novel advice and took less time. Trust from once-close ties had decayed a little but stayed high; shared outlook had not detectably decayed. [research, 1, read; fetch summary; novelty and trust are single-item self-ratings]
- The executives chose which dormant ties to contact; the current ties were picked at random. The authors say so, and say they did not check the self-reports independently. [research, 1, read; fetch summary; authors' own limitations]

## 2. The pool is deep, and people pick the wrong ones

- In a second sample (116 executives, 95 answered), each ranked 10 dormant contacts, then reached their first choice and one drawn at random from the other nine. Rank did not predict usefulness (r = −.07, n.s.). [research, 1, read; fetch summary; same subject pools; self-reported]
- In a later sample (156 executives, 117 answered), value was higher from contacts of higher status, with more trust and willingness to help, and with whom they had talked less often before. People preferred contacts they had known longer and talked to more: the familiar, not the most useful. 94% got at least some valuable advice; 90% enjoyed it. [corrected 2026-10-28: the authors' summary (source 4) says 94% of reconnections, not of executives, gave at least some valuable advice; 90% enjoyed reconnecting] No current ties were compared. [research, 3, read; fetch summary; EMBA executives, n=117; self-reported] [practitioner, 4; authors' summary]

## 3. Reconnecting can fail

- A qualitative study in an Italian textile district and a vignette test with US workers found that a reconnection works when both sides remember the past tie, catch up on what each has done, and see the tie the same way (how close, who ranks higher, whether they compete). Missing steps led to polite contact with little help, sometimes a rupture. [research, 5, abstract-only; fetch summary; qualitative plus vignettes; sizes not seen]
- Firms in European motorsport reactivated latent ties on purpose to escape overloaded, redundant networks. No outcome measure was seen. [research, 6, abstract-only; one industry]

## 5. Agreement with `solo-first-clients.md`

- Agrees: that note (§6) says ties beyond people who could buy are worth having, and moderately weak ties passed on the most jobs. Here, once-distant dormant ties gave the most novel advice. Both point to people outside your current circle.
- Agrees: that note's list for a person with no clients is "former colleagues, employers, friends". Most of those are dormant for someone who changed jobs years ago; this note says their being out of touch is not a reason to skip them.
- Differs in kind, not direction: §6 is a randomised test with job moves as outcome; this is a course task with self-rated advice. It is weaker evidence and about a different outcome.

## What this does not settle

- Whether reconnecting brings a solo practitioner introductions or paid work.
- Whether the result holds outside executives, or when the person reconnecting is asking for business rather than advice.
- Whether once-distant dormant ties beat once-close ones for introductions; advice scores were close (5.59 vs 5.70).
- How often to stay in touch: no study here tests a rhythm.
