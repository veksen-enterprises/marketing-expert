8. **Objective-and-task vs percentage-of-sales methods**: standard marketing textbook material (e.g. Saylor Academy BUS602 "Budgeting Methods"; marketingstudyguide.com). [textbook, practitioner]
   - Objective-and-task: set objectives, cost the tasks needed to reach them, sum. Seen as the most logical but the most work.
   - Percentage of sales: simple and easy to defend, but treats marketing as a cost that follows sales rather than an investment that causes them; it cuts spend when sales fall (when more might be needed) and ignores competitors and strategy changes.

13. **Practitioner and vendor blogs on the first marketing hire** (MarketerHire, "Your Startup's First Marketing Hire"; Founders Connect, "When to Hire Generalists vs. Specialists"). [vendor / practitioner, snippet-only]
    - Common advice: first hire is usually a generalist who can run several channels; pick to match the current bottleneck (e.g. post-PMF B2B with no inbound pipeline → demand gen; pre-revenue consumer → content/organic); hire specialists when one function is both a main growth driver and a recurring problem; a mid-level "player" who has owned a channel at a startup often fits better than a senior manager. MarketerHire sells freelance marketers, so its advice has a commercial angle.

## What the evidence supports

- Average budget as % of revenue is ~8–9% across large firms in two surveys, but varies widely by sector (B2C product much higher than B2B product) and the distribution is skewed (Gartner median ≤ 6%). [analyst / academic survey]
- Private SaaS spends far less on marketing than "30–50% S&M" folklore suggests at the median (SaaS Capital: ~8% marketing, ~23% S&M of ARR; KeyBanc 2024: median S&M 31% of revenue), while public growth SaaS spends far more (~39% S&M). The range is the point: stage, funding and motion dominate. [vendor]
- Nothing found shows that matching an industry %-of-revenue average causes better outcomes. Benchmarks are descriptive. [derived]
