**Access caveat.** The egress proxy blocked every primary host tried (gartner.com, cmosurvey.org, key.com, saas-capital.com). All figures below come from search-engine summaries of the named pages. ## Sources

1. **Gartner, "Gartner 2025 CMO Spend Survey Reveals Marketing Budgets Have Flatlined at 7.7% of Overall Company Revenue"**, press release, 12 May 2025. https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-05-12-gartner-2025-cmo-spend-survey-reveals-marketing-budgets-have-flatlined-at-seven-percent-of-overall-company-revenue (also on Business Wire). [analyst, snippet-only]
   - Marketing budgets 7.7% of company revenue in 2025, the same as 2024.
   - Half of CMOs reported budgets of 6% of revenue or less (so the mean is pulled up by high spenders).
   - 59% of CMOs said they had insufficient budget to execute their 2025 strategy (down 5 points from 2024).
   - Sample: 402 CMOs and marketing leaders, North America, UK and Europe, fielded Feb–Mar 2025; the vast majority with annual revenue over US$1 billion.
   - Paid media 30.6% of the marketing budget, the largest line.
2. **Trade press on the same Gartner survey** (Chief Marketer, "Gartner: 39% of CMOs Plan to Reduce Labor Costs and Cut Agency Allocations"; Campaign; Marketing Brew, May 2025). [analyst via trade press, not re-verified]
   - Remaining split reported as roughly martech 22%, internal labour 22%, agencies 21%.
   - 39% of CMOs planned to cut agency budgets and 39% to reduce labour spending.
3. **The CMO Survey (Duke Fuqua / Deloitte / AMA), Spring 2025 edition**; Topline and Highlights & Insights reports, https://cmosurvey.org/cmosurvey_results/The_CMO_Survey-Highlights_and_Insights_Report-2025.pdf ; Duke Fuqua Insights, "The CMO Survey: Despite Uncertainty, Marketing Budgets Rebound". [academic survey, snippet-only]
   - Marketing budgets 9.4% of company revenue and 11.4% of total company budget, up from 7.7% and 10.1% in Fall 2024.
   - n = 281 US marketing leaders, fielded 21 Jan–12 Feb 2025.
   - By sector (secondary relay): B2C product companies 15.5% of revenue vs B2B product 6.4%. [not re-verified]
   - Sample covers a broader mix of company sizes than Gartner, which is one explanation offered for 9.4% vs 7.7%. (That explanation is from secondary blogs, not the survey authors.) [not re-verified]
4. **SaaS Capital, "2026 Spending Benchmarks for Private B2B SaaS Companies"**. https://www.saas-capital.com/blog-posts/spending-benchmarks-for-private-b2b-saas-companies/ [vendor, snippet-only]
   - 15th annual survey, Q1 2026, more than 1,000 private SaaS companies responding.
   - Median marketing spend 8% of ARR (unchanged from the prior year); median selling costs 13% of ARR (up from 10.5%). So median sales + marketing is about 21% of ARR.
   - Secondary relays say equity-backed companies spend roughly twice as much on marketing as bootstrapped ones (about 12% vs 6% of ARR for marketing). [not re-verified]

   - A 2022 survey relay gives median S&M at 31% of revenue. [not re-verified]

7. **Blossom Street Ventures, "2024 SaaS Operating Margins and Spend Metrics"** (Medium). [vendor, snippet-only, not re-verified]
   - Public SaaS companies (IPO since 2017): S&M about 39% of revenue in 2024. Public companies are larger and growth-funded; not comparable to private medians.
8. **Objective-and-task vs percentage-of-sales methods**: standard marketing textbook material (e.g. Saylor Academy BUS602 "Budgeting Methods"; marketingstudyguide.com). [textbook, practitioner]
   - Objective-and-task: set objectives, cost the tasks needed to reach them, sum. Seen as the most logical but the most work.
   - Percentage of sales: simple and easy to defend, but treats marketing as a cost that follows sales rather than an investment that causes them; it cuts spend when sales fall (when more might be needed) and ignores competitors and strategy changes.
9. **Binet & Field / IPA and LinkedIn B2B Institute on ESOV**: already recorded in research/brand-growth-channels.md. Use for the competitive side of budget setting (set spend relative to your share of market). Not repeated here.
10. **70/20/10 rule**: Coca-Cola's "Content 2020" (c. 2011) split for creative content: 70% low-risk proven work, 20% building on what worked, 10% high-risk new ideas. Seen via WARC, The Media Leader and blogs. [rule-of-thumb; origin: Coca-Cola practice, not a test]
11. **ANA, "The Continued Rise of the In-House Agency: 2023 Edition"**, May 2023. https://www.ana.net/miccontent/show/id/rr-2023-05-rise-in-house-agency (MediaPost, Adweek coverage). [industry association survey, snippet-only]
    - 82% of ANA member respondents have an in-house agency, up from 78% in 2018; n = 162, fielded Feb–Mar 2023; run every five years. Members are large advertisers.
12. **Lenny Rachitsky, "Hiring your early team" (B2B)**, Lenny's Newsletter, 2023 (part of a series on 20 B2B startups). https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/hiring-your-early-team-b2b [practitioner, snippet-only]
    - Snippet: marketing hires were not commonly among the first hires in the companies studied; very few hired a salesperson in their first three hires. Full detail not read.
13. **Practitioner and vendor blogs on the first marketing hire** (MarketerHire, "Your Startup's First Marketing Hire"; Founders Connect, "When to Hire Generalists vs. Specialists"). [vendor / practitioner, snippet-only]
    - Common advice: first hire is usually a generalist who can run several channels; pick to match the current bottleneck (e.g. post-PMF B2B with no inbound pipeline → demand gen; pre-revenue consumer → content/organic); hire specialists when one function is both a main growth driver and a recurring problem; a mid-level "player" who has owned a channel at a startup often fits better than a senior manager. MarketerHire sells freelance marketers, so its advice has a commercial angle.

## What the evidence supports

- Average budget as % of revenue is ~8–9% across large firms in two surveys, but varies widely by sector (B2C product much higher than B2B product) and the distribution is skewed (Gartner median ≤ 6%). [analyst / academic survey]
- Private SaaS spends far less on marketing than "30–50% S&M" folklore suggests at the median (SaaS Capital: ~8% marketing, ~21% S&M of ARR), while public growth SaaS spends far more (~39% S&M). The range is the point: stage, funding and motion dominate. [vendor]
- Nothing found shows that matching an industry %-of-revenue average causes better outcomes. Benchmarks are descriptive. [derived]
