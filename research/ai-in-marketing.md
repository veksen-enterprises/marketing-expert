## Sources

### Productivity

1. **Noy, S. & Zhang, W. "Experimental evidence on the productivity effects of generative artificial intelligence."** *Science* 381(6654), 187–192, 13 Jul 2023. https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adh2586
   - Mid-level professional writing tasks; ChatGPT access cut time taken by ~40% and raised rated output quality by ~18%; reduced productivity inequality between workers; preregistered online experiment, 453 college-educated professionals; concern and excitement about AI temporarily rose (the abstract does not say participants enjoyed tasks more). [research] [read 2026-10-05; corrected]
2. **Dell'Acqua, F., McFowland, E., Mollick, E., Lifshitz-Assaf, H., Kellogg, K., Rajendran, S., Krayer, L., Candelon, F. & Lakhani, K. "Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier: Field Experimental Evidence of the Effects of AI on Knowledge Worker Productivity and Quality."** HBS Working Paper 24-013, Sep 2023. https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/24-013_d9b45b68-9e74-42d6-a1c6-c72fb70c7282.pdf ; SSRN 4573321. Journal version: *Organization Science* (2026), doi:10.1287/orsc.2025.21838; its abstract says "19% less likely" and "significantly improved quality" without the 40% figure.
   - Pre-registered; 758 BCG consultants (~7% of individual contributors). Inside the frontier: 12.2% more tasks, 25.1% faster, >40% higher quality. Outside the frontier: 19 percentage points less likely to produce correct solutions. [research] [read 2026-10-05: working-paper abstract as reproduced at a-mcc.eu (hbs.edu and SSRN blocked fetches); author list confirmed via OpenAlex]
3. **Brynjolfsson, E., Li, D. & Raymond, L. "Generative AI at Work."** *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 140(2), 889–942, 2025; NBER Working Paper 31161 (2023). https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161 ; https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/qjecon/v140y2025i2p889-942..html
   - 5,172–5,179 customer-support agents, staggered rollout. QJE abstract: +15% issues resolved per hour; less experienced/lower-skilled workers improve speed and quality; most experienced see small speed gains and small quality declines. NBER abstract: +14%, 34% for novice and low-skilled workers, minimal impact on experienced; better customer sentiment, fewer escalations, better retention. [research] [read 2026-10-05]

### Homogenisation

4. **Doshi, A. R. & Hauser, O. P. "Generative AI enhances individual creativity but reduces the collective diversity of novel content."** *Science Advances* 10(28), eadn5290, 12 Jul 2024. https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290
   - Online experiment; LLM story ideas made stories rated more creative, better written, more enjoyable, mostly for less creative writers; AI-enabled stories more similar to each other; framed as a social dilemma. [research] [read 2026-10-05]

### AI-generated ad creative

5. **Hartmann, J., Exner, Y. & Domdey, S. "The power of generative marketing: Can generative AI create superhuman visual marketing content?"** *International Journal of Research in Marketing* 42(1), 13–31 (2025; online 2024). https://www.researchgate.net/publication/384211022
   - AI images beat human ones on quality/aesthetics ratings; field study with >173,000 impressions: AI-generated banner ads up to 50% higher CTR than human-made stock photography. [research] [read 2026-10-05]
6. **Meta. "Introducing Enhanced Gen AI Features and Other Tools to Help Build Your Business"** (May 2024) https://www.facebook.com/business/news/Introducing-Enhanced-Gen-AI-Features-and-Other-Tools-to-Help-Build-Your-Business ; **Advantage+ creative** page https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/meta-advantage-plus/creative ; **"Expanding GenAI Transparency for Meta's Ads Products"** (Feb 2025) https://about.fb.com/news/2025/02/gen-ai-transparency-metas-ads-products/
   - Image and text generation; image variations, 9:16 conversion, enhancements; 2–3% conversion lift from background generation for Advantage+ catalog ads (Meta's claim); "AI info" labels on ads made or significantly edited with Meta's gen-AI tools, plus detection of third-party AI edits. A Monos case (−58% incremental cost per purchase) refers to Advantage+ shopping, not generative creative; not used. [first-party] [read 2026-10-05: the 2–3% figure is on the Advantage+ creative page, not the May 2024 news post; the Feb 2025 post (updated 1 Jun 2026) confirms the "AI info" labels and third-party AI detection]
7. **Google Ads Help. "Build a Performance Max asset group using generative AI"** https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15097192 ; "About generated images in Google Ads" https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14150986

### Synthetic respondents

8. **Argyle, L. P., Busby, E. C., Fulda, N., Gubler, J. R., Rytting, C. & Wingate, D. "Out of One, Many: Using Language Models to Simulate Human Samples."** *Political Analysis* 31(3), 337–351, 2023. doi:10.1017/pan.2023.2 [research] [read 2026-10-05: abstract via OpenAlex]
   - Introduces "algorithmic fidelity"; GPT-3 conditioned on demographic backstories reproduced subgroup response patterns in US political surveys.
9. **Brand, J., Israeli, A. & Ngwe, D. "Using LLMs for Market Research"** (earlier title "Using GPT for Market Research"). HBS Marketing Unit Working Paper 23-062, Mar 2023. https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4395751
   - Downward-sloping demand, diminishing marginal utility, state dependence; realistic WTP comparable to human studies; fine-tuning on past survey data improves alignment; limited reproduction of demographic heterogeneity. [research, working paper] [read 2026-10-05: 2023 abstract via Semantic Scholar; 2025 MSI version (Report 25-136) abstract: fine-tuning helps for existing and new features, not for new categories or differences between customer segments]
10. **Bisbee, J., Clinton, J. D., Dorff, C., Kenkel, B. & Larson, J. M. "Synthetic Replacements for Human Survey Data? The Perils of Large Language Models."** *Political Analysis* 32(4), 401–416, 2024. doi:10.1017/pan.2024.5
    - Averages similar to survey data, but smaller variance and different regression coefficients; sensitivity to prompt wording and change over time. [research] [read 2026-10-05: abstract via OpenAlex; ChatGPT personas, 11 groups, ANES 2016–2020 baseline; same prompt differed over a 3-month period]
11. **Wang, A., Morgenstern, J. & Dickerson, J. P. "Large language models that replace human participants can harmfully misportray and flatten identity groups."** *Nature Machine Intelligence* 7, 400–411, 2025. https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-025-00986-z
    - Four LLMs; 3,200 human participants across 16 identities; LLMs misportray and flatten groups; inference-time techniques reduce but don't remove the harm. [research] [read 2026-10-05]

## Open questions

- No independent, large-scale test found of platform generative creative (Advantage+ creative, PMax asset generation) on incremental sales.
- Productivity studies measure task time and rated quality, not marketing outcomes; no controlled study found of AI-assisted campaigns on revenue.
- Whether homogenisation persists as models and prompting improve, and whether it hurts brand distinctiveness in market (no field evidence found).
- Synthetic-respondent research moves fast (a 2026 *Nature* paper on LLMs predicting social-science experiment results appeared in search; not read). Current evidence favours using LLMs for averages and hypotheses, not variance or subgroups.
- Consumer response to AI labels in real purchase settings (most studies are stated-attitude experiments).
- US federal enforcement direction after the Rytr reversal; EU Article 50 Code of Practice details (a Commission page "Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated Content" exists; contents not read).
