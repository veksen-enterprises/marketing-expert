# What a good answer notices (graders only)

Graders score with `rubric.md`; these notes say what "specific to this business" (gate G6) and "diagnosis" (item 3) look like for the fictional cases. A good answer need not hit every point, but missing the first one in each case is a diagnosis error.

## ecom-ads (Fernhill Tea)
- Break-even ROAS at 62% gross margin is about 1.6 (paid_media_math), so 1.4 is below break-even on first orders; with 22% repeat, judge on more than first-order value. Platform-reported ROAS is modelled, not incremental.
- Revenue stayed flat while spend rose: likely saturation or creative fatigue, plus seasonality (May and December gift peaks).
- Cheap wins outside ads: no welcome series, no abandoned-cart email, 4,100 subscribers mailed irregularly; "opens" are inflated by Apple Mail Privacy Protection.
- Free shipping threshold ($50) sits above the $38 average order: a lever to test.
- The page: generic hero copy ("Steeped in Tradition", "world-class"), meta description "Tea.", newsletter discount fits the founders' 15% cap.
- Respects the principles: no deep discounts, no hard-to-cancel subscription.

## local-weekdays (Northside Paws)
- The problem is weekday demand, not awareness: weekends are full. Levers: rebooking at checkout (18% now), weekday-only offers or pricing, recurring appointments, nail-trim walk-ins as a weekday feeder, vets' referrals on weekdays.
- No deposit and a 9% no-show rate cost slots.
- Reviews are stale (last 5 months ago): ask every client, not only happy ones (Google's rule).
- Respects the 7-dogs-a-day cap: growth means a better mix, not more dogs.

## app-downloads (Stillwater)
- Downloads are not the constraint: D30 is 6% overall but 28% for users who log 3 sits in week 1. Activation comes before acquisition.
- Product page converts 31%, search is 71% of downloads: keyword work on name and subtitle, custom product pages, ratings prompt after a success moment.
- Apple Ads Basic: $1.90 per install but 3 trials from $600, so about $200 per trial; don't scale it.
- The r/Meditation post was removed: community rules matter.
- Respects "no streaks, no guilt notifications, no account": no streak mechanics or growth via contact uploads.

## marketplace-city (Kitlend)
- City B is not liquid (22% of searches find a match, 4 rentals a month) after 7 months; a third city would split the same thin effort. The usual answer is: fix liquidity in A and B first (liquidity_math).
- 41% of listings never rented; owners with an early rental list more: focus supply quality and first rentals.
- Runway is about 9 months and the season is May–September: timing risk.
- Respects local pickup only.

## follow-up (Rowcount)
- Before new advice, the answer deals with the two saved moves past their check date. Yarn-shop cards: NUMBERS.md shows 0 of 40 agreed (2 replies), so the stop line (fewer than 3 of 40) was hit; the answer says so and does not propose shop outreach again. Chart-import prompt: nothing in the material says whether it shipped or what share imported; the answer asks, and does not guess.
- Offers to save both outcomes (result with the number, stopHit, measured or recalled) and the new moves as predictions.
- The profile's traction (2,000 active, 190 paying, August) is older than NUMBERS.md (2,150 active, 212 paying, October): use the newer numbers and offer to update.
- Diagnosis: signups are flat at about 380 a month and free-to-Plus is 4.1%, but users who import a chart in week 1 convert at 13%. Activation (the chart import) is the lever, which is why the unknown onboarding outcome matters most.
- Respects no ads and private imported patterns: no ad-supported tier, no publishing users' patterns.

## services-growth (Ledgerline)
- Capacity is 10 clients before hiring; 2 of 3 discovery calls close. The constraint is qualified leads in a narrow niche, not conversion.
- The food-cost spreadsheet already travels between owners: a free tool or template with a contact path.
- Referral sources (accountants, a distributor's rep) can be made systematic: partnerships.
- The website brings non-restaurant leads: niche positioning on the site.
- Respects "restaurants only" and the named-bookkeeper principle.

## solo-health (Olivo Nutrición)
- Demand comes only from people who were told about her (9 of 9 enquiries), and most people who know her haven't been told: her Instagram bio and LinkedIn don't say she takes clients, and 3 of about 25 former colleagues know. Telling the people who already know her, and making every profile say the offer and how to book, comes before new channels.
- Conversion is not the problem: 7 of 9 enquiries booked, 4 of 7 bought the plan. Capacity is about 20 clients, so she has room for 16 more.
- Former colleagues (doctors, nurses) are a referral source with a professional reason to refer: a clear note of who she helps, for what.
- She is a regulated health professional: advertising, testimonials and promises fall under her professional college's code and Spanish health-advertising rules. The playbooks cover Spain's cold-email rules, not health advertising there, so a good answer says what wasn't checked and doesn't invent the rule. Her principles already rule out before-and-after photos and weight-loss promises.
- Ads, TikTok and free first consultations are weak first moves at 15 hours a week and 4 clients; a booking platform listing is cheap to test with a stop line.
- Respects her principles: no paid referral groups, no supplements.

## solo-design (Marea Studio)
- The US work that paid full price all came through one friend's introductions (3 of 6 became projects). That is the working channel; most of his 60-person network doesn't know what he offers. Asking for introductions on purpose, with a one-line offer, comes first.
- The portfolio shows agency work with no offer, no price and no startup focus, and its traffic is mostly from Mexico; LinkedIn says only "Designer". The offer (brand identity for early-stage software startups, from US$3,500) should be on every profile.
- Buying a list and cold-emailing 500 US founders: US commercial email is legal with an opt-out and a real address (CAN-SPAM), but a bought list hurts deliverability and reputation; small, researched one-to-one outreach is the better test.
- Upwork: 2 proposals is too few to judge; the playbooks' stop line is about 20 proposals.
- Payment fees (about 4%) and invoicing from Mexico are a real cost, but the playbooks don't cover getting paid from abroad, so a good answer flags it as not checked.
- Respects his principles: no spec work, no contests.
