## Errors

1. **Misattributed sources (factual).** "Do you own the customer? Yes. Your own accounts and billing (ADR 0025)". ADR 0025 is about local accounts as the identity floor for *self-hosted* instances, not about billing or owning the customer. "self-host path (ADR 0023)": ADR 0023's status is "Accepted, **not built**".
2. **Treats planned features as current defences.** Under "Now (what protects you today)" it lists "open source / self-host". The pricing FAQ says "We're working on a fully self-hosted / offline mode", ADR 0023 is not built and ADR 0025 is partially built. Only the analyzer container is self-run today. In the same way, "your own team plan, Slack alerts" appear as things to build on, but both are on the homepage "On our radar" list and the pricing page says "We don't have a team tier yet". The answer doesn't flag that they are unshipped.
3. **Copy cost not quantified despite the heading.** Prompt step 2 asks for copy cost "in their own revenue or margin terms". The answer gives qualitative reasons only. It could have contrasted per-host pricing (Datadog DBM, roughly $70/host/month list price per the grader's knowledge, unverified) with DT's $16 flat to show the business-model gap it relies on.
4. **Missed positioning fact.** The homepage hero sells the product as "**PlanView**" ("Try PlanView free"), not "DBTool". An answer arguing "the site pulls you toward feature" should have noticed that the hero literally names a feature-sized sub-product.
5. Competitor facts are broadly correct as far as the grader knows: Datadog DBM per-host pricing and CI Visibility; pganalyze per-server pricing and Index Advisor; Performance Insights being folded into CloudWatch Database Insights; the PG18 statistics-restore functions; Supabase's hypopg-based index_advisor and MCP servers from Supabase and Neon. All are flagged as unverified, which is correct. No tool misuse.

## Top strengths

1. It reframes the coding-agent threat as a distribution channel ("the tool the agent calls"), grounded in VISION and in the working MCP server that the site hides. It then finds the concrete contradiction (MCP "soon" vs live `/mcp`, $20 vs $16).
2. It names the competitor the founder left out (Postgres hosts) and a real technical risk (PG18 statistics import weakening the fork's lead). Both are honest and specific.
3. Its evidence discipline is good: labelled assumptions A1–A4, an evidence-strength section, and no invented numbers.

## Server attribution (ranked, most impactful first)

1. **src/prompts.ts `competitive_strategy`: add a stage gate and an output budget (server caused).** The prompt has eight mandatory sections, and the answer followed it to the letter: alternatives, copy cost, sustaining, risk questions, protections, where to fight, responses, warning signs. That is why it runs to 3,000 words and why item 3 loses a point. Fix: add step 0, "Before any competitor analysis: at this company's stage, is competition the real constraint, or is it obscurity / 'no decision'? If pre-revenue or few users, say so first and keep steps 2–8 short." Then add: "Output: short version, ≤2 ranked moves, then the analysis in compact tables; skip steps that don't change a decision."
2. **src/server.ts INSTRUCTIONS rule 5: require cheapest test, time box and stop condition (server caused).** Rule 5 asks only for "mechanism, what to measure, and what result would change your mind". The answer delivered exactly that and nothing about the cheapest test or the stop condition. Fix: "For each move: the cheapest test (≤2 weeks), the metric with a threshold, the stop condition, and one thing not to do." Mirror this in the final lines of every prompt in src/prompts.ts, including `competitive_strategy`.
3. **src/server.ts INSTRUCTIONS rule 0: separate shipped from planned, and cite sources only for what they say (server caused, assistant also at fault).** Nothing tells the assistant to check whether an advantage is live before calling it a defence, or to read an ADR's Status line. Fix: "When listing the company's strengths, mark each as shipped / partial / planned from its own docs (ADR Status, 'soon', roadmap). Never count planned features as current protection. Cite a file only for what it actually says."
4. **src/prompts.ts `competitive_strategy` step 2: make copy cost numeric (server partly).** "In their own revenue or margin terms" is easy to satisfy with words. Fix: "Put their price model next to ours (per host / per seat / flat) with figures and dates if known, flagged unverified. If you cannot give numbers, say 'unquantified'."
5. **src/server.ts INSTRUCTIONS rule 7: give a jargon list and a length cap (server partly; the assistant ignored the spirit).** Rule 7 bans "moat", "flywheel" and "wedge" but not "sustaining", "ICP" or "P&L", and the prompt itself uses "sustaining" and "P&L". Fix: extend the list (ICP, sustaining/disruptive, P&L, counter-positioning, TAM), and say "define on first use or rephrase" and "aim for under ~1,200 words unless asked for depth". Also remove the jargon from prompts.ts step 3 ("Is our innovation sustaining...") or define it there.
6. **knowledge/platform-and-feature-risk.md: add the infrastructure-host case (server gap; the assistant filled it on its own).** The playbook's examples are app stores and OS vendors (Dropbox/Apple). The highest-risk absorber for dev tools is the hosting or infrastructure provider that owns the data and the developer (Supabase/Neon/Vercel-type). A short section on that, and on "be the tool the agent calls" for the MCP era, would make this reasoning repeatable rather than up to the assistant. Also soften any wording that leads to "every absorption was announced at a keynote".
7. **Assistant-only issues (no server change needed):** it missed the "PlanView" branding in the hero, misread ADR 0025, and overstated the Dropbox causality. The server already says "look before critiquing" and "say how strong the evidence is"; these were lapses in execution.
